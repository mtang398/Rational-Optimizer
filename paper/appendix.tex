\section{Normalized rational response geometry}
\label{sec:grain}
\label{app:grain-calculus}

GRAIN learns the shape of a nonlinearity in coordinates that follow the
scale of its inputs. The group normalization and scale restoration act
together: the first controls the inputs presented to the rational
function, while the second carries amplitude information into the
outgoing projection. This construction also exposes the response
geometry used by TILLER.

For one group, write $z=Ax\in\mathbb R^w$ and suppress its layer and
group indices. The activation is
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(z)=\rho\phi(u),\qquad
 u=z/\rho,\qquad
 \rho=(z^\top z/w+\epsilon_{\rm act})^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:grain-normalize}
\end{equation}
The rational coefficients specify a response to relative feature
magnitudes. In the idealized case $\epsilon_{\rm act}=0$ and $z\ne0$,
positive rescaling leaves $u$ unchanged and gives
$\Phi(az)=a\Phi(z)$ for $a>0$. The positive stabilizer makes the map
defined at zero. It is therefore useful to view GRAIN as a learned
shape together with a data-dependent amplitude, rather than as a
rational function applied to unnormalized features.

An incoming matrix update changes both of these quantities. The
normalization derivatives are
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_z\rho=u/w,\qquad
 \frac{\partial u}{\partial z}
 =\rho^{-1}\left(I-\frac{uu^\top}{w}\right).
 \label{eq:grain-normalization-derivative}
\end{equation}
With $f=\phi(u)$, $d_\phi=\phi'(u)$, and
$r_\phi=f-u\odot d_\phi$, the product rule yields
\begin{equation}
 J=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z}
 =\frac{fu^\top}{w}
  +\operatorname{Diag}(d_\phi)\left(I-\frac{uu^\top}{w}\right)
 =\operatorname{Diag}(d_\phi)+\frac{r_\phi u^\top}{w}.
 \label{eq:grain-jacobian}
\end{equation}
The diagonal term applies the local slope to each coordinate. The
rank-one term accounts for their shared change in scale. For a linear
response $\phi(u)=a u$, $r_\phi=0$ and $J=aI$: normalization and
restoration cancel exactly. For a nonlinear response, $r_\phi$ measures
the difference between the function value and its local linear part.
Thus a change in rational shape changes both the features and the
way matrix perturbations propagate through them.

For an input perturbation $v$ and an output sensitivity $b$, the two
Jacobian actions are
\begin{equation}
 Jv=d_\phi\odot v+r_\phi\frac{u^\top v}{w},\qquad
 J^\top b=d_\phi\odot b+u\frac{r_\phi^\top b}{w}.
 \label{eq:grain-jacobian-actions}
\end{equation}
These expressions describe the incoming path through the activation
and its backward sensitivity. The outgoing path acts directly on
$h=\Phi(z)$. This distinction determines the two response summaries
used in the optimizer. The derivatives hold at differentiability
points; at zero arguments the absolute-value terms use the selected
autodifferentiation derivative $\operatorname{sign}(0)=0$.

The positive denominator ensures that finite learned coefficients
introduce no real poles. All groups begin from a common SiLU
approximation and then learn their own nonlinear responses; the
initialization is specified with the training algorithm in
Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm}.

\section{Response-guided matrix directions}
\label{sec:directions}

TILLER constructs an update orientation from two views of the same
momentum. The Muon polar transformation rebalances its singular values;
factorized adaptation rescales entries using their gradient history.
The learned activation determines how far to rotate from the first
direction toward the second. Keeping the norm fixed separates this
orientation choice from the subsequent allocation of strength across
groups.

Let $g_{t,i}^r$ be the accumulated, distributed-averaged, globally clipped
gradient for group $i=(\ell,g)$ and role $r\in\{A,B\}$. With
$v_{0,i}^r=0$, the first-moment buffer and Nesterov momentum are
\begin{equation}
 v_{t,i}^r=\beta_1v_{t-1,i}^r+(1-\beta_1)g_{t,i}^r,\qquad
 m_{t,i}^r=\beta_1v_{t,i}^r+(1-\beta_1)g_{t,i}^r.
 \label{eq:nesterov-momentum}
\end{equation}
We apply Muon's approximate polar map $\mathcal P_5$ separately to each
group block \citep{jordan2024muon,liu2025muon}. For expanded width
$H_r=Gw$, the parent direction is
\begin{equation}
 p_{t,i}^r=\mu_{\rm mlp}\chi_{\rm grp}\mathcal P_5(m_{t,i}^r),\qquad
 \mu_{\rm mlp}=0.2\sqrt{\max\{H_r,d\}},\qquad
 \chi_{\rm grp}=\sqrt{\frac{\min\{H_r,d\}}{G\min\{w,d\}}}.
 \label{eq:group-polar-calibration}
\end{equation}
The factor $\chi_{\rm grp}$ places the collection of groupwise polar
directions on the nominal Frobenius scale of the full projection.
The same calibration applies to the incoming and outgoing roles.

The adaptive direction uses row and column second moments
\citep{shazeer2018adafactor}. Suppressing the group and role indices,
initialize $R_0=C_0=0$ and define
\begin{align}
 R_t&=\beta_2R_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)\operatorname{rowsum}(g_t^2),\nonumber\\
 C_t&=\beta_2C_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)\operatorname{colsum}(g_t^2),\nonumber\\
 V_{t,jk}&=\frac{R_{t,j}C_{t,k}}
 {\max\{\sum_sR_{t,s},\delta_{\rm fp}\}(1-\beta_2^t)},\qquad
 a_t=\mu_{\rm mlp}\frac{m_t}{\sqrt{V_t}+\epsilon_{\rm opt}}.
 \label{eq:factored-direction}
\end{align}
Here row and column sums are taken over squared entries. We use
$\beta_1=\beta_2=0.95$, $\epsilon_{\rm opt}=10^{-8}$, and
$\delta_{\rm fp}=\operatorname{finfo}(\texttt{float32}).\operatorname{tiny}$.
The use of the same $m_t$ makes the two candidates differ in their
matrix transformation rather than in the gradient history they average.

The activation supplies two complementary measurements for choosing
between their orientations. Participation measures the breadth of
the response, while congruence measures its alignment with the
initial response at the current inputs. For a sampled position $k$,
let $J_{ki}$ and $h_{ki}$ denote the group Jacobian and features.
The participation ratios are
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{pr}_A(J)=
 \frac{[\operatorname{tr}(JJ^\top)]^2}{w\frob{JJ^\top}^2},\qquad
 \operatorname{pr}_B(h)=\frac{\|h\|_2^4}{w\|h\|_4^4}.
 \label{eq:response-summary-components}
\end{equation}
For the incoming role, the components are squared singular values of
the Jacobian; for the outgoing role, they are squared feature values.
A nonzero response concentrated in one component has participation
$1/w$, while a response spread equally over $m$ components has
participation $m/w$. This common scale makes breadth comparable
between the two paths.

The summaries emphasize positions where the loss is sensitive to the
corresponding path. With downstream derivative $\xi_{k\ell}$ at the
block output, define
\begin{equation}
 \alpha^A_{ki}=\|J_{ki}^\top B_i^\top\xi_{k\ell}\|_2^2/w,\qquad
 \alpha^B_{ki}=\|\xi_{k\ell}\|_2^2/d,\qquad
 \pi_i^r=\frac{\sum_k\alpha^r_{ki}\operatorname{pr}_r(\mathcal R^r_{ki})}
 {\sum_k\alpha^r_{ki}},
 \label{eq:participation-main}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal R^A_{ki}=J_{ki}$ and $\mathcal R^B_{ki}=h_{ki}$.
For congruence, set $\Gamma^A_{ki}=J_{ki}J_{ki}^\top$ and
$\Gamma^B_{ki}=h_{ki}h_{ki}^\top$. A superscript $0$ denotes the
same quantity evaluated using the initial rational coefficients and
the current input and RMS scale. Then
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_i^r=
 \frac{\sum_k\alpha^r_{ki}\inner{\Gamma^r_{ki}}{\Gamma^{r,0}_{ki}}}
 {\sqrt{\left(\sum_k\alpha^r_{ki}\frob{\Gamma^r_{ki}}^2\right)
 \left(\sum_k\alpha^r_{ki}\frob{\Gamma^{r,0}_{ki}}^2\right)}}.
 \label{eq:congruence-main}
\end{equation}
Congruence is a cosine between weighted collections of response
kernels. Evaluating both collections at the same input isolates the
change in activation shape from a change in the input distribution.
The kernels compare how responses distribute energy; a common
positive rescaling leaves their congruence equal to one.

These definitions give the rotation fraction
$e_i^r=\pi_i^r(1-(\kappa_i^r)^2)$. An unchanged response selects the
Muon orientation. Departure from that response permits a rotation,
and participation moderates it when the response is concentrated in
few components. The bounds follow from the geometry of the summaries:
for nonnegative $v_j$,
$\sum_jv_j^2\leq(\sum_jv_j)^2\leq w\sum_jv_j^2$,
so nonzero participation lies in $[1/w,1]$. The congruence numerator is
nonnegative, since
$\inner{JJ^\top}{J^0J^{0\top}}=\frob{J^\top J^0}^2$ and
$\inner{hh^\top}{h^0h^{0\top}}=(h^\top h^0)^2$.
Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the cosine above by one. Consequently
$e_i^r\in[0,1]$.
\label{app:response-proof}

Suppressing the role index again, remove the parallel component of
$a_i$ and orient the remainder using the current gradient:
\begin{equation}
 a_i^\perp=a_i-\frac{\inner{a_i}{p_i}}{\frob{p_i}^2}p_i,\qquad
 \widetilde q_i=\sqrt{1-e_i}\,p_i+
 \sigma_i\sqrt{e_i}\frac{\frob{p_i}}{\frob{a_i^\perp}}a_i^\perp,
 \label{eq:tangent-chord}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_i=1$ if $\inner{g_i}{a_i^\perp}\geq0$ and $-1$
otherwise. Writing $e_i=\sin^2\vartheta_i$ identifies this operation as
a rotation through $\vartheta_i$ in the plane of the two orthogonal
directions. In particular,
\[
 \frob{\widetilde q_i}^2
 =(1-e_i)\frob{p_i}^2+e_i\frob{p_i}^2=\frob{p_i}^2.
\]
The tangent contribution has nonnegative contraction with $g_i$.
We select $q_i=\widetilde q_i$ when it is finite and both
$\inner{g_i}{p_i}>0$ and $\inner{g_i}{\widetilde q_i}>0$;
otherwise $q_i=p_i$. A zero parent or tangent also selects $p_i$.
Thus the orientation choice preserves the parent norm in either case.

Attention uses this construction with a layer-level response summary.
Let $\overline\pi_\ell^r=G^{-1}\sum_g\pi_{\ell g}^r$, and obtain
$\kappa_\ell^r$ by summing the numerator and the two norm sums in
equation~\ref{eq:congruence-main} across groups before normalization.
The rotation fraction for the QKV and attention-output matrices is
\begin{equation}
 e_\ell^{\rm att}=
 \sqrt{\overline\pi_\ell^A\overline\pi_\ell^B}
 \left(1-\kappa_\ell^A\kappa_\ell^B\right).
 \label{eq:attention-response}
\end{equation}
It combines the breadth of both feed-forward response paths, and a
change in either path can affect the rotation. The polar map groups
the 16 attention heads into blocks of four; the stacked QKV direction
is scaled by $1/\sqrt3$ for its three projections. Factorized moments
for the adaptive tangent are computed at the matrix level. The global
coefficients derived next apply to the paired GRAIN projections;
attention receives its response-guided rotation directly.

\section{Joint loss-response coordination}
\label{sec:loss-measurements}

The direction construction determines the orientation of each group
update. Coordination determines how these directions act together.
Disjoint parameter blocks can make reinforcing or offsetting
contributions to the loss. TILLER records those contributions at
shared activation positions and uses their joint second moment to
redistribute a fixed update norm.

\subsection{Shared response measurements and the loss ledger}
\label{sec:ledger}

For probe $k$ and group $i=(\ell,g)$, perturb both projections along
$(q_i^A,q_i^B)$. Differentiating
$(B_i+\alpha q_i^B)\Phi_i((A_i+\alpha q_i^A)x_{k\ell})$
at $\alpha=0$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \delta y_{ki}=B_iJ_{ki}q_i^Ax_{k\ell}+q_i^Bh_{ki},\qquad
 S_{t,ki}=\inner{q_i^Ax_{k\ell}}{J_{ki}^\top B_i^\top\xi_{k\ell}}
          +\inner{q_i^Bh_{ki}}{\xi_{k\ell}}.
 \label{eq:functional-score}
\end{equation}
Probe sensitivities and parameter gradients must describe the same
loss. If a backward pass averages over $R$ positions per microbatch
and $M$ accumulated microbatches, its captured derivative
$\bar\xi_{k\ell}$ is converted to
\begin{equation}
 \xi_{k\ell}=RM\chi_{\rm clip}\bar\xi_{k\ell},
 \label{eq:cotangent-rescaling}
\end{equation}
where $\chi_{\rm clip}$ is the realized global clipping factor.
This restores the summed-loss scale and applies the same clipping as
the parameter gradients. The clipping factor is held fixed in the
directional measurements.

The two terms follow the incoming and outgoing paths through the
block. Each score is a position's contribution to the directional
loss derivative. Averaging all such contributions on the scale in
Equation~\eqref{eq:cotangent-rescaling} recovers the clipped minibatch derivative. The exact
current derivative for each paired direction is available directly
from the parameter gradients:
\begin{equation}
 s_{t,i}=\inner{g_{t,i}^A}{q_{t,i}^A}
         +\inner{g_{t,i}^B}{q_{t,i}^B}.
 \label{eq:current-score}
\end{equation}
TILLER uses $s_t$ for current descent and the probe matrix
$S_t\in\mathbb R^{K\times C}$ for the variation of contributions
across shared positions. These quantities answer different parts of
the coordination problem.

For coefficients $c$, the vector $S_tc$ contains the combined probe
responses. The cross products in $\|S_tc\|_2^2$ distinguish directions
with identical mean descent but different overlap. For example,
the columns $(2,0)^\top$ and $(0,2)^\top$ each have mean one and
their sum has mean squared response four. Two identical columns
$(2,0)^\top$ retain the same individual means but produce a mean
squared response of eight when added. The ledger captures this
difference, allowing the coefficient model to favor combinations
with less concentrated joint response for a given descent signal.

Weight decay contributes another direction to the same perturbation.
Evaluating equation~\ref{eq:functional-score} with
$(\lambda_{\rm wd}A_i,\lambda_{\rm wd}B_i)$ and summing over the
GRAIN groups gives a vector $a_t^{\rm dec}\in\mathbb R^K$.
The response penalty then expands as
\begin{equation}
 \frac1K\|S_tc+a_t^{\rm dec}\|_2^2
 =c^\top\frac{S_t^\top S_t}{K}c
 +2c^\top\frac{S_t^\top a_t^{\rm dec}}{K}
 +\frac{\|a_t^{\rm dec}\|_2^2}{K}.
 \label{eq:functional-response-energy}
\end{equation}
The final term is independent of $c$. The first two terms supply the
response metric and its cross term with decay.

TILLER refreshes $K=32$ shared probes at steps $1,9,17,\ldots$.
The positions coincide across layers, and each refresh recomputes
the participation, congruence, and response scores at current
parameters. Between refreshes, it reuses the response summaries and
updates the ledger along the current gradient contraction. Let
$\nu=\|S_{\rm ref}\|_{\rm F}/\sqrt K$ be the RMS row norm of the
latest refresh. The intervening rows are
\begin{equation}
 S_t=\boldsymbol1_K(\gamma_ts_t)^\top,\qquad
 a_t^{\rm dec}=\boldsymbol1_K\gamma_td_t^{\rm dec},\qquad
 \gamma_t=\begin{cases}\nu/\|s_t\|_2,&\|s_t\|_2>0,\\0,&\|s_t\|_2=0,
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:trace-matched-rows}
\end{equation}
where $d_t^{\rm dec}$ is the current gradient contraction with the
GRAIN decay direction. For $s_t\ne0$, these rows have the same
second-moment trace as the most recent probes:
$\|S_t\|_{\rm F}^2/K=\nu^2$. They supply current orientation
information along $s_t$ while probe refreshes recover variation
across positions.

The uncompressed ledger would follow
\begin{equation}
 G_t^\star=\beta_2G_{t-1}^\star+
          (1-\beta_2)S_t^\top S_t/K,\qquad
 G_1^\star=S_1^\top S_1/K.
 \label{eq:ledger-target}
\end{equation}
For any $c$, $c^\top G_t^\star c$ is a discounted average of squared
responses for that coefficient combination. The measurements retain
the directions used when they were recorded; discounting emphasizes
the response geometry encountered more recently.

We represent this history by a factor $\mathcal B_t$ and an isotropic
remainder $\tau_t I$. New rows enter the current metric before
compression:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A_t=\begin{bmatrix}
 \sqrt{\beta_2}\mathcal B_{t-1}\\\sqrt{1-\beta_2}S_t
 \end{bmatrix},\qquad
 G_t=\mathcal A_t^\top\mathcal A_t/K+\beta_2\tau_{t-1}I,\qquad
 r_t=\beta_2r_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)S_t^\top a_t^{\rm dec}/K.
 \label{eq:current-ledger-metric}
\end{equation}
At the first step, $\mathcal A_1=\mathcal B_1=S_1$,
$\tau_0=\tau_1=0$, and $r_1=S_1^\top a_1^{\rm dec}/K$.
The current coefficient model therefore uses fresh rows at full
resolution together with the compressed history.

Robust Frequent Directions carries this history forward
\citep{ghashami2016frequent,luo2019robust}. If the singular values of
$\mathcal A_t$ are $\sigma_1\geq\sigma_2\geq\cdots$, set
$\Delta_t=\sigma_{r+1}^2$ for $r=64$ and subtract $\Delta_t$ from
each retained squared singular value. When the rank is at most $r$,
take $\Delta_t=0$ and retain an exact factor. With right singular
vectors $V_{1:r}$, the compressed representation is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B_t=\operatorname{Diag}
 (\sqrt{\sigma_1^2-\Delta_t},\ldots,\sqrt{\sigma_r^2-\Delta_t})
 V_{1:r}^\top,\qquad
 \tau_t=\beta_2\tau_{t-1}+\frac{\Delta_t}{2K}.
 \label{eq:rfd-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
The factor retains dominant combinations of responses. The isotropic
term assigns energy to directions outside its span. The midpoint
$\Delta_t/(2K)$ balances the error introduced by spectral shrinkage,
as the following bound shows.

\begin{proposition}[Response-metric approximation]
\label{prop:ledger-error}
For $K>0$, $\beta_2\in[0,1]$, and $\tau_{t-1}\geq0$, let $G_t$
and $(\mathcal B_t,\tau_t)$ be defined by
equations~\ref{eq:current-ledger-metric} and~\ref{eq:rfd-reconstruction}.
Then $\widehat G_t=\mathcal B_t^\top\mathcal B_t/K+\tau_tI$ is
positive semidefinite and
\begin{equation}
 \|\widehat G_t-G_t\|_2\leq\frac{\Delta_t}{2K},\qquad
 |c^\top(\widehat G_t-G_t)c|\leq
 \frac{\Delta_t}{2K}\|c\|_2^2\quad\text{for every }c\in\mathbb R^C.
 \label{eq:rfd-error}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Both terms of $\widehat G_t$ are positive semidefinite. When
$\Delta_t=0$, the metrics coincide. Otherwise use an orthonormal
basis of right singular vectors of $\mathcal A_t$, completed by its
null space. On a retained direction, shrinkage removes $\Delta_t/K$
and the isotropic term restores $\Delta_t/(2K)$, giving error
$-\Delta_t/(2K)$. On a discarded direction with squared singular
value $s\in[0,\Delta_t]$, the error is $(\Delta_t/2-s)/K$;
on a null direction it is $\Delta_t/(2K)$. Each absolute eigenvalue
error is at most $\Delta_t/(2K)$. The quadratic-form bound follows
from $|c^\top Ec|\leq\|E\|_2\|c\|_2^2$ for symmetric $E$.
\end{proof}

The bound also describes accumulation over the discounted history.
If $E_t=\widehat G_t-G_t^\star$, each compression adds an error
$D_t$ with $\|D_t\|_2\leq\Delta_t/(2K)$, so
$E_t=\beta_2E_{t-1}+D_t$ and $E_1=0$. Applying the triangle
inequality recursively gives
\begin{equation}
 \|E_t\|_2\leq\sum_{j=2}^t\beta_2^{t-j}\frac{\Delta_j}{2K}.
 \label{eq:rfd-history-error}
\end{equation}
Older compression errors are attenuated on the same time scale as
older response measurements.

\subsection{Equal-budget coefficient selection}
\label{sec:transaction}
\label{app:transaction-proof}

The ledger becomes useful for optimization when combined with the
current descent signal. For group energy
$\omega_i=\frob{q_i^A}^2+\frob{q_i^B}^2$ and
$\mathcal E_0=\sum_i\omega_i$, the disjoint parameter blocks give
$\|\Delta\theta(c)\|_2^2=\eta^2\sum_i\omega_ic_i^2$.
Constraining this sum to $\eta^2\mathcal E_0$ preserves the norm
of the uncoordinated structured update. The model in
equation~\ref{eq:overview-coordinate-model} then trades current
descent against the ledger's joint response penalty. Its coefficients
can be signed because the model assesses the combination as a whole.

For positive $\omega_i$, write $W=\operatorname{Diag}(\omega)$ and
$y=W^{1/2}c$. Dividing the objective by $\eta^2$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal Q_t(y)=\tfrac12y^\top M_ty-b_t^\top y,\qquad
 \|y\|_2^2=\mathcal E_0,\\
 M_t=W^{-1/2}G_tW^{-1/2},\qquad
 b_t=W^{-1/2}(s_t/\eta-r_t).
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:whitened-transaction}
\end{equation}
This change of coordinates turns the weighted norm constraint into
a sphere. We use the positive-multiplier branch of its stationarity
equation to construct a proposal. The stationarity equation is
$(M_t+\lambda I)y=b_t$, where the multiplier $\lambda$ controls
the norm. TILLER constructs a proposal in a subspace generated by
$b_t$ and repeated metric actions. A reorthogonalized Lanczos
recurrence \citep{lanczos1950iteration} produces an orthonormal
basis $Q_k$, with $k\leq32$. Each action uses the ledger factor:
\begin{equation}
 M_tv=W^{-1/2}\mathcal A_t^\top
 (\mathcal A_tW^{-1/2}v)/K+\beta_2\tau_{t-1}W^{-1}v.
 \label{eq:ledger-metric-action}
\end{equation}

Let $T_k^+$ be the positive-semidefinite projection of the accumulated
Lanczos matrix. For $y=Q_kz$, diagonalize
$T_k^+=U\operatorname{Diag}(\theta)U^\top$ and set
$\gamma=U^\top Q_k^\top b_t$. The projected stationarity and
norm conditions reduce to
\begin{equation}
 z=U\operatorname{Diag}((\theta+\lambda)^{-1})\gamma,\qquad
 \psi(\lambda)=\sum_j\frac{\gamma_j^2}{(\theta_j+\lambda)^2}
 =\mathcal E_0.
 \label{eq:secular-equation}
\end{equation}
For nonzero $\gamma$ and $\lambda>0$,
$\psi'(\lambda)=-2\sum_j\gamma_j^2/(\theta_j+\lambda)^3<0$.
The positive branch therefore has at most one root. We bracket it by
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_{\rm lo}=\epsilon_{\rm mach}C,\qquad
 \lambda_{\rm hi}=\|b_t\|_2/\sqrt{\mathcal E_0}
                  +\lambda_{\max}(T_k^+)+\lambda_{\rm lo}.
 \label{eq:multiplier-bracket}
\end{equation}
Since $\theta_j\geq0$ and $Q_k$ is orthonormal,
$\psi(\lambda_{\rm hi})\leq\|b_t\|_2^2/\lambda_{\rm hi}^2
\leq\mathcal E_0$. If $\psi(\lambda_{\rm lo})\geq\mathcal E_0$,
64 bisection iterations give the proposal
$c=W^{-1/2}Q_kz$. Otherwise the step retains $c=\boldsymbol1$.

The projected solution is a candidate for the full ledger model.
TILLER evaluates it in $F_t$ and selects it only if it improves on
$\boldsymbol1$, is finite, and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \frac{|c^\top Wc-\mathcal E_0|}{\max\{\mathcal E_0,1\}}\leq10^{-8}.
 \label{eq:coefficient-norm-check}
\end{equation}
For each layer and each matrix role it also checks
\begin{equation}
 \sum_g c_{\ell g}\inner{g_{\ell g}^r}{q_{\ell g}^r}>0,\qquad
 \sum_g c_{\ell g}\inner{m_{\ell g}^r}{q_{\ell g}^r}>0,\qquad r\in\{A,B\}.
 \label{eq:transaction-contractions}
\end{equation}
These contractions are checked for both the candidate and
$\boldsymbol1$. A failed check, a zero coordinate energy, or a
degenerate coefficient problem selects $\boldsymbol1$.
The result is an allocation evaluated against the complete response
model, with the projected solve used to construct the proposal.

The transition changes the matrix-direction construction. During the
TILLER stage, feed-forward directions use groupwise polar maps,
response-guided rotations, and joint coefficients. During the Muon
stage, each full incoming, outgoing, QKV, and attention-output matrix
receives an ordinary Muon update. Copying the first-moment buffers
preserves gradient history across this change. The AdamW optimizer
for the remaining parameters is the same object throughout, so its
moments and step counts continue. The switch is applied after TILLER
update $T_s$; update $T_s+1$ is the first Muon update.
The learning-rate schedule advances with the global training step.
Rational coefficients continue learning through AdamW in both stages.

The denominator in equation~\ref{eq:overview-rational} is at least one,
so finite learned coefficients introduce no real poles. Each group
starts from the same SiLU fit on $[-5,5]$. The numerator and denominator
coefficients, in ascending powers, are
\begin{align}
 (a_0,\ldots,a_5)={}&(2.4915714620520875\!\times\!10^{-5},\
 0.5000668663485823,\ 0.2499441908995771,\nonumber\\
 &0.0526200910151016,\ 0.005525765640115002,\
 0.00024199321178747251),\label{eq:initial-numerator}\\
 (b_1,\ldots,b_4)={}&(0.00022554017910995938,\
 0.10511420350691994,\nonumber\\
 &2.8656992394109812\!\times\!10^{-5},\
 0.0004816962110562025).\label{eq:initial-denominator}
\end{align}
The ten coefficients then learn independently in every group. We use
group width $w=256$ and $\epsilon_{\rm act}=10^{-6}$.

The polar map uses five Newton--Schulz iterations. It transposes tall
inputs, casts the working matrix to bfloat16, and normalizes by a
Frobenius norm accumulated in float32, lower bounded by $10^{-7}$,
and cast to bfloat16. With $(a,b,c)=(3.4445,-4.775,2.0315)$,
the iteration is
\begin{equation}
 X_{j+1}=aX_j+b(X_jX_j^\top)X_j+c(X_jX_j^\top)^2X_j.
 \label{eq:newton-schulz-map}
\end{equation}
The original orientation is restored after the final iteration.
Factorized second moments are maintained in float32. The
coefficient solver uses double precision and two reorthogonalization
passes per Lanczos basis vector. At an early breakdown, it
reorthogonalizes a deterministic cosine vector to start a new block
with zero coupling to the preceding block. The resulting basis is
checked for orthogonality, and negative eigenvalues of the accumulated
projected matrix are set to zero before the multiplier solve.


For a zero response, participation is defined as zero. If either
matrix role has zero total sensitivity, both participation values
are set to one. If either congruence denominator vanishes, both
congruence values are set to one. Congruence is also one when the
rational coefficients equal their initialization. These conventions
give zero departure when the response comparison is undefined.
