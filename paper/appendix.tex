\section{Differential properties of GRAIN}
\label{sec:grain}
\label{app:grain-calculus}

For one feature group, write $\Phi(z)=\rho\phi(u)$, where
$u=z/\rho$ and $\rho=(z^\top z/w+\epsilon_{\rm act})^{1/2}$.
The coefficients of $\phi$ are fixed when taking derivatives with
respect to $z$. Differentiating the scale and normalized input gives
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_z\rho=u/w,\qquad
 \frac{\partial u}{\partial z}=\rho^{-1}(I-uu^\top/w).
\end{equation}
Applying the product rule to $\rho\phi(u)$ then yields
\begin{equation}
 J=\operatorname{Diag}(\phi'(u))+
 \frac{[\phi(u)-u\odot\phi'(u)]u^\top}{w}.
 \label{eq:grain-jacobian}
\end{equation}
The first term is the coordinatewise rational slope. The second is
the response through the shared normalization scale. At the
absolute-value kinks, we use the selected derivative
$\operatorname{sign}(0)=0$.

This expression distinguishes changes in feature shape from changes
in amplitude. A perturbation orthogonal to $u$ leaves $\rho$ unchanged
to first order, so only the diagonal term acts. For a radial
perturbation, set $r_\phi=\phi(u)-u\odot\phi'(u)$ and use
$\|u\|_2^2/w=1-\epsilon_{\rm act}/\rho^2$ to obtain
\begin{equation}
 Jz=\Phi(z)-\frac{\epsilon_{\rm act}}{\rho}r_\phi.
 \label{eq:grain-radial}
\end{equation}
In particular, when $\epsilon_{\rm act}=0$ and $z\ne0$,
$\Phi(az)=a\Phi(z)$ for every $a>0$. The rational curve changes
the response shape while positive input rescaling is carried to
the output. Equation~\ref{eq:grain-radial} gives the correction for
a positive stabilizer. For a linear curve $\phi(u)=au$, $r_\phi=0$
and $\Phi(z)=az$ for every stabilizer value.

The two derivative actions used in TILLER follow from the same
diagonal-plus-rank-one form:
\begin{equation}
 Jv=\phi'(u)\odot v+r_\phi\frac{u^\top v}{w},\qquad
 J^\top\zeta=\phi'(u)\odot\zeta+
 u\frac{r_\phi^\top\zeta}{w}.
 \label{eq:grain-jacobian-actions}
\end{equation}
Thus both the forward response to a perturbation and the backward
loss sensitivity retain the effect of normalization. Each action
uses one inner product in addition to coordinatewise products.

\section{The fixed-norm allocation problem}
\label{sec:transaction}

Consider prescribed paired directions $(q_i^A,q_i^B)$. Their parameter
blocks are disjoint, so the feed-forward update before weight decay has
norm
\begin{equation}
 \|\Delta\theta(c)\|_2^2
 =\eta_t^2\sum_i c_i^2\left(\frob{q_i^A}^2+\frob{q_i^B}^2\right)
 =\eta_t^2c^\top Wc,
 \qquad W=\operatorname{Diag}(\omega).
 \label{eq:allocation-norm}
\end{equation}
The constraint $c^\top Wc=\mathcal E_0$, where
$\mathcal E_0=\boldsymbol1^\top W\boldsymbol1$, therefore fixes
the direction norm at its unit-allocation value. A shared coefficient
preserves the relative scaling of each group's incoming and outgoing
directions, including when that coefficient is negative.

For positive $\omega_i$ and $\eta_t$, substitute $y=W^{1/2}c$
in the objective. This gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \frac{F_t(c)}{\eta_t^2}
 =\mathcal Q_t(y)=\tfrac12 y^\top M_ty-b_t^\top y,
 \qquad \|y\|_2^2=\mathcal E_0,\\
 M_t=W^{-1/2}G_tW^{-1/2},\qquad
 b_t=W^{-1/2}(s_t/\eta_t-r_t).
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:whitened-transaction}
\end{equation}
Differentiating the Lagrangian
$\mathcal Q_t(y)+\lambda(\|y\|_2^2-\mathcal E_0)/2$
yields $(M_t+\lambda I)y=b_t$, the relation stated in the Method.
The positive semidefiniteness of $G_t$ makes $M_t+\lambda I$
positive definite for $\lambda>0$.

TILLER represents this relation in a Lanczos-generated Krylov subspace
\citep{lanczos1950iteration}. Let $Q_k$ be its orthonormal basis and
let $T_k$ be the Lanczos tridiagonal model. Clipping negative
numerical eigenvalues gives $T_k^+=U\operatorname{Diag}(\theta)U^\top$.
For $\gamma=U^\top Q_k^\top b_t$, projected stationarity gives
\begin{equation}
 y=Q_kU\operatorname{Diag}((\theta+\lambda)^{-1})\gamma,
 \qquad
 \psi(\lambda)=\sum_j\frac{\gamma_j^2}{(\theta_j+\lambda)^2}
 =\mathcal E_0.
 \label{eq:secular-equation}
\end{equation}
The scalar equation enforces the norm of the projected point. Its
positive branch has the following characterization.

\begin{proposition}[Positive-multiplier allocation]
\label{prop:allocation}
Let $T_k^+\succeq0$, $Q_k^\top Q_k=I$, and $\gamma\ne0$.
Equation~\ref{eq:secular-equation} has at most one root with
$\lambda>0$. If such a root exists, its projected coordinates are
the unique minimizer of the clipped projected quadratic on the sphere
of squared radius $\mathcal E_0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For positive $\lambda$,
$\psi'(\lambda)=-2\sum_j\gamma_j^2/(\theta_j+\lambda)^3<0$,
which proves uniqueness of the root. Put $b_k=Q_k^\top b_t$ and
$z_*=(T_k^++\lambda I)^{-1}b_k$.
For any $z$ on the same sphere, stationarity and equality of norms give
\begin{equation}
 \left(\tfrac12z^\top T_k^+z-b_k^\top z\right)
 -\left(\tfrac12z_*^\top T_k^+z_*-b_k^\top z_*\right)
 =\tfrac12(z-z_*)^\top(T_k^++\lambda I)(z-z_*).
 \label{eq:projected-model-difference}
\end{equation}
The right side is positive unless $z=z_*$.
\end{proof}

The mapped coefficients $c=W^{-1/2}y$ supply a proposal for $F_t$.
Its improvement is evaluated with the current metric $G_t$;
acceptance also requires the prescribed norm tolerance.

Norm preservation permits sign changes within a layer. Their aggregate
effect on a layer and matrix role is measured by
\begin{equation}
 \sum_g c_{\ell g}\inner{g_{\ell g}^r}{q_{\ell g}^r},\qquad
 \sum_g c_{\ell g}\inner{m_{\ell g}^r}{q_{\ell g}^r},
 \qquad r\in\{A,B\}.
 \label{eq:transaction-contractions}
\end{equation}
Requiring these contractions to be positive for both the proposal and
the unit allocation constrains the whole incoming and outgoing updates,
while allowing individual group coefficients to be negative.

\section{The joint loss-response model}
\label{sec:loss-measurements}

Consider the block $B_i\Phi_i(A_ix_{k\ell})$ at a fixed position.
The derivative along its paired matrix direction is
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\alpha}
 (B_i+\alpha q_i^B)\Phi_i((A_i+\alpha q_i^A)x_{k\ell})
 \right|_{\alpha=0}
 = B_iJ_{ki}q_i^Ax_{k\ell}+q_i^Bh_{ki}.
 \label{eq:functional-score}
\end{equation}
Contracting with the downstream loss derivative gives $S_{t,ki}$
in equation~\ref{eq:overview-score}. The derivative at a hidden
position collects its effect on the entire downstream loss. With
microbatch mean loss over $N$ positions and $M$ accumulation steps,
the captured derivative $\bar\xi$ has scale $1/(NM)$; thus
$\xi=NM\chi_{\rm clip}\bar\xi$, where $\chi_{\rm clip}$ is the
realized gradient-clipping factor, held fixed in this calculation.
Averaging over all positions and processes recovers the clipped
directional derivative
$s_i=\inner{g_i^A}{q_i^A}+\inner{g_i^B}{q_i^B}$.

The full gradient determines average descent. Shared-position
measurements additionally determine how directions act together.
For columns $S_i$ and $S_j$, their contribution to the squared
joint response is $2c_ic_jS_i^\top S_j/K$.
For the scaled directions, the sign of $c_ic_jS_i^\top S_j$
determines reinforcement or cancellation. These cross terms couple
groups even when they occupy
disjoint parameter blocks. Weight decay has the same differential:
replacing the directions by $(\lambda_{\rm wd} A_i,\lambda_{\rm wd} B_i)$ and summing
over $i$ gives a response $a^{\rm dec}$. Expanding the combined response,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\|Sc+a^{\rm dec}\|_2^2}{K}
 =c^\top\frac{S^\top S}{K}c
 +2c^\top\frac{S^\top a^{\rm dec}}{K}
 +\frac{\|a^{\rm dec}\|_2^2}{K}.
 \label{eq:functional-response-energy}
\end{equation}
The final term is independent of the allocation. The first two are
the response metric and decay cross term in $F_t$. Their discounted
history supplies the quadratic penalty, while the current $s_t$
supplies the linear descent term.

\subsection{Approximation of the response history}
\label{sec:ledger}

Let $S_t$ denote the measured rows on a probe step. Between probes,
the row model is $S_t=\boldsymbol1_K(\gamma_ts_t)^\top$, where
$\gamma_t=\nu/\|s_t\|_2$ for nonzero $s_t$ and
$\nu=\|S_{\rm ref}\|_{\rm F}/\sqrt K$ is the latest measured
scale. This model has
$S_t^\top S_t/K=\nu^2s_ts_t^\top/\|s_t\|_2^2$:
its orientation follows the current gradient contractions and its
trace equals the measured $\nu^2$. For $s_t=0$, set $\gamma_t=0$, giving zero rows.
The corresponding decay rows are
$a_t^{\rm dec}=\boldsymbol1_K\gamma_td_t^{\rm dec}$, with
$d_t^{\rm dec}$ the gradient contraction with the decay direction.

For the resulting sequence of measured and modeled responses, with
the second-moment decay $\beta=\beta_2$, define
\begin{equation}
 G_t^\star=\beta G_{t-1}^\star+(1-\beta)S_t^\top S_t/K,
 \qquad G_1^\star=S_1^\top S_1/K.
 \label{eq:ledger-target}
\end{equation}
Each column retains its layer--group identity; each historical row
records the direction used at its own training step.
The ledger approximates this metric by
$\widehat G_t=\mathcal B_t^\top\mathcal B_t/K+\tau_t I$.
If
$\mathcal A_t=[\sqrt\beta\mathcal B_{t-1};\sqrt{1-\beta}S_t]$,
the metric for the current allocation is
\begin{equation}
 G_t=\mathcal A_t^\top\mathcal A_t/K+\beta\tau_{t-1}I,
 \qquad r_t=\beta r_{t-1}+(1-\beta)S_t^\top a_t^{\rm dec}/K.
 \label{eq:current-ledger-metric}
\end{equation}
The first observation is unattenuated:
$\mathcal A_1=\mathcal B_1=S_1$, $\tau_1=0$, and
$r_1=S_1^\top a_1^{\rm dec}/K$. Fresh rows enter $G_t$ before
compression.

The Robust Frequent Directions approximation
\citep{ghashami2016frequent,luo2019robust} has a direct spectral
description. Let $\sigma_j$ and $v_j$ be the singular values and
right singular vectors of $\mathcal A_t$. For a retained rank $r$,
set $\Delta_t=\sigma_{r+1}^2$, or zero if that singular value is
absent. The retained factor has rows
$\sqrt{\sigma_j^2-\Delta_t}\,v_j^\top$ for the available $j\leq r$,
and $\tau_t=\beta\tau_{t-1}+\Delta_t/(2K)$.
The isotropic increment centers the error from spectral shrinkage.

\begin{proposition}[Error in the response metric]
\label{prop:ledger-error}
For finite response rows, $K>0$, $r\geq K$, $0\leq\beta\leq1$, and
$\tau_{t-1}\geq0$, $\widehat G_t$ is positive semidefinite and
\begin{equation}
 \|\widehat G_t-G_t\|_2\leq\frac{\Delta_t}{2K},\qquad
 |c^\top(\widehat G_t-G_t)c|
 \leq\frac{\Delta_t}{2K}\|c\|_2^2.
 \label{eq:rfd-error}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The factor Gram matrix and isotropic term are positive semidefinite.
In the right-singular-vector basis, a retained direction loses
$\Delta_t/K$ by shrinkage and gains $\Delta_t/(2K)$, giving
error $-\Delta_t/(2K)$. A discarded direction with squared singular
value $s\in[0,\Delta_t]$ has error $(\Delta_t/2-s)/K$.
The same expression with $s=0$ covers null directions. Every error
eigenvalue therefore lies in
$[-\Delta_t/(2K),\Delta_t/(2K)]$, proving both bounds.
\end{proof}

Without the isotropic increment, the error interval is
$[-\Delta_t/K,0]$. Centering halves this one-compression spectral
bound. Let $E_t=\widehat G_t-G_t^\star$. Since
$E_t=\beta E_{t-1}+(\widehat G_t-G_t)$ and $E_1=0$,
\begin{equation}
 \|E_t\|_2\leq\sum_{j=2}^t\beta^{t-j}\frac{\Delta_j}{2K},
 \qquad G_t-G_t^\star=\beta E_{t-1}.
 \label{eq:rfd-history-error}
\end{equation}
This connects the sketch approximation to the coefficient model.
If $\|G_t-G_t^\star\|_2\leq\varepsilon_t$, replacing $G_t$ by
$G_t^\star$ changes $F_t(c)$ by at most
$\eta_t^2\varepsilon_t\|c\|_2^2/2$.
Under the fixed-norm constraint and positive $\omega_i$, this is
at most $\eta_t^2\varepsilon_t\mathcal E_0/(2\min_i\omega_i)$,
where $\mathcal E_0=\sum_i\omega_i$.


\section{Geometry of response-guided directions}
\label{sec:directions}

The factorized direction is determined by the marginals of the full
gradient second-moment history. Let $R_t$ and $C_t$ denote its row
and column sums, and let $m_t$ be the shared momentum. Since summation
commutes with exponential averaging, these marginals can be represented
by their own histories. With zero-initialized moments, the adaptive
transformation is
\begin{equation}
 V_t=\frac{R_tC_t^\top}{(\boldsymbol1^\top R_t)(1-\beta_2^t)},
 \qquad a_t=\frac{m_t}{\sqrt{V_t}+\epsilon_{\rm opt}}.
 \label{eq:factored-direction}
\end{equation}
All operations in the second expression are entrywise. For positive
total second moment, the normalized outer product is the rank-one
approximation determined by its row and column marginals
\citep{shazeer2018adafactor}.

The group calibration follows from the norm of an exact polar factor.
The parent is $p=\chi\mathcal P_5(m)$, where $\mathcal P_5$ is the
five-iteration Newton--Schulz polar approximation
\citep{jordan2024muon,liu2025muon}. For $G$ groups of width $w$
and total intermediate width $H_r=Gw$,
$\chi=\sqrt{\min(H_r,d)/(G\min(w,d))}$.
An exact full-rank polar factor of a group matrix has squared
Frobenius norm $\min(w,d)$; hence this calibration makes the sum
over groups equal the whole-projection value $\min(H_r,d)$.
The selected feed-forward direction also receives the usual
whole-matrix multiplier $\mu=0.2\sqrt{\max(H_r,d)}$.

The rotation is specified by the learned response geometry.
For nonzero $J$ and $h$, define
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{pr}_A(J)=
 \frac{\operatorname{tr}(JJ^\top)^2}{w\|JJ^\top\|_{\rm F}^2},
 \qquad \operatorname{pr}_B(h)=\frac{\|h\|_2^4}{w\|h\|_4^4}.
 \label{eq:response-summary-components}
\end{equation}
Both are normalized participation ratios. The first applies to squared
singular values of the Jacobian and the second to squared feature
coordinates. Equal energy in $m$ nonzero components gives $m/w$;
the ratios lie in $[1/w,1]$ by Cauchy--Schwarz.
Their weighted averages, denoted $\pi_i^A$ and $\pi_i^B$, use
$\alpha_{ki}^A=\|J_{ki}^\top B_i^\top\xi_{k\ell}\|_2^2/w$
and $\alpha_{ki}^B=\|\xi_{k\ell}\|_2^2/d$:
\begin{equation}
 \pi_i^r=\frac{\sum_k\alpha_{ki}^r\operatorname{pr}_r(\mathcal R_{ki}^r)}
 {\sum_k\alpha_{ki}^r},\qquad
 \mathcal R_{ki}^A=J_{ki},\quad\mathcal R_{ki}^B=h_{ki}.
 \label{eq:participation-main}
\end{equation}
Zero responses contribute zero participation. The weights are the
squared loss sensitivities of the incoming and outgoing paths.

To compare the learned response with its initialization, define
$\Gamma_{ki}^A=J_{ki}J_{ki}^\top$ and
$\Gamma_{ki}^B=h_{ki}h_{ki}^\top$.
A superscript $0$ denotes the same evaluation with initial rational
coefficients, at the current inputs and scales. For either role,
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_i^r=
 \frac{\sum_k\alpha_{ki}^r\inner{\Gamma_{ki}^r}{\Gamma_{ki}^{r,0}}}
 {\sqrt{\sum_k\alpha_{ki}^r\|\Gamma_{ki}^r\|_{\rm F}^2}
  \sqrt{\sum_k\alpha_{ki}^r\|\Gamma_{ki}^{r,0}\|_{\rm F}^2}}.
 \label{eq:congruence-main}
\end{equation}
This is a cosine in the space of weighted kernel collections.
Positive semidefiniteness and Cauchy--Schwarz give $0\leq\kappa_i^r\leq1$.
These weighted ratios apply when their denominators are positive;
otherwise the group's rotation fractions are zero.
Using the same current inputs in both collections isolates the change
in the learned activation. If $\psi=\arccos\kappa$, the fraction
$e=\pi(1-\kappa^2)=\pi\sin^2\psi$ combines the kernel angle with
the breadth of its response.

For a nonzero parent and tangent, the corresponding matrix rotation is
\begin{equation}
 a^\perp=a-\frac{\inner{a}{p}}{\frob{p}^2}p,
 \qquad \widetilde q=
 \sqrt{1-e}\,p+\sigma\sqrt e\frac{\frob{p}}{\frob{a^\perp}}a^\perp,
 \label{eq:tangent-chord}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ makes $\sigma\inner{g}{a^\perp}\geq0$.
Orthogonality gives
\begin{equation}
 \frob{\widetilde q}^2=\frob p^2,
 \qquad
 \inner{g}{\widetilde q}
 =\sqrt{1-e}\inner{g}{p}
 +\sqrt e\frac{\frob p}{\frob{a^\perp}}
 |\inner{g}{a^\perp}|.
 \label{eq:rotation-property}
\end{equation}
The angle $\vartheta$ between $p$ and $\widetilde q$ therefore
satisfies $\sin^2\vartheta=\pi\sin^2\psi$.
Broad responses admit larger rotations for the same kernel departure;
an unchanged kernel gives zero rotation. The common multiplier $\mu$
preserves the angle and multiplies both squared norms by $\mu^2$.

Attention uses the layer's two response roles jointly. Let
$\overline\pi_\ell^A,\overline\pi_\ell^B$ be group-averaged
participation, and let $\kappa_\ell^A,\kappa_\ell^B$ use kernel
inner products and squared norms aggregated across groups.
Its rotation fraction is
$e_\ell^{\rm att}=\sqrt{\overline\pi_\ell^A\overline\pi_\ell^B}
(1-\kappa_\ell^A\kappa_\ell^B)$.
All four role statistics lie in $[0,1]$, so this layerwise fraction
also lies in $[0,1]$.
