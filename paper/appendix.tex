\section{Jacobian of the normalized rational activation}
\label{sec:grain}
\label{app:grain-calculus}

An incoming matrix update changes both the normalized arguments of
GRAIN and the scale restored at its output. Differentiating both
paths gives the response used by TILLER. For one group, suppress
the layer and group indices and write $z=Ax\in\mathbb R^w$:
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(z)=\rho\phi(u),\qquad
 u=z/\rho,\qquad
 \rho=\left(z^\top z/w+\epsilon_{\rm act}\right)^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:grain-normalize}
\end{equation}
The scalar rational function in equation~\ref{eq:overview-rational}
acts coordinatewise on $u$; its coefficients are held fixed in
the derivatives below.

The normalized coordinates describe relative feature magnitudes,
while $\rho$ carries the group amplitude. When
$\epsilon_{\rm act}=0$, a nonzero input satisfies
$\Phi(az)=a\Phi(z)$ for every $a>0$: positive rescaling leaves the
arguments of the learned curve unchanged and scales its output by
$a$. The implemented positive stabilizer keeps the map defined at
the origin. Retaining this stabilizer, the derivatives of the scale
and normalized coordinates are
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_z\rho=\frac{u}{w},\qquad
 \frac{\partial u}{\partial z}
 =\rho^{-1}\left(I-\frac{uu^\top}{w}\right).
 \label{eq:grain-normalization-derivative}
\end{equation}
When $\epsilon_{\rm act}=0$, the matrix in parentheses projects
onto the subspace orthogonal to $u$. Normalization then removes a
radial perturbation from the curve's input, while the outer factor
$\rho$ retains its effect on output amplitude.

At differentiability points, let $f=\phi(u)$,
$d_\phi=\phi'(u)$, and $r_\phi=f-u\odot d_\phi$. At a zero
scalar argument, the absolute-value terms use the selected
autodifferentiation value $\operatorname{sign}(0)=0$.
At these kinks, subsequent uses of $J$ denote the resulting selected
linearization.
The product rule combines the two response paths:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 J=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z}
 &=\frac{fu^\top}{w}
   +\operatorname{Diag}(d_\phi)
       \left(I-\frac{uu^\top}{w}\right)\\
 &=\operatorname{Diag}(d_\phi)+\frac{r_\phi u^\top}{w}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:grain-jacobian}
\end{equation}
The diagonal term applies the rational slope at each coordinate.
The rank-one term couples the coordinates through their shared
scale, with $r_\phi$ measuring the difference between the response
value and its local linear part. For $\phi(u)=a u$, this difference
vanishes and $J=aI$, consistent with the exact cancellation
$\Phi(z)=az$ even when $\epsilon_{\rm act}>0$. Learning a nonlinear
curve changes both its coordinatewise slopes and this coupling.

The same decomposition separates changes in feature proportions
from changes in amplitude. For a perturbation $v$ with
$u^\top v=0$, the scale has zero first-order change and
$Jv=d_\phi\odot v$. Along the input itself,
$u^\top u/w=1-\epsilon_{\rm act}/\rho^2$ gives
$Jz=\Phi(z)-(\epsilon_{\rm act}/\rho)r_\phi$.
The radial response therefore recovers $Jz=\Phi(z)$ in the
unstabilized case, as required by positive homogeneity.

TILLER applies this Jacobian through coordinatewise products and
inner products. For an input perturbation $v\in\mathbb R^w$ and
an output sensitivity $\zeta\in\mathbb R^w$,
\begin{equation}
 Jv=d_\phi\odot v+r_\phi\frac{u^\top v}{w},\qquad
 J^\top\zeta=d_\phi\odot\zeta+
                 u\frac{r_\phi^\top\zeta}{w}.
 \label{eq:grain-jacobian-actions}
\end{equation}
Each action requires one inner product after the response and slope
have been evaluated, avoiding a dense $w\times w$ Jacobian. In
the paired projection $B\Phi(Ax)$, an incoming matrix direction
$U$ acts through $BJUx$, whereas an outgoing direction $V$ acts
through $V\Phi(Ax)$. These two paths supply the Jacobian and
feature summaries used in Appendix~\ref{sec:directions}.

\section{Response-guided matrix directions}
\label{sec:directions}

TILLER chooses the orientation of each matrix update before
coordinating update strengths across groups. It forms two
transformations of the same momentum: a groupwise Muon direction
and a factorized adaptive direction. The adaptive component
orthogonal to the Muon direction supplies a tangent. The learned
response controls the rotation along this tangent, and the current
gradient determines its sign. Preserving the calibrated Muon norm
leaves the joint allocation in Appendix~\ref{sec:loss-measurements}
to distribute a fixed update budget.

For group $i=(\ell,g)$ and role $r\in\{A,B\}$, let
$g_{t,i}^r$ denote the training gradient after accumulation,
distributed averaging, and global clipping. Starting from
$v_{0,i}^r=0$, the first-moment buffer and Nesterov momentum are
\begin{equation}
 v_{t,i}^r=\beta_1v_{t-1,i}^r+(1-\beta_1)g_{t,i}^r,\qquad
 m_{t,i}^r=\beta_1v_{t,i}^r+(1-\beta_1)g_{t,i}^r.
 \label{eq:nesterov-momentum}
\end{equation}
Muon rebalances the singular values of this momentum with an
approximate polar map $\mathcal P_5$ using five Newton--Schulz
iterations \citep{jordan2024muon,liu2025muon}. The map acts on each
$w\times d$ incoming row block and each $d\times w$ outgoing
column block. For total rational intermediate width $H_r=Gw$, define the
parent direction and calibration factors by
\begin{equation}
 p_{t,i}^r=\chi_{\rm grp}\mathcal P_5(m_{t,i}^r),\qquad
 \chi_{\rm grp}=\sqrt{\frac{\min\{H_r,d\}}{G\min\{w,d\}}},\qquad
 \mu_{\rm mlp}=0.2\sqrt{\max\{H_r,d\}}.
 \label{eq:group-polar-calibration}
\end{equation}
An exact polar map with full block rank would give total squared
Frobenius norm $G\min\{w,d\}$ across the group blocks. The factor
$\chi_{\rm grp}$ matches this nominal norm to $\min\{H_r,d\}$ for
a whole projection, for either matrix role. The rotation is formed
at the scale of $p_{t,i}^r$; the final Muon multiplier
$\mu_{\rm mlp}$ is applied after selection.

The adaptive transformation rescales momentum entries using the
history of squared gradients \citep{shazeer2018adafactor}.
Suppress the group and role indices, initialize $R_0=C_0=0$, and set
\begin{align}
 R_t&=\beta_2R_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)\operatorname{rowsum}(g_t^2),
 \nonumber\\
 C_t&=\beta_2C_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)\operatorname{colsum}(g_t^2),
 \nonumber\\
 V_{t,jk}&=\frac{R_{t,j}C_{t,k}}
 {\max\{\sum_sR_{t,s},\delta_{\rm fp}\}(1-\beta_2^t)},\qquad
 a_t=\frac{m_t}{\sqrt{V_t}+\epsilon_{\rm opt}}.
 \label{eq:factored-direction}
\end{align}
Squares, square roots, and division are elementwise. Row and column
sums commute with exponential averaging, so $R_t$ and $C_t$ are
the corresponding marginals of the full second-moment history.
Their normalized outer product approximates that history with
additional storage linear in the matrix dimensions. Both $p_t$
and $a_t$ transform the same momentum: the former operates on
singular values, while the latter adapts to the gradient scales
of matrix entries. Numerical constants, including the denominator
floor $\delta_{\rm fp}$, are specified in
Appendix~\ref{app:numerical}.

The two directions define a rotation in the Frobenius geometry of
matrix updates. Removing the component of $a_t$ parallel to $p_t$
leaves a tangent to the sphere through $p_t$. Moving along this
tangent introduces entrywise gradient-scale information while
preserving the parent's norm. The response statistics below choose
the rotation angle; they are computed separately for the incoming
and outgoing paths.

The activation response determines how far the update rotates from
the parent. At sampled position $k$, write $J_{ki}$ for the group
Jacobian and $h_{ki}=\Phi_i(A_ix_{k\ell})$ for its features.
The incoming path acts through $J_{ki}$; the outgoing path
recombines $h_{ki}$. Their response breadth is measured by
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{pr}_A(J)=
 \frac{[\operatorname{tr}(JJ^\top)]^2}{w\frob{JJ^\top}^2},\qquad
 \operatorname{pr}_B(h)=\frac{\|h\|_2^4}{w\|h\|_4^4}.
 \label{eq:response-summary-components}
\end{equation}
The incoming ratio measures concentration across squared singular
values; the outgoing ratio measures concentration across squared
feature coordinates. Equal energy in $m$ nonzero components gives
participation $m/w$, while concentration in one gives $1/w$.
Thus incoming breadth is spectral and outgoing breadth refers to
the chosen feature coordinates.

Positions receive more weight when the corresponding path is more
sensitive to the loss. Let $\xi_{k\ell}\in\mathbb R^d$ be the
downstream derivative at the block output. With
$\mathcal R^A_{ki}=J_{ki}$ and $\mathcal R^B_{ki}=h_{ki}$, define
\begin{equation}
 \alpha^A_{ki}=\frac{\|J_{ki}^\top B_i^\top\xi_{k\ell}\|_2^2}{w},
 \qquad
 \alpha^B_{ki}=\frac{\|\xi_{k\ell}\|_2^2}{d},\qquad
 \pi_i^r=\frac{\sum_k\alpha^r_{ki}
                  \operatorname{pr}_r(\mathcal R^r_{ki})}
                  {\sum_k\alpha^r_{ki}}.
 \label{eq:participation-main}
\end{equation}
The incoming weight is the mean squared loss derivative with respect
to the preactivation; the outgoing weight is the corresponding
quantity at the block output, shared across groups in the layer.
These local sensitivities emphasize response geometry at positions
where feature perturbations affect the loss. Their use separates
the sensitivity weighting from the input or feature amplitude that
also enters a full matrix-gradient norm.

We compare the current response with the initial rational curve at
the same current inputs. For this comparison, define the kernels
$\Gamma^A_{ki}=J_{ki}J_{ki}^\top$ and
$\Gamma^B_{ki}=h_{ki}h_{ki}^\top$. The first describes how the
Jacobian distributes an isotropic input perturbation over feature
coordinates; the second records the feature vector's orientation
and magnitude. A superscript $0$ denotes evaluation with the
initial rational coefficients at the current normalized input and
RMS scale. The loss-weighted kernel cosine, which we call
congruence, is
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_i^r=
 \frac{\sum_k\alpha^r_{ki}
             \inner{\Gamma^r_{ki}}{\Gamma^{r,0}_{ki}}}
 {\sqrt{\left(\sum_k\alpha^r_{ki}\frob{\Gamma^r_{ki}}^2\right)
              \left(\sum_k\alpha^r_{ki}\frob{\Gamma^{r,0}_{ki}}^2\right)}}.
 \label{eq:congruence-main}
\end{equation}
Here $\inner{\cdot}{\cdot}$ is the Frobenius inner product.
Congruence is the cosine between the two collections of kernels,
with each position weighted by $\sqrt{\alpha^r_{ki}}$.
Evaluating both curves at the current input isolates the effect of
the learned coefficients on this geometry. The comparison is
invariant to a common positive scaling of the kernel collection
and to sign reversals of $J$ or $h$; it measures alignment of
response energy. In particular, $\phi=c\phi^0$ with $c\ne0$ scales
both kernels by $c^2$ and gives $\kappa=1$. Overall response gain
still enters the joint loss scores through $J$ and $h$, while this
cosine controls rotation according to kernel alignment.

The polar and adaptive transformations use different information from
the same momentum. The polar map rebalances singular values, whereas
factorized adaptation incorporates row and column gradient scales.
Learning the activation changes the response through which these
directions act. We use the initial curve as a reference for controlling
how much adaptive information enters the direction: kernel departure
permits rotation away from the polar parent, while current response
participation attenuates this rotation when response energy is
concentrated. The squared tangent weight is
$e_i^r=\pi_i^r(1-(\kappa_i^r)^2)$. This rule relates the rotation
angle to the angle between response kernels. If
$\psi_i^r=\arccos\kappa_i^r$ is the kernel angle and
$\vartheta_i^r$ is the direction rotation, then
$\sin^2\vartheta_i^r=\pi_i^r\sin^2\psi_i^r$.
Participation therefore attenuates the kernel angle:
$\vartheta_i^r\leq\psi_i^r$, with equality at full participation.
Aligned kernels retain the parent direction; a broad response
whose kernel geometry has changed allows a larger adaptive
component.

The statistics keep this rule within its angular range. For
nonnegative component energies $v_j$,
$\sum_jv_j^2\leq(\sum_jv_j)^2\leq w\sum_jv_j^2$ places each
nonzero participation ratio in $[1/w,1]$.
Assigning zero to a zero response and taking the loss-weighted
average gives $0\leq\pi_i^r\leq1$ when the total weight is positive.
The kernel products satisfy
$\inner{JJ^\top}{J^0J^{0\top}}=\frob{J^\top J^0}^2$ and
$\inner{hh^\top}{h^0h^{0\top}}=(h^\top h^0)^2$.
Nonnegativity and Cauchy--Schwarz give
$0\leq\kappa_i^r\leq1$ whenever its denominator is positive.
Together with the zero-response and zero-sensitivity conventions
in Appendix~\ref{app:numerical}, these bounds give
$0\leq e_i^r\leq1$.
\label{app:response-proof}

To form the rotation, suppress time and role indices and remove
the adaptive direction's component parallel to the parent:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 a_i^\perp&=a_i-
       \frac{\inner{a_i}{p_i}}{\frob{p_i}^2}p_i,\\
 \widetilde q_i&=\sqrt{1-e_i}\,p_i+
       \sigma_i\sqrt{e_i}\,
       \frac{\frob{p_i}}{\frob{a_i^\perp}}a_i^\perp.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:tangent-chord}
\end{equation}
For nonzero parent and tangent, this rotates $p_i$ through
$\vartheta_i\in[0,\pi/2]$. Orthogonality gives
$\frob{\widetilde q_i}^2=(1-e_i)\frob{p_i}^2+
e_i\frob{p_i}^2=\frob{p_i}^2$ in exact arithmetic.
We set $\sigma_i=1$ when
$\inner{g_i}{a_i^\perp}\geq0$ and $\sigma_i=-1$ otherwise.
The tangent then contributes nonnegatively to
$\inner{g_i}{\widetilde q_i}$, the first-order descent signal for
a subtraction update.

Selection checks this descent signal conditional on the parent.
An admissible candidate is used only when both
$\inner{g_i}{p_i}>0$ and
$\inner{g_i}{\widetilde q_i}>0$; otherwise the parent is retained.
Admissibility requires finite values and nondegenerate directions,
with the exact arithmetic tests given in
Appendix~\ref{app:numerical}. The final direction $q_i$ is
$\mu_{\rm mlp}$ times the selection, so
$\frob{q_i}=\frob{\mu_{\rm mlp}p_i}$ in exact arithmetic when
the numerical floors are inactive. A fallback has this norm
directly.

The attention projections help determine the representations processed
by the learned feed-forward map in the same Transformer layer. We use
that map's response geometry as a shared layer signal for the attention
rotation. Each attention matrix supplies its own momentum, factorized
moments, tangent, and gradient-selected sign; only the rotation fraction
is shared. Let
$\overline\pi_\ell^r=G^{-1}\sum_g\pi_{\ell g}^r$, and form
$\kappa_\ell^r$ by summing the numerator and the two squared-norm
sums in equation~\ref{eq:congruence-main} across groups before
taking the cosine. The QKV and attention-output matrices share
\begin{equation}
 e_\ell^{\rm att}=
 \sqrt{\overline\pi_\ell^A\overline\pi_\ell^B}
       \left(1-\kappa_\ell^A\kappa_\ell^B\right).
 \label{eq:attention-response}
\end{equation}
Both feed-forward paths contribute to the breadth factor, while
departure in either kernel collection can increase the rotation.
Each matrix uses equation~\ref{eq:tangent-chord} with this fraction.
Appendix~\ref{app:numerical} specifies the
attention polar blocks and calibration, and
Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm} specifies response refreshes.
Attention uses the selected, calibrated direction directly. The
joint coefficients derived next apply to the paired GRAIN projections.



\section{Joint loss-response coordination}
\label{sec:loss-measurements}

The directions in Appendix~\ref{sec:directions} specify how each matrix
changes. Their strengths must also be chosen jointly: directions in
disjoint parameter blocks can reinforce or offset one another through
the network. TILLER measures their loss responses at shared activation
positions, retains a discounted second moment of these responses, and
uses it to allocate the current feed-forward direction norm. The
gradient supplies the current descent signal; the response history
supplies information about how the directions act together.

\subsection{Loss responses at shared positions}

For a layer--group pair $i=(\ell,g)$, fix the current direction
$(q_i^A,q_i^B)$ and a probe position $k$. Let $x_{k\ell}$ be the
block input, $h_{ki}=\Phi_i(A_ix_{k\ell})$ its group features, and
$J_{ki}$ the Jacobian in equation~\ref{eq:grain-jacobian}.
Differentiating
$(B_i+\alpha q_i^B)\Phi_i((A_i+\alpha q_i^A)x_{k\ell})$
at $\alpha=0$ gives the block-output perturbation and its contraction
with the downstream loss derivative:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \delta y_{ki}&=B_iJ_{ki}q_i^Ax_{k\ell}+q_i^Bh_{ki},\\
 S_{t,ki}&=\inner{q_i^Ax_{k\ell}}{J_{ki}^\top B_i^\top\xi_{k\ell}}
             +\inner{q_i^Bh_{ki}}{\xi_{k\ell}}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:functional-score}
\end{equation}
The incoming term changes the features through their Jacobian; the
outgoing term changes their recombination. Here $\xi_{k\ell}$ is the
derivative of the summed minibatch loss with respect to the block
output at that position, multiplied by the realized global clipping
factor. The cotangent collects that hidden position's influence on
the downstream loss terms. Thus each score records one position's contribution to the
loss derivative along a paired matrix direction.

The probe derivatives use the same loss scaling as the parameter
gradients. Suppose each microbatch contains $R$ positions and its
mean loss is divided by the number $M$ of accumulated microbatches
before backpropagation. The captured derivative $\bar\xi_{k\ell}$
then includes the factor $1/(RM)$. We recover the summed-loss scale
and apply the realized clipping factor $\chi_{\rm clip}$ through
\begin{equation}
 \xi_{k\ell}=RM\chi_{\rm clip}\bar\xi_{k\ell}.
 \label{eq:cotangent-rescaling}
\end{equation}
The clipping factor is held fixed when evaluating a direction.
Averaging the scores over all positions recovers the corresponding
clipped minibatch derivative. For the current paired directions, that
derivative is available directly from the full parameter gradients:
\begin{equation}
 s_{t,i}=\inner{g_{t,i}^A}{q_{t,i}^A}
         +\inner{g_{t,i}^B}{q_{t,i}^B}.
 \label{eq:current-score}
\end{equation}
TILLER therefore uses $s_t$ without probe approximation in the linear
descent term. The matrix $S_t\in\mathbb R^{K\times C}$ records how
these contributions vary across the $K$ shared positions, where
$C=LG$ is the number of layer--group pairs.

For an allocation $c$, the combined responses are $S_tc$. Their
second moment includes the cross products between groups, so it can
distinguish allocations that have the same individual descent scores.
For example, the columns $(2,0)^\top$ and $(0,2)^\top$ each have mean
one and individual mean squared response two. Their sum has mean
squared response four. Two identical columns $(2,0)^\top$ have the
same individual summaries, but their sum has mean squared response
eight. At this fixed measurement, the cross terms identify response
overlap that the individual summaries omit.

Weight decay enters the response model through the same calculation.
Substitute $(\lambda_{\rm wd}A_i,\lambda_{\rm wd}B_i)$ for the paired
direction in equation~\ref{eq:functional-score} and sum over all
GRAIN groups to obtain $a_t^{\rm dec}\in\mathbb R^K$. Expanding the
squared response of the structured update and decay gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac1K\|S_tc+a_t^{\rm dec}\|_2^2
 =c^\top\frac{S_t^\top S_t}{K}c
 +2c^\top\frac{S_t^\top a_t^{\rm dec}}{K}
 +\frac{\|a_t^{\rm dec}\|_2^2}{K}.
 \label{eq:functional-response-energy}
\end{equation}
The last term is independent of $c$. The first two give the response
metric and its cross term with decay, which the ledger accumulates.
We use this squared response to regularize the current linear descent
model. The linear term values improvement along the current gradient;
the penalty measures the joint magnitude of the proposed step in the
recorded loss-response coordinates. At equal current descent, the model
prefers the allocation with smaller recorded combined response, including
decay. Cross terms make this comparison sensitive to reinforcement and
cancellation, while the fixed direction norm prevents reducing the
penalty simply by shrinking the entire structured update.
The factors $\eta$ and $\eta^2$ in
equation~\ref{eq:overview-coordinate-model} express linear descent
and squared responses at the size of the applied step.

\subsection{Discounted response history}
\label{sec:ledger}

TILLER refreshes $K=32$ probes at steps $1,9,17,\ldots$. Each probe
refers to the same example--token position across layers, so a row of
$S_t$ combines responses that occurred together. A refresh recomputes
participation, congruence, and scores at the current parameters;
Appendix~\ref{app:probe-selection} specifies the position selection.
Between refreshes, the direction construction reuses the latest
participation and congruence values, while momentum, adaptive moments,
directions, and gradient contractions continue to change every step.

The intervening ledger updates use synthetic rows aligned with the
current descent vector. Let $\nu=\|S_{\rm ref}\|_{\rm F}/\sqrt K$
be the RMS row norm of the latest measured response matrix. With
$d_t^{\rm dec}$ denoting the current clipped-gradient contraction
with the GRAIN decay direction, define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 S_t=\boldsymbol1_K(\gamma_ts_t)^\top,\qquad
 a_t^{\rm dec}=\boldsymbol1_K\gamma_td_t^{\rm dec},\\
 \gamma_t=\begin{cases}
 \nu/\|s_t\|_2,&\|s_t\|_2>0,\\
 0,&\|s_t\|_2=0.
 \end{cases}
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:trace-matched-rows}
\end{equation}
For $s_t\ne0$, this second moment has rank at most one and trace
$\|S_t\|_{\rm F}^2/K=\nu^2$. The construction updates its
orientation using the current gradient while retaining the latest
measured response scale. Variation across positions is supplied by
the probe refreshes.

The uncompressed target for this sequence of measured and synthetic
rows is the discounted second moment
\begin{equation}
 G_t^\star=\beta_2G_{t-1}^\star+
          (1-\beta_2)S_t^\top S_t/K,\qquad
 G_1^\star=S_1^\top S_1/K.
 \label{eq:ledger-target}
\end{equation}
We use $\beta_2=0.95$, the same decay as the factorized second moments.
For any $c$, the quantity $c^\top G_t^\star c$ is a discounted
average of squared responses for that coefficient combination.
Columns retain their layer--group identities, and each historical
measurement retains the direction used when it was recorded.
Discounting lets recently observed relationships between group update
strengths guide the current allocation.

To store this history, TILLER retains a factor $\mathcal B_{t-1}$
and an isotropic remainder $\tau_{t-1}I$. The stored approximation
is $\widehat G_{t-1}=\mathcal B_{t-1}^\top\mathcal B_{t-1}/K+
\tau_{t-1}I$. New rows enter the metric for the current coefficient
selection at full resolution:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal A_t&=\begin{bmatrix}
 \sqrt{\beta_2}\mathcal B_{t-1}\\
 \sqrt{1-\beta_2}S_t
 \end{bmatrix},&
 G_t&=\mathcal A_t^\top\mathcal A_t/K+\beta_2\tau_{t-1}I,\\
 r_t&=\beta_2r_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)S_t^\top a_t^{\rm dec}/K.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:current-ledger-metric}
\end{equation}
At the first step, $\mathcal A_1=\mathcal B_1=S_1$,
$\tau_0=\tau_1=0$, and $r_1=S_1^\top a_1^{\rm dec}/K$.
Thus $G_t^\star$ describes the uncompressed target, $G_t$ is the
metric used now, and $\widehat G_t$ is the compressed metric saved
for later steps. The current selection uses $G_t$ before introducing
the new compression error.

We compress $\mathcal A_t$ with Robust Frequent Directions
\citep{ghashami2016frequent,luo2019robust}. Let its singular values
be $\sigma_1\geq\sigma_2\geq\cdots$ and retain at most $r=64$
rows. If its rank exceeds $r$, set $\Delta_t=\sigma_{r+1}^2$ and
subtract this value from each retained squared singular value. With
right singular vectors $V_{1:r}$, the stored representation becomes
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal B_t&=\operatorname{Diag}
 (\sqrt{\sigma_1^2-\Delta_t},\ldots,\sqrt{\sigma_r^2-\Delta_t})
 V_{1:r}^\top,\\
 \tau_t&=\beta_2\tau_{t-1}+\frac{\Delta_t}{2K}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:rfd-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
If the rank is at most $r$, we take $\Delta_t=0$ and retain an exact
factor. The retained rows describe the dominant response combinations.
Adding half the shrinkage as an isotropic term balances the error
between retained and discarded directions, as the following
exact-arithmetic bound shows.

\begin{proposition}[Response-metric approximation]
\label{prop:ledger-error}
For $K>0$, $\beta_2\in[0,1]$, and $\tau_{t-1}\geq0$, let $G_t$
and $(\mathcal B_t,\tau_t)$ be defined by
equations~\ref{eq:current-ledger-metric} and~\ref{eq:rfd-reconstruction}.
Then $\widehat G_t=\mathcal B_t^\top\mathcal B_t/K+\tau_tI$ is
positive semidefinite and
\begin{equation}
 \|\widehat G_t-G_t\|_2\leq\frac{\Delta_t}{2K},\qquad
 |c^\top(\widehat G_t-G_t)c|\leq
 \frac{\Delta_t}{2K}\|c\|_2^2
 \quad\text{for every }c\in\mathbb R^C.
 \label{eq:rfd-error}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Both terms of $\widehat G_t$ are positive semidefinite. When
$\Delta_t=0$, the metrics coincide. Otherwise, use an orthonormal
basis of right singular vectors of $\mathcal A_t$, completed by its
null space. On each retained direction, shrinkage removes
$\Delta_t/K$ and the isotropic term restores $\Delta_t/(2K)$, so
the error is $-\Delta_t/(2K)$. On a discarded direction with squared
singular value $s\in[0,\Delta_t]$, the error is $(\Delta_t/2-s)/K$;
on a null direction, it is $\Delta_t/(2K)$. All absolute eigenvalue
errors are therefore at most $\Delta_t/(2K)$. The second inequality
follows from $|c^\top Ec|\leq\|E\|_2\|c\|_2^2$ for symmetric $E$.
\end{proof}

The quadratic-form bound controls how compression changes the response
penalty for any allocation. Its effect on the history follows by
writing $E_t=\widehat G_t-G_t^\star$. Each compression contributes
$D_t=\widehat G_t-G_t$ with $\|D_t\|_2\leq\Delta_t/(2K)$, giving
$E_t=\beta_2E_{t-1}+D_t$ and $E_1=0$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \|E_t\|_2\leq\sum_{j=2}^t\beta_2^{t-j}\frac{\Delta_j}{2K}.
 \label{eq:rfd-history-error}
\end{equation}
Older compression errors decay along with older measurements. Because
the fresh rows enter before compression, the current metric satisfies
$G_t-G_t^\star=\beta_2E_{t-1}$. The bounds quantify the fidelity of
both metrics to the discounted measured-and-synthetic response
history, with current selection retaining the full fresh responses.

\subsection{Fixed-norm joint allocation}
\label{sec:transaction}
\label{app:transaction-proof}

Coordination changes the strengths of the paired directions while
preserving their combined norm at the current step. Define
$\omega_i=\frob{q_i^A}^2+\frob{q_i^B}^2$ and
$\mathcal E_0=\sum_i\omega_i$. Since the groups occupy disjoint
parameter blocks, the structured feed-forward update has norm
$\|\Delta\theta(c)\|_2^2=\eta^2\sum_i\omega_ic_i^2$.
The constraint $\sum_i\omega_ic_i^2=\mathcal E_0$ therefore matches
the current all-ones allocation before weight decay. Under this
constraint, the model in equation~\ref{eq:overview-coordinate-model}
balances current descent against recorded squared responses and their
cross term with decay. The latter depend on the directions at the
times they were recorded. Signed coefficients permit the model to
assess their combined action across groups and layers.

For $\omega_i>0$, set $W=\operatorname{Diag}(\omega)$ and
$y=W^{1/2}c$. Dividing $F_t$ by $\eta^2$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal Q_t(y)=\tfrac12y^\top M_ty-b_t^\top y,\qquad
 \|y\|_2^2=\mathcal E_0,\\
 M_t=W^{-1/2}G_tW^{-1/2},\qquad
 b_t=W^{-1/2}(s_t/\eta-r_t).
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:whitened-transaction}
\end{equation}
This change of coordinates turns the weighted constraint into a
sphere. Its stationarity equation is $(M_t+\lambda I)y=b_t$.
Since $M_t$ is positive semidefinite, choosing $\lambda>0$ makes
the system positive definite and the solution norm monotone in
$\lambda$. TILLER uses this positive branch to construct a proposal.
The solve requires finite inputs, positive group energies, a positive
learning rate, and $b_t\ne0$; Appendix~\ref{app:coefficient-numerics}
specifies the numerical checks on this domain.

A reorthogonalized Lanczos construction starts from $b_t$ and repeated
metric actions \citep{lanczos1950iteration}. Orthogonal restarts can
extend the basis when a block closes, giving $Q_k$ with
$k\leq\min\{32,C\}$ columns. The metric acts through its factors:
\begin{equation}
 M_tv=W^{-1/2}\mathcal A_t^\top
 (\mathcal A_tW^{-1/2}v)/K+\beta_2\tau_{t-1}W^{-1}v.
 \label{eq:ledger-metric-action}
\end{equation}
This evaluates the quadratic model without forming a dense
$C\times C$ matrix. Let $T_k^+$ be the positive-semidefinite
projection of the symmetric matrix accumulated by Lanczos. For
$y=Q_kz$, diagonalize
$T_k^+=U\operatorname{Diag}(\theta)U^\top$ and set
$\gamma=U^\top Q_k^\top b_t$. The projected stationarity equation
and norm constraint reduce to
\begin{equation}
 z=U\operatorname{Diag}((\theta+\lambda)^{-1})\gamma,\qquad
 \psi(\lambda)=\sum_j\frac{\gamma_j^2}{(\theta_j+\lambda)^2}
 =\mathcal E_0.
 \label{eq:secular-equation}
\end{equation}
For nonzero $\gamma$ and $\lambda>0$,
$\psi'(\lambda)=-2\sum_j\gamma_j^2/(\theta_j+\lambda)^3<0$,
so there is at most one positive root. When the numerical bracket
contains this root, bisection gives the proposal
$c=W^{-1/2}Q_kz$. Otherwise, the method retains $c=\boldsymbol1$.
Appendix~\ref{app:coefficient-numerics} specifies the bracket and
termination settings.

The proposal is evaluated in the full $C$-dimensional model $F_t$.
It is accepted only if it is finite, strictly improves on
$F_t(\boldsymbol1)$, and satisfies the prescribed norm tolerance.
For every layer and each matrix role, acceptance also requires
positive gradient and momentum contractions:
\begin{equation}
 \sum_g c_{\ell g}\inner{g_{\ell g}^r}{q_{\ell g}^r}>0,\qquad
 \sum_g c_{\ell g}\inner{m_{\ell g}^r}{q_{\ell g}^r}>0,
 \qquad r\in\{A,B\}.
 \label{eq:transaction-contractions}
\end{equation}
These contractions must be positive for both the proposal and
$\boldsymbol1$. If an acceptance condition fails, the method retains
the uncoordinated allocation. The improvement test uses $F_t$ with the
current metric $G_t$; the projected model constructs the proposal.

\section{Training algorithms and numerical specification}
\label{app:implementation}
\label{app:algorithm}
\label{app:numerical}

Full-horizon and staged training share the same TILLER updates through
a chosen step $T_s$. Setting $T_s=T$ applies these updates throughout
the training horizon. For $T_s<T$, the procedure takes two additional
inputs: a continuation update rule $\mathcal U$ for the feed-forward
and attention matrices, and its state-initialization map $\mathcal I$.
The map specifies the initial continuation state from the learned
parameters, the TILLER state, and $T_s$, including moment buffers,
internal step counts, and any required matrix grouping. This pair
defines the staged member of the training family.

Table~\ref{tab:algorithm} gives the common procedure. The transition
follows TILLER update $T_s$, so update $T_s+1$ is the first application
of $\mathcal U$. The model parameters and global learning-rate schedule
continue across this boundary. AdamW moments and step counts also
continue for the rational coefficients and other tensors assigned to
AdamW. Thus GRAIN keeps learning after the matrix update rule changes,
while the TILLER response measurements cease.

Alongside the matrix moment buffers $(v,R,C)$ and AdamW state,
TILLER stores the initial rational coefficients, the latest
participation and congruence summaries, the measured RMS row norm
$\nu$, and the ledger state $(\mathcal B,\tau,r)$. Matrix moments
are updated and directions and gradient contractions are formed
every step. Thus cached response geometry guides current updates
rather than reusing an earlier matrix direction.

\subsection{Activation initialization and matrix directions}

Every group begins with the same rational approximation to SiLU on
$[-5,5]$. In ascending powers, its coefficients are
\begin{align}
 (a_0,\ldots,a_5)={}&(2.4915714620520875\!\times\!10^{-5},\
 0.5000668663485823,\ 0.2499441908995771,\nonumber\\
 &0.0526200910151016,\ 0.005525765640115002,\
 0.00024199321178747251),\label{eq:initial-numerator}\\
 (b_1,\ldots,b_4)={}&(0.00022554017910995938,\
 0.10511420350691994,\nonumber\\
 &2.8656992394109812\!\times\!10^{-5},\
 0.0004816962110562025).\label{eq:initial-denominator}
\end{align}
The ten coefficients subsequently learn independently in each group.
The denominator in equation~\ref{eq:overview-rational} remains at
least one for finite coefficients. We use group width $w=256$ and
$\epsilon_{\rm act}=10^{-6}$. If $H$ is the SwiGLU intermediate
width, GRAIN uses $Gw=3H/2$: its two projections contain
$2d(3H/2)=3dH$ dense weights, matching the three SwiGLU projections.

The polar direction is approximated by five Newton--Schulz iterations.
For each matrix block, a tall input is transposed so that the Gram
matrix uses the smaller dimension. The working matrix is cast to
bfloat16 and divided by its Frobenius norm, accumulated in float32,
lower bounded by $10^{-7}$, and cast to bfloat16. With
$(a,b,c)=(3.4445,-4.775,2.0315)$, the iteration is
\begin{equation}
 X_{j+1}=aX_j+b(X_jX_j^\top)X_j+c(X_jX_j^\top)^2X_j.
 \label{eq:newton-schulz-map}
\end{equation}
The original orientation is restored after the fifth iteration.
Factorized second moments are maintained in float32, with
$\beta_1=\beta_2=0.95$ and $\epsilon_{\rm opt}=10^{-8}$ as in
Appendix~\ref{sec:directions}.

The feed-forward polar maps act on individual group blocks and use
the calibration in equation~\ref{eq:group-polar-calibration}.
For attention, each of Q, K, and V is divided into four contiguous
row blocks, and the output projection into four contiguous column
blocks. Each block spans $d/4$ features. The stacked QKV parent is
scaled by $1/\sqrt3$ to account for its three projections.
Factorized moments and the tangent rotation use each whole attention
matrix. After selection, a matrix of shape $n\times m$ receives the
Muon scale $0.2\sqrt{\max\{n,m\}}$.

The rotation guard evaluates the geometry at the parent scale before
the final Muon multiplier. It requires finite inputs, finite evaluated
coefficients and candidate contraction, and strictly positive parent
and adaptive squared norms. The tangent squared norm is computed as
\begin{equation}
 d_\perp=\max\!\left\{
 \frob{a_i}^2-
 \frac{\inner{a_i}{p_i}^2}{\max\{\frob{p_i}^2,\delta_{\rm fp}\}},0
 \right\},\qquad
 \delta_{\rm fp}=\operatorname{finfo}(\texttt{float32}).\operatorname{tiny}.
 \label{eq:numerical-tangent-norm}
\end{equation}
The candidate requires $d_\perp>\delta_{\rm fp}$; projection and
tangent normalization use the same denominator floor. It is selected
only when both its gradient contraction and the parent contraction
are positive. For finite parent and adaptive directions, a failed
guard selects the parent by setting its combination coefficient to
one and the adaptive coefficient to zero. The resulting direction
must pass a finite-value check before it is applied.

\subsection{Probe selection and response conventions}
\label{app:probe-selection}

Global gradient clipping uses threshold one. If $g_{\rm raw}$ is
the accumulated, distributed-averaged gradient before clipping,
the probe cotangents use the same realized factor
$\chi_{\rm clip}=\min\{1,(\|g_{\rm raw}\|_2+10^{-6})^{-1}\}$
in equation~\ref{eq:cotangent-rescaling}.

The $K=32$ probes are selected deterministically from flattened
example--token positions. With $P$ distributed processes, process
$p\in\{0,\ldots,P-1\}$ contributes
$K_p=\lfloor(p+1)K/P\rfloor-\lfloor pK/P\rfloor$ probes.
For $M=4$ accumulated microbatches, it initially captures
$m_p=\lceil K_p/M\rceil$ positions from each microbatch. To select
$m>1$ positions from $N$ rows, it uses zero-based indices
$\lfloor j(N-1)/(m-1)\rfloor$ for $j=0,\ldots,m-1$; a single
selected position uses index zero, and zero selected positions give
an empty set. The same rule selects $K_p$ rows from the concatenated
$Mm_p$ captured rows. Inputs, preactivations, features, and output
derivatives use the same indices in every layer. Gathering these
rows supplies the shared $K$-position response matrix.

Participation and congruence use conventions that keep their rotation
fractions defined at zero responses. At an individual position,
a zero Jacobian or feature vector has participation zero. After
loss-sensitivity weighting within a group, if either matrix role
has zero total sensitivity, both group participation values are set
to one. For group congruence, if either role has a zero denominator,
both values are set to one; equality of the rational coefficients
to their initialization also sets both values to one. The congruence
convention takes precedence in the product
$\pi_i^r(1-(\kappa_i^r)^2)$: an undefined comparison then gives zero
departure and retains the parent orientation. Attention forms its
congruence after summing the kernel statistics across groups and uses
the same unit-congruence convention if either aggregate denominator
vanishes. For the synthetic rows, a score norm no larger than
$\operatorname{finfo}(\texttt{float64}).\operatorname{tiny}$ is treated
as zero in equation~\ref{eq:trace-matched-rows}.

\subsection{Coefficient-solver numerics}
\label{app:coefficient-numerics}

Response factors and the persistent ledger are stored in float32;
the coefficient solve and its acceptance tests use double precision.
The implementation requires $C\geq2$, finite response and contraction
inputs, nonnegative isotropic residuals, $\omega_i>0$ for every
group, $\eta>0$, and $b_t\ne0$. Invalid inputs, including zero
group energies or a zero right-hand side, raise an error. The
all-ones fallback in Appendix~\ref{sec:transaction} applies after
these input checks and a valid basis construction.

Lanczos uses $k=\min\{32,C\}$ basis vectors and two
reorthogonalization passes against the accumulated basis at each
step. Let $v_j$ be its $j$th basis vector. A residual norm no greater
than $64\epsilon_{\rm mach}\max\{\|M_tv_j\|_2,1\}$ triggers an
attempted restart. The seed has coordinates
$[u_j]_a=\cos(aj)$ for $a=1,\ldots,C$ and $j$ starting at one.
After two reorthogonalization passes, a seed with norm above the
same threshold is normalized and starts a new block; its coupling
to the preceding block is set to zero. If the seed is unusable, the
routine uses the residual divided by its norm floored at
$\operatorname{finfo}(\texttt{float64}).\operatorname{tiny}$, then
checks the resulting basis. It requires finite basis and projected entries and
$\|Q_k^\top Q_k-I\|_{\rm F}\leq2\!\times\!10^{-10}k$;
failure raises an error.

The accumulated symmetric Lanczos matrix is eigendecomposed and its
negative eigenvalues are clipped to zero. This enforces the
positive-semidefinite projected model used by the positive-multiplier
solve. With double-precision machine epsilon $\epsilon_{\rm mach}$,
the multiplier bracket is
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_{\rm lo}=\epsilon_{\rm mach}C,\qquad
 \lambda_{\rm hi}=\|b_t\|_2/\sqrt{\mathcal E_0}
                  +\lambda_{\max}(T_k^+)+\lambda_{\rm lo}.
 \label{eq:multiplier-bracket}
\end{equation}
Nonnegative projected eigenvalues and orthonormality of $Q_k$ give
$\psi(\lambda_{\rm hi})\leq\|b_t\|_2^2/\lambda_{\rm hi}^2
\leq\mathcal E_0$. If $\psi(\lambda_{\rm lo})\geq\mathcal E_0$,
64 bisection iterations give a proposal; otherwise the unit
allocation is retained. The norm check is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{|c^\top Wc-\mathcal E_0|}{\max\{\mathcal E_0,1\}}\leq10^{-8}.
 \label{eq:coefficient-norm-check}
\end{equation}
Acceptance uses the original factor representation of $G_t$ and
the current gradient and momentum contractions. The norm tolerance
applies to the double-precision proposal. Selected coefficients
are then cast to float32 and multiply the float32 matrix directions;
the resulting updates are cast to the parameter dtype.
