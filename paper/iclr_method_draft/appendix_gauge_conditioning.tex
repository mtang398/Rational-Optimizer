\section{Gauge Conditioning of MatrixPolicy}
\label{app:gauge-conditioning}

This appendix isolates a narrow mathematical property of the RLB parameterization and the
MatrixPolicy rebalance step.  The result should not be read as a dominance theorem for
MatrixPolicy over AdamW.  Such a theorem would be false without restrictive assumptions on
the objective, initialization, hyperparameters, stochastic gradients, and implementation
state.  The claim established here is instead more limited: RLB blocks contain a positive
scale gauge, and standard coordinatewise adaptive updates are not equivariant with respect
to this gauge.  The MatrixPolicy rebalance step removes part of this representative
dependence by moving the matrices to a controlled point on the same gauge orbit.

\subsection{RLB gauge}
\label{app:gauge-conditioning:rlb-gauge}

For one rational feed-forward layer, write
\begin{align}
    z_l &= A_l x_l, & z_{l,g} &= \operatorname{group}_g(z_l), \\
    r_{l,g} &= \sqrt{m^{-1}\|z_{l,g}\|_2^2+\epsilon_{\rm rms}},
    & u_{l,g} &= z_{l,g}/r_{l,g}, \\
    h_{l,g} &= r_{l,g}R_{l,g}(u_{l,g}),
    & y_l &= B_l\operatorname{concat}_{g=1}^G h_{l,g}.
\end{align}
Here $A_l$ selects the domains presented to the rational maps, while $B_l$ recombines the
resulting rational features.  Let $B_{l,g}$ denote the block of columns of $B_l$
corresponding to group $g$.  For $a_g>0$, define the groupwise transformation
\begin{equation}
    A_{l,g}\mapsto a_g A_{l,g},
    \qquad
    B_{l,g}\mapsto a_g^{-1}B_{l,g}.
    \label{eq:app-positive-gauge}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, with $s_g=\log a_g$,
\begin{equation}
    A_{l,g}\mapsto e^{s_g} A_{l,g},
    \qquad
    B_{l,g}\mapsto e^{-s_g} B_{l,g}.
    \label{eq:app-log-gauge}
\end{equation}
We write this transformation as $T_s(A_l,B_l)$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact gauge in the homogeneous block]
\label{lem:app-exact-gauge}
If $\epsilon_{\rm rms}=0$, then for any $s\in\mathbb{R}^G$,
\begin{equation}
    f_{T_s(A_l,B_l)}(x_l)=f_{(A_l,B_l)}(x_l).
\end{equation}
Consequently, any loss that depends on $(A_l,B_l)$ only through the represented network
function is constant on the orbit $\{T_s(A_l,B_l):s\in\mathbb{R}^G\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
It suffices to consider one group and write $a=a_g$.  If
$A_{l,g}'=aA_{l,g}$, then $z_{l,g}'=az_{l,g}$.  When
$\epsilon_{\rm rms}=0$, the group radius is homogeneous, so
$r_{l,g}'=ar_{l,g}$.  Therefore $u_{l,g}'=z_{l,g}'/r_{l,g}'=u_{l,g}$ and
$h_{l,g}'=r_{l,g}'R_{l,g}(u_{l,g}')=a h_{l,g}$.  The corresponding block of
$B_l$ is scaled by $a^{-1}$, hence
\begin{equation}
    B_{l,g}'h_{l,g}'=(a^{-1}B_{l,g})(ah_{l,g})=B_{l,g}h_{l,g}.
\end{equation}
The equality holds groupwise and therefore for the full layer output.
\end{proof}

The implementation uses a nonzero additive stabilizer $\epsilon_{\rm rms}$.  The exact
symmetry above is then replaced by a local approximate symmetry.  This distinction is
minor numerically but important mathematically.

\begin{assumption}[Local nondegeneracy]
\label{ass:app-nondegenerate}
On the set of activations considered, there exist constants $0<\sigma<M<\infty$ and
$S<\infty$ such that, for all groups,
\begin{equation}
    \sigma^2 \leq m^{-1}\|z_{l,g}\|_2^2 \leq M^2,
    \qquad
    |s_g|\leq S.
\end{equation}
Assume also that the rational maps $R_{l,g}$ are bounded and Lipschitz on the corresponding
compact set of normalized inputs, and that the relevant blocks of $B_l$ have bounded
operator norm.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Approximate gauge with an RMS floor]
\label{prop:app-approx-gauge}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:app-nondegenerate}, there exists a constant $C$ independent of
$\epsilon_{\rm rms}$ such that
\begin{equation}
    \|f_{T_s(A_l,B_l)}(x_l)-f_{(A_l,B_l)}(x_l)\|_2
    \leq C\epsilon_{\rm rms}.
    \label{eq:app-approx-gauge-bound}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for every $\delta>0$ there exists
$\epsilon_0>0$ such that $\epsilon_{\rm rms}<\epsilon_0$ implies
\begin{equation}
    \|f_{T_s(A_l,B_l)}(x_l)-f_{(A_l,B_l)}(x_l)\|_2<\delta.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $S_z=m^{-1}\|z\|_2^2$ and $a=e^s$.  The radius satisfies
\begin{align}
&\left|\sqrt{a^2S_z+\epsilon_{\rm rms}}
-a\sqrt{S_z+\epsilon_{\rm rms}}\right| \\
&\qquad =
\frac{|1-a^2|\epsilon_{\rm rms}}
{\sqrt{a^2S_z+\epsilon_{\rm rms}}+a\sqrt{S_z+\epsilon_{\rm rms}}}
\leq
\frac{|1-a^2|}{2a\sigma}\epsilon_{\rm rms}.
\end{align}
The same bound, together with $S_z\in[\sigma^2,M^2]$, implies that the normalized inputs
$u(z)$ and $u(az)$ differ by $O(\epsilon_{\rm rms})$ uniformly over the compact region.
Since $R_{l,g}$ is Lipschitz and the relevant blocks of $B_l$ are bounded, the group output
after applying $B_{l,g}$ differs from the original group output by
$O(\epsilon_{\rm rms})$.  Summing over finitely many groups gives
\eqref{eq:app-approx-gauge-bound}.  The $\delta$--$\epsilon$ statement follows by taking
$\epsilon_0=\delta/C$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Gauge directions are not function directions}
\label{app:gauge-conditioning:tangent}

Assume first the homogeneous case $\epsilon_{\rm rms}=0$.  Since the loss is constant along
$T_s$, differentiating at $s_g=0$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d}{ds_g}L\bigl(e^{s_g}A_{l,g},e^{-s_g}B_{l,g}\bigr)\bigg|_{s_g=0}
    =0.
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
    \left\langle \nabla_{A_{l,g}}L, A_{l,g}\right\rangle
    -
    \left\langle \nabla_{B_{l,g}}L, B_{l,g}\right\rangle
    =0.
    \label{eq:app-gauge-tangent-orthogonality}
\end{equation}
Thus $(A_{l,g},-B_{l,g})$ is tangent to a direction that changes the parameterization but
not the represented function.  Equation~\eqref{eq:app-gauge-tangent-orthogonality} is the
basic obstruction for a coordinatewise optimizer: the Euclidean coordinates contain a
scale degree of freedom that is not a function-space degree of freedom.

\subsection{The MatrixPolicy rebalance as a canonicalization step}
\label{app:gauge-conditioning:matrixpolicy}

MatrixPolicy uses the gauge freedom to control the relative scale of $A_{l,g}$ and
$B_{l,g}$.  Define the observed log-ratio
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_g
    =
    \log \rms(A_{l,g})-
    \log \rms(B_{l,g}).
    \label{eq:app-gamma}
\end{equation}
Let $\tau_g$ be the target log-ratio computed by the optimizer from group-level statistics
such as gain and relative gradient pressure.  Ignoring clipping, the rebalance step is
\begin{equation}
    \ell_g=\frac{1}{2}(\tau_g-\gamma_g),
    \qquad
    A_{l,g}\leftarrow e^{\ell_g}A_{l,g},
    \qquad
    B_{l,g}\leftarrow e^{-\ell_g}B_{l,g}.
    \label{eq:app-mp-rebalance}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Targeting the log-ratio]
\label{lem:app-target-log-ratio}
If clipping is inactive, the rebalance step in \eqref{eq:app-mp-rebalance} maps
$\gamma_g$ exactly to $\tau_g$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After the rebalance,
\begin{align}
    \gamma_g'
    &=\log \rms(e^{\ell_g}A_{l,g})-
      \log \rms(e^{-\ell_g}B_{l,g}) \\
    &=\gamma_g+2\ell_g
     =\gamma_g+\tau_g-\gamma_g
     =\tau_g.
\end{align}
\end{proof}

The clipped implementation is a bounded version of the same operation.  Hence the
rebalance should be interpreted as a local canonicalization of the gauge representative:
it changes the coordinates used by the optimizer, while preserving the represented
function exactly in Lemma~\ref{lem:app-exact-gauge} and up to
$O(\epsilon_{\rm rms})$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:app-approx-gauge}.

\subsection{Coordinatewise adaptive updates are not gauge-equivariant}
\label{app:gauge-conditioning:adamw}

Let $\Phi$ denote one optimizer step applied to $(A,B)$ and its optimizer state.  A step is
gauge-equivariant if, for every $s$,
\begin{equation}
    \Phi(T_s(A,B))=T_s(\Phi(A,B)).
    \label{eq:app-equivariance}
\end{equation}
Gauge equivariance is a natural property here because $T_s(A,B)$ represents the same
function as $(A,B)$ in the homogeneous block.  If \eqref{eq:app-equivariance} fails, then
the optimizer's next function-space move depends on an arbitrary scale representative.

For clarity, consider a single group and suppress layer and group indices.  Write the
AdamW matrix update in the simplified form
\begin{equation}
    M^+=(1-\eta\lambda)M
    -\eta\frac{m_M}{\sqrt{v_M}+\epsilon_{\rm opt}},
    \label{eq:app-adamw}
\end{equation}
where $m_M$ and $v_M$ are the first- and second-moment estimates and division is
coordinatewise.  Consider the log-gauge transform $A_s=e^sA$ and $B_s=e^{-s}B$.  Even if
we give AdamW the ideally transformed moment state,
\begin{equation}
    m_{A,s}=e^{-s}m_A,
    \qquad
    v_{A,s}=e^{-2s}v_A,
    \label{eq:app-moment-transform-a}
\end{equation}
with the analogous transformation for $B$, the update does not commute with the gauge.
Indeed, for $\epsilon_{\rm opt}=0$,
\begin{equation}
    A_s^+-e^sA^+
    =
    \eta(e^s-1)\frac{m_A}{\sqrt{v_A}},
    \label{eq:app-adamw-non-equivariant-a}
\end{equation}
which is nonzero for $s\neq0$ whenever $m_A\neq0$.  Similarly,
\begin{equation}
    B_s^+-e^{-s}B^+
    =
    \eta(e^{-s}-1)\frac{m_B}{\sqrt{v_B}}.
    \label{eq:app-adamw-non-equivariant-b}
\end{equation}
Thus a coordinatewise adaptive step is not an update on the quotient space induced by the
RLB gauge.  The mismatch remains generically nonzero for
$\epsilon_{\rm opt}>0$.

\subsection{A scalar separation illustrating gauge regret}
\label{app:gauge-conditioning:separation}

The preceding non-equivariance can be made concrete in a one-dimensional example.  This
example is not intended to model the full MatrixPolicy implementation.  It isolates the
effect of using a canonical representative before a coordinatewise adaptive update.

Let
\begin{equation}
    f(a,b)=ab,
    \qquad
    L(a,b)=\frac{1}{2}(ab-y)^2,
\end{equation}
with $a,b>0$, $w=ab>y>0$, and residual $\rho=w-y$.  The gauge action is
\begin{equation}
    (a,b)\mapsto(e^s a,e^{-s}b),
\end{equation}
which preserves $ab$.  Define the balanced representative
\begin{equation}
    C(w)=(\sqrt{w},\sqrt{w}).
\end{equation}
Starting from $C(w)$, the first Adam-style normalized coordinate step with
$\lambda=0$ and $\epsilon_{\rm opt}=0$ has
\begin{equation}
    a^+=a-\eta,\qquad b^+=b-\eta,
\end{equation}
because $\partial L/\partial a=\rho b>0$ and
$\partial L/\partial b=\rho a>0$.  The balanced representative gives
\begin{equation}
    w_{\rm bal}^+=(\sqrt{w}-\eta)^2
    =w-2\eta\sqrt{w}+\eta^2,
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}
    L_{\rm bal}^+
    =\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho-2\eta\sqrt{w}+\eta^2\right)^2.
    \label{eq:app-balanced-loss}
\end{equation}
In contrast, the gauge-equivalent representative
\begin{equation}
    (a_s,b_s)=(e^s\sqrt{w},e^{-s}\sqrt{w})
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{align}
    w_s^+
    &=(e^s\sqrt{w}-\eta)(e^{-s}\sqrt{w}-\eta) \\
    &=w-\eta\sqrt{w}(e^s+e^{-s})+\eta^2 \\
    &=w-2\eta\sqrt{w}\cosh(s)+\eta^2,
\end{align}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
    L_s^+
    =\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho-2\eta\sqrt{w}\cosh(s)+\eta^2\right)^2.
    \label{eq:app-imbalanced-loss}
\end{equation}
Since $\cosh(s)$ is unbounded, for every $\eta>0$ there exist gauge-equivalent
representatives such that
\begin{equation}
    L_s^+>L_{\rm bal}^+.
\end{equation}
One sufficient condition is
\begin{equation}
    \cosh(s)
    >
    \frac{\rho+\eta^2+|\rho-2\eta\sqrt{w}+\eta^2|}{2\eta\sqrt{w}}.
    \label{eq:app-separation-condition}
\end{equation}
Thus the same function value can lead to a strictly worse one-step loss solely because the
coordinatewise adaptive update was applied to a less balanced gauge representative.

\begin{remark}[Interpretation of the toy example]
The scalar construction should be viewed as a gauge-regret example, not as a faithful
model of language-model training.  Its role is to show that representative dependence is
not merely a formal inconvenience: it can change the one-step loss.  MatrixPolicy's
rebalance step is designed to reduce this source of variation in the RLB matrices before
subsequent updates accumulate optimizer state.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Robustness of the separation}
\label{app:gauge-conditioning:robustness}

The scalar separation was written in the clean limit
$\lambda=\epsilon_{\rm opt}=\epsilon_{\rm rms}=0$.  The strict inequality persists under
small perturbations.

\begin{corollary}[Local $\delta$--$\epsilon$ robustness]
\label{cor:app-delta-epsilon}
Let
\begin{equation}
    \Delta(s,\eta,\lambda,\epsilon_{\rm opt},\epsilon_{\rm rms})
    =L_s^+-L_{\rm bal}^+
\end{equation}
denote the one-step gap in the scalar construction, restricted to a compact nondegenerate
region where optimizer denominators are bounded away from zero.  Suppose that, for some
$s_0$,
\begin{equation}
    \Delta(s_0,\eta,0,0,0)=m>0.
\end{equation}
Then, for every $\epsilon_{\rm gap}\in(0,m)$, there exists $\delta>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
    |\lambda|+\epsilon_{\rm opt}+\epsilon_{\rm rms}<\delta
\end{equation}
implies
\begin{equation}
    \Delta(s_0,\eta,\lambda,\epsilon_{\rm opt},\epsilon_{\rm rms})
    >m-\epsilon_{\rm gap}>0.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
On the stated compact region, the one-step maps and the loss are continuous functions of
$(\lambda,\epsilon_{\rm opt},\epsilon_{\rm rms})$.  Since the unperturbed gap is positive,
continuity gives a neighborhood of $(0,0,0)$ on which the change in the gap is smaller
than any prescribed $\epsilon_{\rm gap}\in(0,m)$.  This proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Scope}
\label{app:gauge-conditioning:scope}

The analysis above supports a design motivation rather than a complete optimization
proof.  It establishes that RLB contains a scale gauge, that MatrixPolicy's rebalance step
acts as a bounded canonicalization of that gauge, and that AdamW-style coordinatewise
adaptive steps are not invariant to the resulting equivalence relation.  It does not prove
that MatrixPolicy converges faster, reaches a lower loss, or dominates AdamW for arbitrary
training runs.  The empirical comparison in the main text must therefore remain the basis
for claims about validation loss, while this appendix explains one structural reason that
an RLB-specific matrix policy can behave differently from a generic optimizer.
