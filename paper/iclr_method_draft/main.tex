\begin{abstract}
Rational feed-forward blocks introduce useful nonlinear structure, but they also
change the geometry seen by an optimizer.  We study Rational Local Basis (RLB)
feed-forward layers in Transformer language models and introduce Rational Matrix
Policy Optimization, an optimizer that treats the input and output matrices around
each rational block as role-specific objects rather than generic dense weights.
The method combines a standard base optimizer for the rest of the model with
role-depth scaling, on-policy group statistics, and a bounded gauge rebalance for
the RLB matrices.  A preliminary three-seed real-corpus screen on FineWeb and
FineWeb-Edu shows about a 0.15 validation-loss improvement over same-protocol
SiLU+AdamW and the strongest non-MatrixPolicy control in that screen.  These
results motivate a full optimizer benchmark with tuned baselines, speed-to-target
curves, stability accounting, and scale/transfer tests.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Most language-model optimizer studies treat the feed-forward block as a generic
collection of matrices.  That is appropriate for standard elementwise activations,
but it misses structure when the feed-forward layer is itself a learned rational
map.  In an RLB block, one matrix chooses the normalized domains presented to
rational groups, the rational coefficients shape each group response, and a second
matrix recombines the resulting features into the residual stream.  These roles
create optimizer-visible structure that is not captured by a single scalar
learning-rate rule.

This paper studies the following claim: rational feed-forward networks expose
geometry that a block-aware optimizer can exploit, and an optimizer that uses that
geometry can train rational language models more robustly than generic optimizers
under the same base protocol.  The method is Rational Matrix Policy Optimization
(MatrixPolicy).  It keeps the surrounding Transformer training recipe unchanged
and modifies only the optimization of matrices adjacent to RLB groups.  The
central mechanism is not that rational activations are intrinsically better than
SiLU; it is that the rational parameterization gives the optimizer additional
structure, including a positive scale gauge and group-level pressure signals.

This work makes three contributions.  First, it defines the RLB matrix
roles and the MatrixPolicy update used in our language-model experiments.  Second,
it reports a preliminary but replicated real-corpus screen on FineWeb and
FineWeb-Edu, including divergent plain-RLB controls rather than hiding them.
Third, Appendix~\ref{app:gauge-conditioning} gives a narrow theoretical
motivation: RLB blocks have a scale gauge, coordinatewise adaptive updates are not
equivariant to this gauge, and MatrixPolicy's rebalance step acts as a bounded
canonicalization of the representative.  The appendix is deliberately not a
convergence theorem; the empirical benchmark remains the basis for claims about
validation loss.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Adam-style adaptive methods normalize coordinatewise gradient moments and remain
the default optimizer family for many Transformer language-model runs
\citep{kingma2015iclr-adam}.  Recent optimizer papers have raised the evidence
standard for new optimizers: fair tuning over baseline families, throughput and
memory accounting, speed-to-target curves, stability reporting, and transfer
beyond the selection setting are now expected.  MatrixPolicy should be judged
against that standard, not against a single default learning rate.

Rational feed-forward layers are a different axis from generic optimizer design.
They replace a fixed elementwise nonlinearity with learned rational maps applied
to normalized groups.  This gives the optimizer access to quantities that do not
exist in a SiLU FFN: group radii, rational-coefficient pressures, input/output
matrix roles, and a positive gauge relating the adjacent matrices.  MatrixPolicy
is therefore a structure-specific optimizer.  It is complementary to broad
optimizer families such as AdamW, Muon-style matrix updates, schedule-free
methods, factored adaptive methods, and matrix-preconditioned methods; the final
paper benchmark must tune and compare against those families directly.

\section{Rational Matrix Policy Optimization}
\label{sec:method}

Let $\mathcal{L}(\theta)$ be the language-model training objective.  For layer
$l$, the RLB block projects the residual-stream input $x_l\in\mathbb{R}^d$ into
$G$ groups of width $m$,
\begin{align}
    z_l=A_lx_l, \qquad z_{l,g}=\operatorname{group}_g(z_l),
\end{align}
normalizes each group by its RMS radius,
\begin{align}
    r_{l,g}=\sqrt{m^{-1}\lVert z_{l,g}\rVert_2^2+\epsilon_{\rm rms}},
    \qquad u_{l,g}=z_{l,g}/r_{l,g},
\end{align}
and applies a learned rational map in normalized coordinates,
\begin{align}
    h_{l,g}=r_{l,g}R_{l,g}(u_{l,g}),
    \qquad
    y_l=B_l\operatorname{concat}_{g=1}^G h_{l,g}.
\end{align}
The input matrix $A_l$ selects rational domains, the coefficients of $R_{l,g}$
shape the group response, and the output matrix $B_l$ recombines rational
features.  MatrixPolicy uses this separation by partitioning parameters as
\begin{align}
    \theta=\theta_{\mathrm{base}}\cup\theta_{\mathrm{coeff}}
    \cup\{A_l,B_l\}_{l=1}^L.
\end{align}
The base parameters use the same optimizer as the non-rational baseline.  Rational
coefficients use a coefficient rule.  MatrixPolicy is applied only to $A_l$ and
$B_l$.

In the homogeneous case $\epsilon_{\rm rms}=0$, each group has a positive scale
gauge:
\begin{align}
    A_{l,g}\mapsto a_gA_{l,g},\qquad
    B_{l,g}\mapsto a_g^{-1}B_{l,g},\qquad a_g>0.
\end{align}
The represented function is unchanged because $z_{l,g}$ and $r_{l,g}$ scale
together, $u_{l,g}$ is unchanged, and the inverse scale in $B_l$ cancels the scale
in $h_{l,g}$.  With a nonzero RMS floor, this symmetry is approximate but remains
locally controlled under mild nondegeneracy assumptions
(Appendix~\ref{app:gauge-conditioning}).  This matters because optimizer state is
stored in coordinates, not in the quotient space of represented functions.

At step $t$, each matrix role $r\in\{\mathrm{in},\mathrm{out}\}$ receives a
role-depth factor $\rho_r(l)$ and an on-policy statistic factor
$s_{\mathrm{stat}}(l,r,t)$.  The matrix update is
\begin{align}
    M_{l,r}^{t+1}=M_{l,r}^{t}
    &+\Delta_{\mathrm{adapt}}\!\left(M_{l,r};
      \eta_t a_{\mathrm{mat}}(l,r,t)[1-\mu(l,r,t)]\right) \nonumber\\
    &+\Delta_{\mathrm{mat}}\!\left(M_{l,r};
      \eta_t a_{\mathrm{matnorm}}\mu(l,r,t)\right),
\end{align}
where $M_{l,\mathrm{in}}=A_l$, $M_{l,\mathrm{out}}=B_l$, $\eta_t$ is the shared
base learning rate, $\Delta_{\mathrm{adapt}}$ is an Adam-style adaptive step, and
$\Delta_{\mathrm{mat}}$ is a short early matrix-normalized step.  The mixture
coefficient $\mu(l,r,t)$ is active only early in training.  After the matrix
update, MatrixPolicy computes a target log-ratio for each group and applies the
bounded rebalance
\begin{align}
    A_{l,g}\leftarrow e^{\ell_g}A_{l,g},\qquad
    B_{l,g}\leftarrow e^{-\ell_g}B_{l,g}.
\end{align}
The update changes the representative used by the optimizer while preserving, or
with $\epsilon_{\rm rms}>0$ approximately preserving, the represented block
function.  Full hyperparameter definitions are in Appendix~\ref{app:details}.

\begin{center}
\fbox{%
\begin{minipage}{0.92\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 1: Rational Matrix Policy step.}
Given gradients $\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta^t)$ and base learning rate $\eta_t$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Update exponential moving averages of rational-group pressures.
    \item Compute role-depth and group-stat multipliers for each RLB matrix.
    \item Update non-rational parameters with the base optimizer and update
    rational coefficients with the coefficient rule.
    \item Update each $A_l$ and $B_l$ with the MatrixPolicy mixture step.
    \item Apply the bounded positive-gauge rebalance to each rational group.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The current evidence is a same-protocol real-corpus screen rather than the final
ICLR benchmark.  All rows use a 123M-parameter Transformer, GPT-2 tokenizer,
100M-token training budget, the same validation slice, three seeds
$(1337,2027,3407)$, equal global batch size, shared base schedule, and common
evaluation cadence.  The screen compares standard SiLU+AdamW, plain RLB+AdamW,
RLB+Muon, and RLB+MatrixPolicy with group-stat scaling.  Failed or nonfinite
plain-RLB rows are counted in stability reporting.

These results are promising because the gap replicates across two web corpora and
because plain RLB+AdamW is not a sufficient explanation: it is marginal on
FineWeb and has seed-sensitive instability on FineWeb-Edu.  They do not yet constitute a
complete optimizer-paper result.  A submission-ready benchmark includes tuned
optimizer-family surfaces, mean-plus-standard-deviation curves, bootstrap or
paired confidence intervals, divergence accounting, speed-to-target and
throughput tables, academic-scale model/token scaling, transfer beyond the HPO
corpora, and mechanism diagnostics that measure whether MatrixPolicy improves
function-changing movement relative to gauge drift.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

MatrixPolicy is a structure-specific optimizer.  Its advantage, if it survives
the full benchmark, should be interpreted as evidence that RLB geometry can be
used productively by the optimizer, not as evidence that every rational activation
or every matrix policy dominates standard feed-forward training.  The gauge
analysis in Appendix~\ref{app:gauge-conditioning} explains why generic
coordinatewise adaptive updates can depend on an arbitrary RLB scale
representative, but it does not prove faster convergence or lower final loss.

The main limitations are empirical.  The current screen is not yet tuned against
the strongest optimizer families, does not include a larger model or longer-token
run, and does not yet report speed-to-target under final tuned configurations.
The current confidence intervals are based on only three seeds and should be
treated as stability checks.  A submission-ready version needs the full optimizer
benchmark described above before making final claims.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Rational feed-forward layers expose optimizer-visible structure that generic
coordinatewise updates do not explicitly use.  MatrixPolicy uses RLB matrix roles,
group pressures, and gauge rebalancing to optimize rational FFNs with a small,
localized change to the training recipe.  Preliminary three-seed real-corpus
results support the direction; the remaining work is to turn that evidence into a
fully tuned, speed-aware, and mechanism-grounded optimizer benchmark.

\section{MatrixPolicy Details}
\label{app:details}

\paragraph{Role-depth factors.}
For normalized layer depth $d_l=l/(L-1)$,
\begin{align}
    \rho_{\mathrm{in}}(l)&=\clip(1-0.50(d_l-0.5),0.55,1.40),\\
    \rho_{\mathrm{out}}(l)&=\clip(1+1.00(d_l-0.5),0.55,1.40),\\
    a_{\mathrm{role}}(l,r)&=\max(0.10,1+1.20(\rho_r(l)-1)).
\end{align}
The local adaptive scale is
\begin{align}
    a_{\mathrm{mat}}(l,r,t)=\clip(3.0\,a_{\mathrm{role}}(l,r)a_{\mathrm{stat}}(l,r,t),0.40,4.0).
\end{align}

\paragraph{On-policy group statistics.}
For group $g$, MatrixPolicy tracks relative gradient pressures
\begin{align}
    p_{\mathrm{in},g}=\frac{\rms(\nabla A_{l,g})}{\rms(A_{l,g})},\qquad
    p_{\mathrm{out},g}=\frac{\rms(\nabla B_{l,g})}{\rms(B_{l,g})},\qquad
    p_{\mathrm{rat},g}=\rms(\nabla\theta_{\mathrm{coeff},g}).
\end{align}
With EMAs $q_{\mathrm{in},g}$, $q_{\mathrm{out},g}$, and $q_{\mathrm{rat},g}$,
we define
\begin{align}
    \pi_g=\log q_{\mathrm{in},g}-\log q_{\mathrm{out},g},\qquad
    \alpha_g=\log q_{\mathrm{rat},g}-\frac{1}{2}(\log q_{\mathrm{in},g}+\log q_{\mathrm{out},g}).
\end{align}

\paragraph{Group-stat preconditioning.}
The group preconditioner is centered and clipped:
\begin{align}
    c_g=c_{\mathrm{gain},g}c_{\mathrm{pressure},g}c_{\mathrm{activity},g},
    \qquad c_g\leftarrow c_g/\geom(c),
    \qquad c_g\leftarrow\clip(c_g,0.75,1.35).
\end{align}
The factors are
\begin{align}
    c_{\mathrm{gain},g}=\left(\frac{\geom(k)}{k_g}\right)^{0.20},\qquad
    c_{\mathrm{pressure},g}=\exp(0.10\,d_{\mathrm{pressure},g}),
\end{align}
where $d_{\mathrm{pressure},g}=-\pi_g$ for $A_l$ and $+\pi_g$ for $B_l$. The
activity damping term is
\begin{align}
    c_{\mathrm{activity},g}=\exp[-0.20\,\relu((\alpha_g-0.05)/0.45)].
\end{align}

\paragraph{Gauge rebalance.}
Let
\begin{align}
    \gamma_g=\log\rms(A_{l,g})-\log\rms(B_{l,g}).
\end{align}
The target log-ratio is
\begin{align}
    \tau_g=\frac{1}{2}(\log d^{\mathrm{gain}}_g-\log o^{\mathrm{gain}}_g)
    +\beta_{\mathrm{pressure}}(\log q_{\mathrm{in},g}-\log q_{\mathrm{out},g}).
\end{align}
The applied correction is
\begin{align}
    \ell_g=\frac{1}{2}(\tau_g-\gamma_g),\qquad
    \ell_g\leftarrow\clip(s(t,l)a^{\mathrm{activity}}_g\ell_g,-\ell_{\max},\ell_{\max}).
\end{align}
The transform $A_{l,g}\leftarrow e^{\ell_g}A_{l,g}$ and
$B_{l,g}\leftarrow e^{-\ell_g}B_{l,g}$ preserves the homogeneous RLB function
exactly and the stabilized implementation up to the RMS-floor perturbation
described in Appendix~\ref{app:gauge-conditioning}.
