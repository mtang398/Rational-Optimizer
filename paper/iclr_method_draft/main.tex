\begin{abstract}
A useful LLM pretraining recipe moves the loss-time frontier by reaching the
same validation loss with fewer tokens or less wall-clock time.  We target this
objective through the Transformer nonlinear sublayer, treating it as an
optimizer-visible interface.  MatrixPolicy with Rational Latent Basis (RLB)
replaces a fixed scalar response with grouped rational responses that expose
local gain, coefficient-activity, and matrix-balance statistics to a role-aware
update.  MatrixPolicy uses these signals to update only the sublayer input and
output matrices, while rational coefficients and the rest of the Transformer
remain on AdamW.  Across DCLM, FineWeb-Edu, FineWeb, Dolma-sample, and C4 with a
fixed 12-layer, width-768 decoder, MatrixPolicy reaches matched validation-loss
targets with fewer tokens and lower measured arrival time than SiLU controls
under the same protocol.  It saves 19.5--22.8\% target-arrival tokens versus
SiLU with AdamW and 29.2--34.0\% versus SiLU with Muon at 100M tokens, and
22.3--26.4\% and 6.7--9.8\%, respectively, at 300M tokens.  [todo: Insert the
larger-scale training result here.]

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Recent LLM optimizer papers frame practical efficiency through loss-time and
compute-time frontiers, asking which training recipe reaches a target loss with
fewer tokens, FLOPs, or wall-clock time
\citep{shah2025practicalefficiency,liu2025muon,semenov2025benchmarking}.  This
criterion also exposes an activation-design question.  In a modern decoder
block, the nonlinear sublayer is a learned matrix--nonlinearity--matrix map.  The
activation sets local gain and curvature, while the adjacent matrices choose and
recombine the coordinates on which that response acts.  Standard training
pipelines leave this local structure implicit to the optimizer.  Activation
coefficients and adjacent matrices are updated as ordinary parameters, leaving
gain, coefficient-activity, and role information inside the sublayer unused by
the matrix update.

We make this interface concrete with Rational Latent Basis (RLB) and
MatrixPolicy.  RLB maps residual states into grouped latent coordinates,
normalizes each group by its RMS radius, and applies a trainable real-pole-free
P5/Q4 rational response.  The resulting response-gain, coefficient-activity,
matrix-pressure, and pair-balance signals are detached from the forward graph and
passed to MatrixPolicy.  MatrixPolicy uses them to update only the RLB input and
output matrices; attention, embeddings, normalization parameters, rational
coefficients, and ordinary Transformer tensors remain on AdamW.  This local
division lets the nonlinear sublayer expose the quantities it creates without
changing the optimizer used for the rest of the model.

We evaluate target arrival by measuring the tokens and wall-clock time required
to reach a fixed validation-loss target under the same model, corpus, and
protocol.
In the completed study, the same 12-layer, width-768 causal Transformer is
trained on DCLM, FineWeb-Edu, FineWeb, Dolma-sample, and C4.  The experiments
include RLB-only controls and matched SiLU with AdamW and SiLU with Muon
baselines under the same model, data, and measurement protocol.  MatrixPolicy
reaches shared validation-loss targets earlier at both 100M and 300M token
budgets.  [todo: Insert the larger-scale training result here.]  The contribution
is an activation design that exposes optimizer-visible matrix roles, a localized
role-aware optimizer for the exposed matrix pair, and matched evidence that this
interface reduces target-arrival tokens and wall-clock time across five
pretraining corpora.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Activation design for Transformer nonlinear sublayers has moved from fixed
pointwise curves to gated matrix sublayers.  Early Transformer-style sublayers
used fixed pointwise responses such as GELU or Swish/SiLU
\citep{vaswani2017attention,hendrycks2016gelu,ramachandran2017swish}.  GLU
introduced multiplicative feature selection for language modeling
\citep{dauphin2017language}, GLU variants such as SwiGLU improved Transformer
sublayers in compute-matched settings \citep{shazeer2020glu}, and large decoder
models adopted SwiGLU together with pre-normalized Transformer components
\citep{chowdhery2022palm,touvron2023llama}.  These works identify the nonlinear
block as the natural comparison point for RLB because the response is coupled to
the matrices that prepare and mix it.  RLB keeps that sublayer location while
replacing the fixed curve with grouped trainable rational responses.

Choosing a trainable rational response builds on earlier evidence that
low-degree quotients can serve as learnable nonlinearities.  Pad\'e Activation
Units learned such quotients end to end as activation functions
\citep{molina2020pade}, rational neural networks gave approximation efficiency
results for smooth-function classes \citep{boulle2020rational}, and RAFT
evaluated rational activation functions inside Transformer pretraining and
fine-tuning \citep{fang2023transformers}.  Recent theory strengthens the
motivation by proving expressivity advantages for trainable low-degree rational
activations over a broad set of modern fixed activations, including gated and
Transformer-style nonlinearities \citep{tang2026rationalexpressivity}.  RLB
therefore follows the rational-activation lineage; MatrixPolicy tests the
additional effect of making the surrounding matrices optimizer-visible.

Making the surrounding matrices optimizer-visible connects RLB to a fast-moving
LLM optimizer literature.  Adam and AdamW remain central baselines
\citep{kingma2015iclr-adam,loshchilov2019decoupled}, while state-efficient or
diagonal adaptive methods such as Adafactor and CAME reduce optimizer memory or
alter adaptive-state estimates \citep{shazeer2018adafactor,luo2023came}.  Other
recent methods change the update rule itself.  Lion, Schedule-Free methods, and
cautious optimizers alter momentum, sign, or schedule behavior
\citep{chen2023lion,defazio2024road,liang2026cautious}, while Sophia, GaLore,
and Adam-mini change curvature, low-rank, or per-block learning-rate structure
\citep{liu2024sophia,zhao2024galore,zhang2025adammini}.  Practical-efficiency
studies evaluate optimizers by the tokens, compute, or time needed to reach
matched losses
\citep{shah2025practicalefficiency,schlotthauer2025budget,semenov2025benchmarking,wen2026fantastic}.
The matrix restriction in MatrixPolicy connects most directly to matrix-aware
optimization.  Shampoo uses tensor preconditioners \citep{gupta2018shampoo},
SOAP stabilizes Shampoo-style preconditioning by running Adam in the
preconditioner eigenbasis \citep{vyas2025soap}, and Muon uses orthogonalized
matrix updates for LLM training \citep{liu2025muon}.  Recent optimizer
comparisons emphasize fair tuning, model-scale dependence, and
setting-dependent tradeoffs \citep{zhao2025deconstructing,wen2026fantastic}.
MatrixPolicy follows this matrix-aware direction, with updates restricted to the
RLB input/output matrix pair and conditioned on activation-derived sublayer
statistics.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

RLB and MatrixPolicy are designed as a single interface.  RLB defines the
nonlinear sublayer and the detached summaries it exposes; MatrixPolicy consumes
those summaries to update the two adjacent matrices.  Rational coefficients and
ordinary Transformer parameters remain on the AdamW path in the evaluated
configuration.

Throughout the method we use
\begin{align}
    \clip(x,a,b)&=\min\{\max\{x,a\},b\}, \\
    \smooth(a,b,x)&=3\omega^2-2\omega^3,
    \qquad
    \omega=\clip\left(\frac{x-a}{b-a},0,1\right).
\end{align}

\subsection{Rational Latent Basis}
\label{sec:method-rlb}

For layer $l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}$, let $x_l\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ be the input to the
nonlinear sublayer.  RLB uses an input matrix $A_l\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times d}$ and
an output matrix $B_l\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times H}$ with latent width $H=Gm$.  Let
$A_{l,g}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$ denote the row block of $A_l$, and let
$B_{l,g}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}$ denote the matching column block of $B_l$.
In the implementation, $A_l$ and $B_l$ are the bias-free
\texttt{in\_proj.weight} and \texttt{out\_proj.weight} of the nonlinear
sublayer.  The evaluated runs use an RMS floor
$\epsilon_{\rm rms}=10^{-6}$.
Each group has a trainable P5/Q4 rational response
\begin{align}
    P_{l,g}(u)&=\sum_{i=0}^{5}a_{l,g,i}u^i,
    \label{eq:rlb-p}\\
    Q_{l,g}(u)&=
    1+|b_{l,g,1}|\,|u|+|b_{l,g,2}|\,u^2
     +|b_{l,g,3}|\,|u|^3+|b_{l,g,4}|\,u^4,
    \label{eq:rlb-q}\\
    R_{l,g}(u)&=P_{l,g}(u)/Q_{l,g}(u).
    \label{eq:rlb-response}
\end{align}
Define the preactivation, RMS radius, normalized coordinate, and grouped
rational feature by
\begin{align}
    z_{l,g}(x_l)
    &=
    A_{l,g}x_l,
    \qquad
    \rho_{l,g}(x_l)
    =
    \sqrt{m^{-1}\lVert z_{l,g}(x_l)\rVert_2^2+\epsilon_{\rm rms}},
    \label{eq:rlb-coordinate}\\
    u_{l,g}(x_l)
    &=
    \frac{z_{l,g}(x_l)}{\rho_{l,g}(x_l)},
    \qquad
    h_{l,g}(x_l)
    =
    \rho_{l,g}(x_l)R_{l,g}(u_{l,g}(x_l)),
    \qquad
    h_l=\operatorname{concat}_{g=1}^{G}h_{l,g}.
    \label{eq:rlb-feature}
\end{align}
The RLB forward map is the single block expression
\begin{equation}
    y_l
    =
    B_lh_l
    =
    \sum_{g=1}^{G}
    B_{l,g}
    \rho_{l,g}(x_l)
    R_{l,g}\!\left(
    \frac{A_{l,g}x_l}{\rho_{l,g}(x_l)}
    \right).
    \label{eq:rlb-forward}
\end{equation}
The scalar functions $P_{l,g}$, $Q_{l,g}$, and $R_{l,g}$ are applied
coordinatewise when their input is a vector.  Since $Q_{l,g}(u)\ge1$ for all
real $u$, the implemented response has no real pole.  The response is piecewise
rational because of the absolute-value terms; derivative statistics use the
autodiff convention $\sign(0)=0$ and equations involving $R'_{l,g}$ are
interpreted almost everywhere.  Numerator and denominator coefficients are
initialized by a SiLU fit on $[-5,5]$ and are then trained by AdamW.  The evaluated
model uses only these groupwise P5/Q4 rational responses.

The forward map in \eqref{eq:rlb-forward} separates the normalized coordinate
at which the rational response is evaluated from the group radius returned to
the residual stream.  This radius-factor form exposes the positive homogeneity
behind the matrix-pair coordinate formalized in
Appendix~\ref{app:rlb-calculus}.  The floor
$\epsilon_{\rm rms}>0$ is an additive squared-RMS stabilizer.  For fixed curves
$R_l=\{R_{l,g}\}_{g=1}^{G}$, write this block map as $f_l(x;A_l,B_l,R_l)$.
For any fixed input $x$ such that
$A_{l,g}x\ne0$ for every group, if $\epsilon_{\rm rms}=0$, then for any
$\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{G}$ and
$D_l(\lambda)=\diag(\lambda_1I_m,\ldots,\lambda_GI_m)$,
\begin{align}
    f_l(x;A_l,B_l,R_l)
    =
    f_l(x;D_l(\lambda)A_l,B_lD_l(\lambda)^{-1},R_l).
    \label{eq:positive-rescale}
\end{align}
This positive matrix-pair rescaling is exact only in the unfloored map.  In the
actual training dynamics with the stabilizing RMS floor and optimizer state,
MatrixPolicy uses the same direction as a bounded balance coordinate.

\subsection{MatrixPolicy}
\label{sec:method-update}

MatrixPolicy is the optimizer-side policy attached to the RLB matrix pair
$(A_l,B_l)$.  We use the name for the matrix-specific controls in the evaluated
optimizer configuration.  Other Transformer tensors follow their assigned
backbone optimizer, and the rational coefficients contribute activity signals
while retaining their AdamW no-decay updates in the reported runs.  The policy
reads detached summaries.  These summaries are produced by the RLB forward
hook, by post-global-clipping gradients observed before MatrixPolicy rescales
them, and by fixed-grid probes of the rational response.  They feed, in order,
group-gradient gating, a role/depth matrix step, and reciprocal pair rescaling.

This ordering matters.  Let $t$ be the one-based optimizer step after the
wrapper increments its counter, $T$ the planned number of steps, and
$\xi_t=t/T$.  The surrounding optimizer wrapper first constructs detached
gradient memories from the current step; the inner matrix optimizer uses those
memories to rescale matrix gradients and apply AdamW/Muon updates; the wrapper
then measures the updated matrix pair and applies the reciprocal rescale.  No
summary tensor enters the forward graph.  For notation, set
$M_{l,\mathrm{in}}=A_l$ and $M_{l,\mathrm{out}}=B_l$, with
$\sigma\in\{\mathrm{in},\mathrm{out}\}$ denoting the two roles.  For any tensor
$V$, define
\begin{align}
    \rms(V)=\sqrt{\operatorname{mean}(V^2)},
    \qquad
    \rms_{\epsilon}(V)=\sqrt{\operatorname{mean}(V^2)+\epsilon}.
    \label{eq:rms-definition}
\end{align}
The evaluated implementation uses $\epsilon_{\rm live}=10^{-6}$ for cached
forward gains and $\epsilon_p=\epsilon_{\rm probe}=10^{-8}$ for pressure,
matrix-ratio, and fixed-grid probe floors.

The first family of summaries is the on-policy pressure and activity memory.
Here ``on-policy'' means that the wrapper records statistics from the current
training trajectory.  All gradients in this paragraph are optimizer-visible loss
gradients after global clipping and before MatrixPolicy group-gradient scaling.
Let $\Theta^R_{l,g}$ collect the trainable rational-response coefficient blocks
in group $(l,g)$; for the reported P5/Q4 response these are the numerator and
denominator coefficient tensors.  The count $|\Theta^R_{l,g}|$ is a block count,
and each $\operatorname{mean}$ is over scalar entries in one block.  The
instantaneous detached measurements are
\begin{align}
    p^{\rm in}_{l,g}
    &=
    \frac{\rms_{\epsilon_p}(\nabla A_{l,g})}
    {\rms_{\epsilon_p}(A_{l,g})},
    &
    p^{\rm out}_{l,g}
    &=
    \frac{\rms_{\epsilon_p}(\nabla B_{l,g})}
    {\rms_{\epsilon_p}(B_{l,g})},
    \label{eq:matrix-pressure}\\
    p^{R}_{l,g}
    &=
    \left[
    \frac{1}{|\Theta^R_{l,g}|}
    \sum_{\theta\in\Theta^R_{l,g}}
    \operatorname{mean}((\nabla\theta)^2)
    +\epsilon_p
    \right]^{1/2}.
    \label{eq:rational-activity-raw}
\end{align}
The matrix quantities are relative RMS-gradient proxies, while $p^R_{l,g}$ is
raw coefficient-gradient energy.  The wrapper stores detached EMA memories
$q^{\rm in}$, $q^{\rm out}$, and $q^R$,
\begin{align}
    q^{r}_{l,g,t}
    =
    0.95\,q^{r}_{l,g,t-1}+0.05\,p^{r}_{l,g,t},
    \qquad
    r\in\{\mathrm{in},\mathrm{out},R\},
    \label{eq:pressure-ema}
\end{align}
initialized from the first observed values.  We reserve $q$ in this subsection
for these detached policy memories; the rational denominator remains denoted by
$Q_{l,g}$.  The two contrasts used by all three controls are
\begin{align}
    \pi_{l,g}
    =
    \log q^{\rm in}_{l,g}-\log q^{\rm out}_{l,g},
    \qquad
    \alpha_{l,g}
    =
    \log q^{R}_{l,g}
    -\frac{1}{2}\left(\log q^{\rm in}_{l,g}+\log q^{\rm out}_{l,g}\right).
    \label{eq:pressure-activity}
\end{align}
The pressure contrast $\pi_{l,g}$ compares the two adjacent matrix roles, and
the activity contrast $\alpha_{l,g}$ compares rational-coefficient gradient
energy with the geometric mean of those role memories.  Appendix
\ref{app:matrixpolicy-proxies} derives the local gradient geometry behind these
proxies.  We drop the step subscript when the time is clear.

The second family of summaries comes from the RLB forward-statistics hook.  The
hook samples normalized coordinates and caches per-group response and derivative
RMS values,
\begin{align}
    \hat d^{\rm live}_{l,g}
    =
    \sqrt{\operatorname{mean}\!\left(R'_{l,g}(u_{l,g})^2\right)+\epsilon_{\rm live}},
    \qquad
    \hat o^{\rm live}_{l,g}
    =
    \sqrt{\operatorname{mean}\!\left(R_{l,g}(u_{l,g})^2\right)+\epsilon_{\rm live}}.
    \label{eq:live-gains}
\end{align}
The mean is over sampled scalar normalized coordinates for group $(l,g)$ across
the most recent refreshed sample, sequence positions, and the $m$ group
coordinates.  The derivative gain is the input-role gain proxy, and the response
gain is the output-role gain proxy.  If a cache is unavailable at a step, the
implementation sets the corresponding gain multiplier to identity while keeping
the pressure/activity memories active.

The third family is produced only for the post-step pair rescale.  Let
$\mathcal{U}=\{u_k\}_{k=1}^{129}$ be a uniform probe grid over $[-5,5]$, and
define
$\rms_{\mathcal{U}}(\varphi)=\sqrt{|\mathcal{U}|^{-1}\sum_{u_k\in\mathcal{U}}\varphi(u_k)^2}$.
The fixed-grid probe producer refreshes every ten optimizer steps and emits
\begin{align}
    \hat d^{\rm probe}_{l,g}
    &=
    \sqrt{\rms_{\mathcal U}(R'_{l,g})^2+\epsilon_{\rm probe}},
    \qquad
    \hat o^{\rm probe}_{l,g}
    =
    \sqrt{\rms_{\mathcal U}(R_{l,g})^2+\epsilon_{\rm probe}}.
    \label{eq:probe-gains}
\end{align}
These probes describe rational curve shape on a shared interval; they are
separate from the sampled live gains in \eqref{eq:live-gains}.  After the
role/depth matrix step and immediately before reciprocal rescaling, the wrapper
also measures the detached pair-state coordinate
\begin{align}
    \gamma_{l,g}
    =
    \log\rms_{\epsilon_p}(A_{l,g})
    -\log\rms_{\epsilon_p}(B_{l,g}).
    \label{eq:post-step-pair-ratio}
\end{align}
Only the probe gains and $\gamma_{l,g}$ are emitted by this summary family.  The
third control then computes $\tau_{l,g}$ and $\chi^{\rm rs}_{l,g}$ from those
summaries and from the pressure and activity memories.

\subsubsection{Group-Gradient Gating}

The first control redistributes gradient scale among groups inside each matrix
role.  It reads the sampled live gains, the pressure contrast $\pi_{l,g}$, and
the activity contrast $\alpha_{l,g}$, then produces scaled gradients for
$A_l$ and $B_l$.  A smooth gate
$\phi_t=\smooth(0.02,0.30,\xi_t)$ turns on this control in the reported runs.
The evaluated manifest sets gain, pressure, and activity strengths to $0.20$,
$0.10$, and $0.20$, and clips group multipliers to $[0.75,1.35]$.  For positive
group values $v_j$, define
$\geom_{j=1}^{G}(v_j)=\exp\!\left(G^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{G}\log v_j\right)$.  The
group-gradient rule is
\begin{equation}
\begin{alignedat}{2}
    \tilde{\pi}_{l,g}
    &=
    \clip(\pi_{l,g},-1.50,1.50),
    \qquad&
    c^{\rm activity}_{l,g}
    &=
    \exp\!\left[
    -0.20\phi_t
    \relu\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{l,g}-0.05}{0.45}\right)
    \right],
    \\[2pt]
    c^{\rm gain}_{l,g,\mathrm{in}}
    &=
    \left(
    \frac{\geom_{j=1}^{G}\hat d^{\rm live}_{l,j}}
         {\hat d^{\rm live}_{l,g}}
    \right)^{0.20\phi_t},
    &
    c^{\rm gain}_{l,g,\mathrm{out}}
    &=
    \left(
    \frac{\geom_{j=1}^{G}\hat o^{\rm live}_{l,j}}
         {\hat o^{\rm live}_{l,g}}
    \right)^{0.20\phi_t},
    \\[2pt]
    c^{\rm pressure}_{l,g,\mathrm{in}}
    &=
    \exp(-0.10\phi_t\tilde{\pi}_{l,g}),
    &
    c^{\rm pressure}_{l,g,\mathrm{out}}
    &=
    \exp(+0.10\phi_t\tilde{\pi}_{l,g}),
    \\[2pt]
    c^{\rm raw}_{l,g,\sigma}
    &=
    c^{\rm gain}_{l,g,\sigma}
    c^{\rm pressure}_{l,g,\sigma}
    c^{\rm activity}_{l,g},
    &
    c_{l,g,\sigma}
    &=
    \clip\!\left(
    \frac{c^{\rm raw}_{l,g,\sigma}}
         {\geom_{j=1}^{G}c^{\rm raw}_{l,j,\sigma}},
    0.75,1.35
    \right).
\end{alignedat}
\label{eq:group-policy}
\end{equation}
Live derivative gain controls the input role, live response gain controls the
output role, and pressure acts with opposite signs on the two roles.  When
$\pi_{l,g}>0$, the input-side relative-gradient EMA is larger than the
output-side EMA, so the gate reduces the input-role gradient scale and increases
the output-role gradient scale for that group.  The activity term damps groups
whose rational coefficient gradients are large relative to the adjacent matrix
memories.  The geometric recentering preserves the role-wise mean log multiplier
before clipping; Appendix~\ref{app:matrixpolicy-role-gate} gives the log-space
identity.

The scaled matrix gradients are
\begin{align}
    \widetilde\nabla A_{l,g}=c_{l,g,\mathrm{in}}\nabla A_{l,g},
    \qquad
    \widetilde\nabla B_{l,g}=c_{l,g,\mathrm{out}}\nabla B_{l,g}.
    \label{eq:scaled-gradients}
\end{align}
Let $\widetilde\nabla M_{l,\mathrm{in}}$ be the row-block concatenation of
$\{\widetilde\nabla A_{l,g}\}_{g=1}^{G}$, and let
$\widetilde\nabla M_{l,\mathrm{out}}$ be the column-block concatenation of
$\{\widetilde\nabla B_{l,g}\}_{g=1}^{G}$.

\subsubsection{Role/Depth Matrix Step}

The second control applies the matrix optimizer step to the scaled gradients.
It assigns different depth trends to the input and output roles, runs AdamW on
both matrices, and activates a transient Muon substep early in training.  In the
reported configuration, the AdamW multiplier depends on role and depth; the
pressure and activity memories enter this control through the Muon attenuation
factor.  Let $\delta_l=(l-1)/(L-1)$ for $L>1$ and $\delta_l=1/2$ otherwise.  The
branch schedules are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \varrho_{\mathrm{in}}(l)
    &=
    \clip(1-0.50(\delta_l-0.5),0.55,1.40),
    \\
    \varrho_{\mathrm{out}}(l)
    &=
    \clip(1+1.00(\delta_l-0.5),0.55,1.40),
    \\[2pt]
    s^{\rm Adam}_{l,\sigma}
    &=
    \clip\!\left(
    3.0\max\{0.10,1+1.20(\varrho_\sigma(l)-1)\},
    0.40,4.0
    \right),
    \\[2pt]
    \Psi_{l,t}
    &=
    \clip\!\left(G^{-1}\sum_{g=1}^{G}|\pi_{l,g}|,0,1.50\right),
    \\
    \Omega_{l,t}
    &=
    \relu\!\left(\frac{G^{-1}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\alpha_{l,g}-0.05}{0.45}\right),
    \\[2pt]
    \psi_{l,t}
    &=
    \clip\!\left(\exp[-0.30\Psi_{l,t}-0.65\Omega_{l,t}],0.10,1.15\right),
    \\[2pt]
    \omega^{\mu}_t
    &=
    \smooth(0.02,0.12,\xi_t)
    [1-\smooth(0.20,0.36,\xi_t)],
    \\[2pt]
    \mu_{l,\sigma,t}
    &=
    \clip\!\left(
    0.75\,\omega^{\mu}_t\varrho_\sigma(l)\psi_{l,t},
    0,0.75
    \right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrix-branch-schedule}
\end{equation}
Here $s^{\rm Adam}_{l,\sigma}$ is the role/depth AdamW multiplier.  The aggregate
pressure $\Psi_{l,t}$ and activity $\Omega_{l,t}$ form the Muon attenuation
$\psi_{l,t}$, so the active Muon fraction $\mu_{l,\sigma,t}$ is largest during
the early Muon window and shrinks when the detached summaries indicate large
role imbalance or large rational-coefficient activity.
For $M_{l,\mathrm{in}}=A_l$ and $M_{l,\mathrm{out}}=B_l$, let $\eta_t$ be the
base learning rate and let $s_\mu=1.0$ be the evaluated Muon learning-rate scale.
MatrixPolicy applies an AdamW matrix step followed by any active Muon substep.
The gate $\mu_{l,\sigma,t}$ scales the two optimizer learning rates; AdamW and
Muon keep separate states and are applied sequentially:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
    \widetilde M_{l,\sigma}^{\,t+1}
    =
    \operatorname{AdamW}_{\eta_t s^{\rm Adam}_{l,\sigma}(1-\mu_{l,\sigma,t})}
    (M_{l,\sigma}^{t};\widetilde\nabla M_{l,\sigma}^{t}),\\
    M_{l,\sigma}^{t+1}
    =
    \operatorname{Muon}_{\eta_t s_\mu \mu_{l,\sigma,t}}
    (\widetilde M_{l,\sigma}^{\,t+1};\widetilde\nabla M_{l,\sigma}^{t}),
\end{gathered}
   \label{eq:matrix-branch-step}
\end{equation}
The AdamW step uses the training-protocol betas, weight decay, and
learning-rate schedule in Appendix~\ref{app:experiment-protocol}.  The Muon
substep is the \texttt{torch.optim.Muon} update on two-dimensional RLB matrices
with momentum $0.95$, five Newton--Schulz steps, and match-RMS learning-rate
adjustment \citep{liu2025muon}; it becomes inactive when all
$\mu_{l,\sigma,t}$ decay to zero.  Appendix~\ref{app:matrixpolicy-muon-control}
states the matrix-spectrum geometry behind this transient branch.

\subsubsection{Reciprocal Pair Rescaling}

The third control chooses a bounded positive representative of the same RLB
matrix pair after the matrix update.  It is implemented by the surrounding
transport wrapper and is evaluated every five optimizer steps after the
AdamW/Muon branch; fixed-grid probe gains are refreshed every ten optimizer
steps and reused between refreshes.  It consumes the probe gains, the post-step
log ratio $\gamma_{l,g}$, and the pressure/activity memories.  The target
combines the probe-gain balance coordinate derived in
Appendix~\ref{app:matrixpolicy-pair-rescale} with a bounded pressure offset:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \bar{\pi}_{l,g}
    &=
    \clip(\pi_{l,g},-1.25,1.25),
    \\[2pt]
    \tau_{l,g}
    &=
    \frac{1}{2}
    \left(
    \log \hat d^{\rm probe}_{l,g}
    -
    \log \hat o^{\rm probe}_{l,g}
    \right)
    +0.25\,\bar{\pi}_{l,g},
    \\
    \bar{\alpha}_{l,g}
    &=
    \clip(\alpha_{l,g},-1.25,1.25),
    \\[2pt]
    \chi^{\rm rs}_{l,g}
    &=
    \exp(0.10\,\bar{\alpha}_{l,g}),
    \qquad \text{(rescale-speed factor)}
    \\[2pt]
    s_{l,t}
    &=
    0.50\,\smooth(0.03,0.35,\xi_t)
    \clip(1+0.15(\delta_l-0.5),0.75,1.25).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:rescale-signals}
\end{equation}
The current log ratio $\gamma_{l,g}$ is measured after the matrix step.  The
clipped log step and reciprocal update are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \ell_{l,g,t}
    &=
    \clip\!\left(
    \frac{1}{2}[\tau_{l,g}-\gamma_{l,g}]s_{l,t}\chi^{\rm rs}_{l,g},
    -0.030,0.030
    \right),
    \\
    A_{l,g}
    &\leftarrow
    e^{\ell_{l,g,t}}A_{l,g},
    \qquad
    B_{l,g}
    \leftarrow
    e^{-\ell_{l,g,t}}B_{l,g}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:rescale-step}
\end{equation}
The first term of $\tau_{l,g}$ equalizes derivative and response probe gains in
the positive log coordinate, the pressure term shifts the target within a
bounded range, and $\chi^{\rm rs}$ changes the clipped rescale speed.  Larger
$\alpha_{l,g}$ accelerates this representative-balancing move, while the earlier
gradient gate and Muon attenuation use the same activity contrast to reduce
gradient-driven matrix motion.  Because \eqref{eq:rescale-signals} uses floored
RMS values, \eqref{eq:rescale-step} is exact in the nonzero, unfloored
idealization described by \eqref{eq:positive-rescale}; near the RMS floor it is a
clipped balance step.  Optimizer-state buffers are scaled covariantly:
first-moment-like entries for $A_{l,g}$ use $e^{\ell_{l,g,t}}$,
first-moment-like entries for $B_{l,g}$ use $e^{-\ell_{l,g,t}}$, and
square-average entries use the corresponding squared scales.

\section{Rational Latent Basis Calculus}
\label{app:rlb-calculus}

This appendix establishes three local identities underlying RLB and
MatrixPolicy.  They are the pole-free P5/Q4 response derivatives, the Jacobian
of the RMS-normalized group map, and the positive matrix-pair coordinate used by
MatrixPolicy.  All calculations are local to one layer and one group unless
indices are shown explicitly.

\subsection{Scalar Response Derivatives}
\label{app:rlb-response-derivatives}

The evaluated response is the groupwise P5/Q4 function defined in the method.
For a fixed layer-group pair $(l,g)$, suppress indices and write
\begin{align}
    P(u)
    &=
    \sum_{i=0}^{5}a_i u^i,
    \notag\\
    \phi_1(u)=|u|,\quad
    \phi_2(u)=u^2,\quad
    \phi_3(u)=|u|^3,\quad
    \phi_4(u)=u^4,
    \notag\\
    Q(u)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{4}|b_j|\phi_j(u),
    \qquad
    R(u)=P(u)/Q(u).
    \label{eq:appendix-local-pq}
\end{align}
For vector inputs below, $R(u)$ and $R'(u)$ are applied coordinatewise.  Since
$Q(u)\ge 1$ on the real line, the scalar response has no real pole, while its
numerator and denominator coefficients remain explicit trainable coordinates.
Away from $u=0$,
\begin{align}
    R'(u)
    &=
    \frac{P'(u)Q(u)-P(u)Q'(u)}{Q(u)^2},
    \label{eq:appendix-r-prime}\\
    P'(u)
    &=
    \sum_{i=1}^{5} i a_i u^{i-1},
    &
    Q'(u)
    &=
    |b_1|\sign(u)+2|b_2|u+3|b_3|u|u|+4|b_4|u^3 .
\end{align}
For derivatives with respect to $u$, we use the autodiff subgradient convention
$\sign(0)=0$ at $u=0$.  The coefficient features are
\begin{align}
    \frac{\partial R(u)}{\partial a_i}
    &=
    \frac{u^i}{Q(u)},
    &
    \frac{\partial R(u)}{\partial b_j}
    &=
    -\frac{P(u)}{Q(u)^2}\sign(b_j)\phi_j(u),
    \label{eq:appendix-coeff-features}
\end{align}
with the coefficient subgradient convention $\sign(0)=0$ at $b_j=0$.  For the
RLB feature $h(z)=r(z)R(u(z))$, these scalar features are multiplied by $r(z)$
before the output matrix and upstream adjoint enter the loss gradient.
Consequently the three summary families in the method have direct scalar
origins: $R(u)$ for response gain, $R'(u)$ for derivative gain, and coefficient
gradients through the features in \eqref{eq:appendix-coeff-features}.  Here
$\Theta^R_{l,g}$ denotes the response-parameter block used in the
rational-activity summary \eqref{eq:rational-activity-raw}; for the evaluated
P5/Q4 response, $\Theta^R_{l,g}=\{a_{l,g,\cdot},b_{l,g,\cdot}\}$.

\subsection{RMS-Normalized Group Map and Positive Pair Coordinate}
\label{app:rlb-positive-coordinate}

Let $m$ be the group width, and let $\epsilon_{\rm rms}\ge0$ denote the additive
squared-RMS stabilizer used in the normalized RLB coordinate, so the minimum
radius is $\sqrt{\epsilon_{\rm rms}}$.  For a group vector
$z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$, define
\begin{align}
    r(z)=\sqrt{m^{-1}\lVert z\rVert_2^2+\epsilon_{\rm rms}},
    \qquad
    u(z)=z/r(z),
    \qquad
    h(z)=r(z)R(u(z)),
\end{align}
where $R$ acts coordinatewise.  On the domain $r(z)>0$, the radius and
normalized-coordinate differentials are
\begin{align}
    \mathrm{d}r
    &=
    \frac{z^\top\mathrm{d}z}{m r}
    =
    \frac{u^\top\mathrm{d}z}{m},
    &
    \mathrm{d}u
    &=
    \frac{1}{r}\left(I-\frac{uu^\top}{m}\right)\mathrm{d}z .
    \label{eq:appendix-du}
\end{align}
The factor $I-uu^\top/m$ is the exact differential factor of the implemented
normalized coordinate; it becomes the tangent projector to the RMS sphere in the
unfloored case.  Away from scalar-response kinks, the group Jacobian is
\begin{align}
    J_h(z)
    :=
    \frac{\partial h}{\partial z}
    =
    R(u)\frac{u^\top}{m}
    +
    \diag(R'(u))\left(I-\frac{uu^\top}{m}\right).
    \label{eq:appendix-group-jacobian}
\end{align}
The Jacobian in \eqref{eq:appendix-group-jacobian} separates the radial response
term from the normalized-coordinate derivative term.  Response RMS therefore
serves as an output-role signal, and derivative RMS serves as an input-role
signal.  Both remain proxies: response RMS omits the radius, while derivative
RMS omits the radial Jacobian term, the output matrix, and the upstream gradient.

The same normalization gives the positive matrix-pair coordinate used by
MatrixPolicy.  Set $\epsilon_{\rm rms}=0$ and write $r_0,u_0,h_0$ for the
resulting unfloored map.  If $z\ne0$ and $\lambda>0$, then
\begin{align}
    r_0(\lambda z)=\lambda r_0(z),
    \qquad
    u_0(\lambda z)=u_0(z),
    \qquad
    h_0(\lambda z)=\lambda h_0(z).
\end{align}
For a group contribution $y_{l,g}=B_{l,g}h(A_{l,g}x_l)$, with $R$ fixed,
$\epsilon_{\rm rms}=0$, and $A_{l,g}x_l\ne0$, this homogeneity creates a
positive scale redundancy.  Scaling $A_{l,g}$ by $\lambda$ and the matching block
$B_{l,g}$ by $\lambda^{-1}$ leaves $y_{l,g}$ unchanged, giving
\eqref{eq:positive-rescale} groupwise.  The implemented floor perturbs this
symmetry only through the radius and normalized coordinate.  Write
\begin{align}
    r_\epsilon(z)
    &=
    \sqrt{m^{-1}\lVert z\rVert_2^2+\epsilon_{\rm rms}},
    &
    u_\epsilon(z)
    &=
    z/r_\epsilon(z),
    &
    h_\epsilon(z)
    &=
    r_\epsilon(z)R(u_\epsilon(z)).
\end{align}
For $\rho^2=m^{-1}\lVert z\rVert_2^2$,
\begin{align}
    u_\epsilon(\lambda z)
    =
    \kappa(\lambda,z)u_\epsilon(z),
    \qquad
    \kappa(\lambda,z)
    =
    \frac{\lambda\sqrt{\rho^2+\epsilon_{\rm rms}}}
    {\sqrt{\lambda^2\rho^2+\epsilon_{\rm rms}}}.
    \label{eq:appendix-kappa}
\end{align}
The floor error separates into a radius error and a normalized-coordinate error.
If $L_R$ is a Lipschitz constant of the scalar response on an interval
containing every coordinate on the segment between $u_\epsilon(z)$ and
$\kappa(\lambda,z)u_\epsilon(z)$, then after the reciprocal output compensation,
\begin{align}
    \left\|\lambda^{-1}h_\epsilon(\lambda z)-h_\epsilon(z)\right\|_2
    &\le
    \left|\frac{r_\epsilon(\lambda z)}{\lambda}-r_\epsilon(z)\right|
    \left\|R(u_\epsilon(z))\right\|_2 \notag\\
    &\quad+
    \frac{r_\epsilon(\lambda z)}{\lambda}
    L_R|\kappa(\lambda,z)-1|\left\|u_\epsilon(z)\right\|_2 .
    \label{eq:appendix-floor-bound}
\end{align}
For $z\ne0$, both terms vanish when $\epsilon_{\rm rms}=0$.  Under
$|\log\lambda|\le c$, the relative radius mismatch
$|r_\epsilon(\lambda z)/(\lambda r_\epsilon(z))-1|$ and
$|\kappa(\lambda,z)-1|$ are
$O(e^{2c}\epsilon_{\rm rms}/\rho^2)$ as
$\epsilon_{\rm rms}/\rho^2\to0$; equivalently, the absolute radius mismatch in
\eqref{eq:appendix-floor-bound} is $O(e^{2c}\epsilon_{\rm rms}/\rho)$.
The constants depend on the response and derivative bounds over the interval
above.  Near the RMS floor, the reciprocal pair direction is therefore only an
approximate balance direction, so MatrixPolicy uses bounded log-scale moves
rather than treating it as an exact symmetry.

\section{Optimizer Design and MatrixPolicy Geometry}
\label{app:matrixpolicy-step}

This appendix follows the order of the optimizer pipeline.  First, it relates
the detached summaries to local matrix-gradient geometry.  It then analyzes the
role gate, the early matrix-normalized branch, and the final reciprocal
pair-rescale move.  In the reported configuration, the transport wrapper is used
for detached pressure/activity memories and reciprocal pair rescaling; quotient
projection, coefficient metric preconditioning, matrix preconditioning, pressure
preconditioning, and curve-amplitude transport have zero strength.
The effective order is
\begin{align}
    \nabla\mathcal{L}
    &\longrightarrow
    \operatorname{sg}(q^{\rm in},q^{\rm out},q^R,\pi,\alpha)
    \longrightarrow
    (\widetilde\nabla A_{l,g},\widetilde\nabla B_{l,g})
    \notag\\
    &\longrightarrow
    (\Delta A^{\rm Adam+\mu}_{l,g},\Delta B^{\rm Adam+\mu}_{l,g})
    \longrightarrow
    (A_{l,g},B_{l,g})
    \mapsto
    (e^\ell A_{l,g},e^{-\ell}B_{l,g}).
    \label{eq:appendix-matrixpolicy-pipeline}
\end{align}

\subsection{Detached Summaries as Matrix-Role Proxies}
\label{app:matrixpolicy-proxies}

For one token of one training example, let $\bar y_l=\nabla_{y_l}\mathcal{L}$ and
$J_{l,g}=\partial h_{l,g}/\partial z_{l,g}$ from
\eqref{eq:appendix-group-jacobian}.  Here $x_l$ is the RLB input,
$z_{l,g}=A_{l,g}x_l$, $h_{l,g}=h(z_{l,g})$, and
$y_l=\sum_g B_{l,g}h_{l,g}$.  The adjacent matrix gradients factor as
\begin{align}
    \nabla_{B_{l,g}}\mathcal{L}
    &=
    \bar y_l h_{l,g}^\top,
    &
    \nabla_{A_{l,g}}\mathcal{L}
    &=
    \left(J_{l,g}^\top B_{l,g}^\top\bar y_l\right)x_l^\top .
    \label{eq:appendix-matrix-gradients}
\end{align}
Batch and sequence gradients are sums or averages of these outer-product terms.
The factorization in \eqref{eq:appendix-matrix-gradients} separates the two
local role signals.  The output-role gradient depends on the realized feature
$h_{l,g}$, whereas the input-role gradient depends on the local RLB Jacobian
before propagation to $x_l$.  The response RMS and derivative RMS in
\eqref{eq:live-gains} therefore serve as local output- and input-role gain
proxies, with data distribution, radius factors, matrix orientation, and
upstream gradients supplied by the full training gradient.

The pressure memories in \eqref{eq:pressure-ema} summarize post-global-clipping,
pre-group-policy gradients as update-magnitude proxies.  Their signed contrast
is $\pi_{l,g}=\log q^{\rm in}_{l,g}-\log q^{\rm out}_{l,g}$.  The scalar proxy
behind these memories is the first-order change in log RMS.  For either adjacent
matrix block, write the current matrix as $W$ and a small update as
$W^+=W-\eta\mathcal{G}$.  In the unfloored model, for nonzero $W$ satisfying
$\eta\lVert\mathcal{G}\rVert_F<c\lVert W\rVert_F$ with fixed $c<1$,
\begin{align}
    \log\rms(W^+)-\log\rms(W)
    =
    -\eta
    \frac{\langle\mathcal{G},W\rangle_F}{\lVert W\rVert_F^2}
    +O\!\left(
    \eta^2\frac{\lVert\mathcal{G}\rVert_F^2}{\lVert W\rVert_F^2}
    \right),
    \label{eq:appendix-log-rms-update}
\end{align}
and by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\begin{align}
    \left|
    \frac{\langle\mathcal{G},W\rangle_F}{\lVert W\rVert_F^2}
    \right|
    \le
    \frac{\lVert\mathcal{G}\rVert_F}{\lVert W\rVert_F}
    =
    \frac{\rms(\mathcal{G})}{\rms(W)} .
    \label{eq:appendix-pressure-bound}
\end{align}
The implemented $p^{\rm in}$ and $p^{\rm out}$ replace the last ratio by
floored RMS proxies.  The floor regularizes the ratio near zero, while the clips
in the method turn their log contrasts into bounded controls.  Thus
$\pi_{l,g}$ identifies which role currently has the larger EMA of the relative
update proxy, and $\alpha_{l,g}$ identifies when raw rational-coefficient
gradient energy is large relative to adjacent matrix-pressure memories.  Along
the exact positive pair direction of the unfloored fixed-curve map, reciprocal
infinitesimal rescaling has zero data-loss directional derivative; the rescale
subsection below uses this same coordinate for bounded pressure balancing.

\subsection{Role-Gate Log Geometry}
\label{app:matrixpolicy-role-gate}

The group-gradient policy in \eqref{eq:group-policy} acts in log space.  Let
\begin{align}
    \bar d_l
    =
    \frac{1}{G}\sum_{j=1}^{G}\log \hat d^{\rm live}_{l,j},
    \qquad
    \bar o_l
    =
    \frac{1}{G}\sum_{j=1}^{G}\log \hat o^{\rm live}_{l,j}.
\end{align}
Before clipping and activity damping, the gain component satisfies
\begin{align}
    \log c^{\rm gain}_{l,g,\mathrm{in}}
    =
    -0.20\phi_t(\log\hat d^{\rm live}_{l,g}-\bar d_l),
    \qquad
    \log c^{\rm gain}_{l,g,\mathrm{out}}
    =
    -0.20\phi_t(\log\hat o^{\rm live}_{l,g}-\bar o_l).
    \label{eq:appendix-gain-centering}
\end{align}
Thus groups with above-geometric-mean sampled derivative or response gain receive
smaller gradient multipliers in the corresponding role, while below-mean groups
receive larger multipliers.  The pressure factor has the role signs
\begin{align}
    \log c^{\rm pressure}_{l,g,\mathrm{in}}
    =
    -0.10\phi_t\,\tilde\pi_{l,g},
    \qquad
    \log c^{\rm pressure}_{l,g,\mathrm{out}}
    =
    +0.10\phi_t\,\tilde\pi_{l,g},
    \label{eq:appendix-pressure-signs}
\end{align}
before geometric recentering.  Hence larger input-role pressure decreases the
input-side log scale and increases the output-side log scale relative to groups
with smaller pressure contrast.  The rational-activity term then damps both
adjacent matrix roles for groups whose response coefficients are moving strongly:
\begin{align}
    \log c^{\rm activity}_{l,g}
    =
    -0.20\phi_t
    \relu\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{l,g}-0.05}{0.45}\right).
    \label{eq:appendix-activity-damping}
\end{align}
This activity term contributes the same groupwise log factor to both roles
before role-wise recentering.  Groups with large rational-coefficient activity
therefore receive smaller adjacent matrix-gradient multipliers.  After
multiplying the gain, pressure, and activity terms, recentering by the group
geometric mean gives
\begin{align}
    \widehat c_{l,g,\sigma}
    =
    \frac{c_{l,g,\sigma}}{\geom_{j=1}^{G}c_{l,j,\sigma}},
    \qquad
    \frac{1}{G}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\log \widehat c_{l,g,\sigma}=0 .
    \label{eq:appendix-centered-policy}
\end{align}
Together, the gain, pressure, activity, and recentering equations make the first
control a bounded redistribution of role-specific update scale.  The role-wise
mean log multiplier is zero before clipping.  The policy changes only groupwise
magnitudes; within each group, the update direction remains the current
optimizer-gradient direction.

\subsection{Early Matrix-Normalized Branch}
\label{app:matrixpolicy-muon-control}

The second control combines a persistent coordinate-adaptive AdamW component
with an early matrix-normalized Muon component.  For a matrix gradient $G$,
AdamW keeps entrywise moment estimates \citep{kingma2015iclr-adam,loshchilov2019decoupled},
\begin{align}
    M &\leftarrow \beta_1M+(1-\beta_1)G,
    &
    V &\leftarrow \beta_2V+(1-\beta_2)(G\odot G),
    \notag\\
    D^{\rm Adam}_{ij}
    &=
    \frac{M_{ij}}{\sqrt{V_{ij}}+\epsilon},
    \label{eq:appendix-adam-coordinate}
\end{align}
suppressing bias correction and weight decay.  This normalization adapts matrix
entries coordinatewise.  It leaves row subspaces, column subspaces, and singular
values to emerge implicitly from the entrywise-scaled update.

Matrix spectra matter for the RLB pair because
\eqref{eq:appendix-matrix-gradients} gives single-example adjacent gradients as
outer products; after averaging over batch and sequence positions, the training
gradients are sums of such terms.  Singular modes of $B_l$ couple
output-residual directions to latent features, and singular modes of $A_l$
couple latent coordinates to input-residual directions.  During early basis
formation, a highly anisotropic singular spectrum moves only a narrow set of
these coupled directions strongly.

Let $H_{l,\sigma,t}=U\Sigma V^\top$ be the compact SVD of the Muon momentum
matrix for role $\sigma\in\{\mathrm{in},\mathrm{out}\}$.  The ideal polar
direction is
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{P}(H_{l,\sigma,t})=UV^\top,
    \qquad
    H=\sum_i\sigma_i u_iv_i^\top
    \mapsto
    \sum_i u_iv_i^\top
    \quad\text{on the nonzero singular subspace}.
    \label{eq:appendix-polar-map}
\end{align}
Thus the Muon branch inherits the row and column spaces of the matrix-gradient
momentum and changes the singular-value profile.  The canonical polar factor is
a maximizer of the spectral-norm constrained linearized descent problem,
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{P}(H)
    \in
    \argmax_{\lVert D\rVert_2\le 1}\langle D,H\rangle_F,
    \qquad
    \langle\mathcal{P}(H),H\rangle_F=\lVert H\rVert_*,
    \qquad
    \mathcal{P}(aH)=\mathcal{P}(H)
    \quad(a>0,\ H\ne0).
    \label{eq:appendix-polar-steepest}
\end{align}
The implementation uses the Muon finite Newton--Schulz approximation with
momentum and match-RMS learning-rate adjustment \citep{liu2025muon}.

The branch equation in \eqref{eq:matrix-branch-step} is sequential because AdamW
and Muon keep separate optimizer states.  Its first-order expansion is
\begin{align}
    \Delta M_{l,\sigma}
    =
    -\eta_t s^{\rm Adam}_{l,\sigma}(1-\mu_{l,\sigma,t})
    D^{\rm Adam}_{l,\sigma,t}
    -\eta_t s_\mu\mu_{l,\sigma,t}D^\mu_{l,\sigma,t}
    +O(\eta_t^2),
    \label{eq:appendix-branch-first-order}
\end{align}
after the role-aware group scaling has set the group/role gradient scales.
Flatter singular weighting gives weaker but nonzero basis-forming directions of
$A_l$ and $B_l$ more relative movement early.  The spectrum-normalized branch
therefore acts only during the early window; after it decays, the persistent
matrix controls are AdamW and reciprocal pair rescaling.

The Muon gate in \eqref{eq:matrix-branch-schedule} is monotone in the two layer
summaries.  Away from clipping boundaries and the ReLU kink, the derivatives of
$\log\psi_{l,t}$ with respect to $\Psi_{l,t}$ and $\Omega_{l,t}$ are $-0.30$
and $-0.65$ inside the $\psi$ clipping interval.  Thus the Muon fraction is
largest when role pressure and rational-coefficient activity are both small.
Large $|\pi_{l,g}|$ marks
unequal input/output pressure, and large $\alpha_{l,g}$ marks rational
coefficients moving strongly relative to the matrix pair.  These are precisely
the regimes where the spectrum-normalized substep is attenuated.

\subsection{Reciprocal Pair-Rescale Geometry}
\label{app:matrixpolicy-pair-rescale}

The final MatrixPolicy operation on each RLB group is the reciprocal
reparameterization in \eqref{eq:rescale-step}.  It is applied after the
AdamW/Muon matrix branch by the transport wrapper, every five optimizer steps in
the reported configuration.  The preceding branch supplies the descent update;
the reciprocal move is a bounded post-step reparameterization of the same
matrix pair,
\begin{align}
    A_{l,g}^{+}=e^{\ell_{l,g,t}}A_{l,g},
    \qquad
    B_{l,g}^{+}=e^{-\ell_{l,g,t}}B_{l,g},
    \label{eq:appendix-reciprocal-move}
\end{align}
The construction follows the positive pair coordinate in
Appendix~\ref{app:rlb-positive-coordinate}.  With the rational response held
fixed, the unfloored map satisfies
$h_{l,g}(\lambda A_{l,g}x)=\lambda h_{l,g}(A_{l,g}x)$ for $\lambda>0$, so
replacing $(A_{l,g},B_{l,g})$ by
$(\lambda A_{l,g},\lambda^{-1}B_{l,g})$ leaves $B_{l,g}h_{l,g}$ unchanged.
The RMS floor breaks exact invariance, with material error only near the floor,
which is why the implemented move uses floored RMS values and a small clipped
log step.

This coordinate controls training dynamics because near-equivalent
representatives can have different relative gradient pressures.
Let
\begin{align}
    p^{A}_{l,g}
    =
    \frac{\rms(\nabla A_{l,g})}{\rms(A_{l,g})},
    \qquad
    p^{B}_{l,g}
    =
    \frac{\rms(\nabla B_{l,g})}{\rms(B_{l,g})}.
\end{align}
In the homogeneous unfloored model, $J_h(\lambda z)=J_h(z)$ almost everywhere.
Here $p^{A,+}_{l,g}$ and $p^{B,+}_{l,g}$ denote the relative gradients that
would be observed after re-evaluating the loss at the rescaled representative.
Combining this with \eqref{eq:appendix-matrix-gradients}, the reciprocal
change in \eqref{eq:appendix-reciprocal-move} gives the role-pressure scaling
\begin{align}
    p^{A,+}_{l,g}
    \approx
    e^{-2\ell_{l,g,t}}p^{A}_{l,g},
    \qquad
    p^{B,+}_{l,g}
    \approx
    e^{2\ell_{l,g,t}}p^{B}_{l,g}.
    \label{eq:appendix-pressure-gauge}
\end{align}
Thus increasing the log-ratio coordinate reduces relative input-side pressure
and increases relative output-side pressure in the representative model.  This
is the sign used by the pressure term in \eqref{eq:rescale-signals}.  When
$\pi_{l,g}>0$, the positive offset raises the target $\gamma$, moving the
representative in the direction that reduces future input-side relative pressure
and increases future output-side relative pressure.

The curve-shape part of the target is also a balance condition.  In the same
representative coordinate, input-role scale varies as
$\hat d^{\rm probe}_{l,g}e^{-\gamma_{l,g}}$ and output-role scale varies as
$\hat o^{\rm probe}_{l,g}e^{\gamma_{l,g}}$.  Equalizing these two role scales
gives
\begin{align}
    \gamma^\star_{l,g}
    =
    \frac{1}{2}
    \left(
    \log\hat d^{\rm probe}_{l,g}
    -
    \log\hat o^{\rm probe}_{l,g}
    \right),
    \label{eq:appendix-gamma-star}
\end{align}
which is the first term of $\tau_{l,g}$.  The probes are fixed-grid summaries of
the current rational curve; consequently the target follows the shared curve
shape over $\mathcal U$.

For nonzero matrices and unfloored RMS values,
\begin{align}
    \gamma_{l,g}
    &=
    \log\rms(A_{l,g})-\log\rms(B_{l,g}),
    \notag\\
    \gamma_{l,g}^{+}
    &=
    \gamma_{l,g}+2\ell_{l,g,t}.
    \label{eq:appendix-gamma-shift}
\end{align}
Substituting the unclipped log step from \eqref{eq:rescale-step} gives
\begin{align}
    \lambda_{l,g,t}
    &=
    s_{l,t}\chi^{\rm rs}_{l,g},
    \notag\\
    \gamma_{l,g}^{+}
    &=
    (1-\lambda_{l,g,t})\gamma_{l,g}
    +\lambda_{l,g,t}\tau_{l,g}.
    \label{eq:appendix-rescale-contraction}
\end{align}
Because $0\le s_{l,t}\le0.625$ and
$\chi^{\rm rs}_{l,g}\le\exp(0.125)$ under the method clips,
$0\le\lambda_{l,g,t}<1$ before log-step clipping, so the representative
coordinate contracts toward $\tau_{l,g}$.  The clip
$|\ell_{l,g,t}|\le0.030$ bounds each active reciprocal move and keeps the floor
error in Appendix~\ref{app:rlb-positive-coordinate} within the local regime.

The activity factor has the opposite sign in this speed control.  Large
$\alpha_{l,g}$ damps gradient-driven matrix motion in \eqref{eq:group-policy}
and \eqref{eq:matrix-branch-schedule}; here
$\chi^{\rm rs}_{l,g}=\exp(0.10\bar\alpha_{l,g})$ only changes the speed of the
post-step representative move and does not enlarge the gradient step.  The
wrapper also rescales
optimizer-state buffers covariantly: first-moment-like entries use the same
factor as the corresponding matrix block, and square-average entries use the
squared factor.  Together with Appendix~\ref{app:rlb-positive-coordinate}, these
relations specify both the mathematical direction and the approximation boundary
of the pair-rescale move used in MatrixPolicy.
