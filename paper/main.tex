\begin{abstract}
Learning an activation function changes both the represented features and
their response to parameter updates. We introduce full TILLER (Tangents
Informed by a Loss Ledger for Equal-budget Reweighting) and the hybrid
TILLER--Muon, two loss-informed training methods for Transformers.
GRAIN, Groupwise Rational Activation with Internal Normalization, learns
rational responses in normalized feature coordinates and restores each group's
input scale at its output. The shared TILLER update uses these learned
responses to construct and coordinate matrix directions. It rotates Muon
directions toward factorized adaptive
tangents, then allocates their combined norm across groups using a compact
history of directional loss responses. This allocation accounts for interactions
among updates across the network. Full TILLER maintains this coordination
throughout training; TILLER--Muon combines it with momentum-preserving Muon
continuation. Both use learned nonlinearities to represent features and guide
structured updates according to their joint effect on the loss.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The feed-forward blocks of a Transformer determine how intermediate features
are transformed and recombined. Their nonlinear responses are commonly fixed
in advance, as in GELU and the SiLU gate of SwiGLU
\citep{hendrycks2016gelu,shazeer2020glu}. Learning these responses gives the
network an additional way to adapt its representation. Trainable rational
functions provide a compact family for this purpose: they can approximate
familiar activations and learn new shapes through the same gradient updates
used to train the surrounding weights \citep{molina2020pade}.
Their compositional structure also supports approximation advantages over
fixed activation families \citep{boulle2020rational,tang2026expressivity}.
Group-shared rational functions have already made this approach practical in
Transformer feed-forward layers \citep{yang2025kat}.

A learned nonlinearity also changes the optimization problem. An update to
the incoming projection reaches the block output through the current
activation Jacobian; an update to the outgoing projection acts on the current
activated features. As the rational coefficients change, both responses
change with them. Consequently, matrix updates of the same magnitude can
produce different feature changes at different groups or stages of training.
Updates from separate groups can also reinforce or offset one another in
their effect on the loss. These relationships make the activation response
useful information for choosing an update, beyond its role in the forward
computation.

Matrix-aware optimizers offer a natural starting point for using this
information. Shampoo preconditions gradients according to their tensor
structure \citep{gupta2018shampoo}, while Muon approximately orthogonalizes
momentum updates and has been developed for language-model training
\citep{jordan2024muon,liu2025muon}. Norm-based interpretations clarify the
geometry of such updates \citep{bernstein2024old}. Activation and derivative
statistics also enter curvature-based methods such as K-FAC and the
matrix surrogate of Newton--Muon \citep{martens2015kfac,du2026newtonmuon}.
Our focus is the connection between learned nonlinear responses and the joint
effect of structured update directions. We use that connection at two levels:
to adjust the direction within a matrix block and to coordinate the strengths
of those directions across the network.

We develop full TILLER (Tangents Informed by a Loss Ledger for Equal-budget
Reweighting) and the hybrid TILLER--Muon around a shared activation and
loss-informed update construction. The activation,
GRAIN (Groupwise Rational Activation with Internal Normalization),
evaluates a separate learned rational function for each feature group
in coordinates normalized by the group's root mean square. It then restores
that scale, allowing the rational coefficients to control response shape while
the output retains the input amplitude. This construction supplies a structured
Jacobian that describes the feature changes induced by incoming updates.
The activated features provide the corresponding response for outgoing updates.

The shared TILLER update uses these responses to guide optimization.
Within each matrix group,
it constructs a norm-preserving rotation from a Muon direction toward a
factorized adaptive direction. Response participation and departure from the
initial response determine the rotation. Across groups, sampled directional
loss responses form a compact temporal ledger. A quadratic model combines
the current descent signal with the interactions recorded in this ledger to
redistribute a fixed total update norm. The optimizer can therefore change
where the structured update acts while retaining its overall magnitude.

These components provide a joint design for learning nonlinear representations
and optimizing the matrices around them. Full TILLER carries response-guided
coordination through training. TILLER--Muon combines this coordination with
ordinary Muon continuation, preserving the learned activation, matrix momentum,
and the optimizer state of the remaining parameters. The two variants share
the same activation and update construction; their schedules determine how
long loss-response information guides the matrix directions.

\section{Related Work}

Learnable activations extend the function family available to a network
beyond a fixed scalar response. PReLU learns a negative-input slope
\citep{he2015rectifiers}, whereas GELU and SwiGLU specify smooth responses
that have become common choices for feed-forward computation
\citep{hendrycks2016gelu,shazeer2020glu}. Pad\'{e} activation units learn
rational coefficients end to end and use a modified denominator to avoid
real poles \citep{molina2020pade}. Rational neural networks connect the
composition of low-degree activations to approximation efficiency
\citep{boulle2020rational}. The paper \emph{Rational Neural Networks have
Expressivity Advantages} extends this approximation-theoretic perspective
to modern smooth activation families and gated and Transformer-style
nonlinearities \citep{tang2026expressivity}. Group-shared rational functions
in the Kolmogorov--Arnold Transformer provide the closest architectural
precedent for learning distinct responses within a feed-forward block
\citep{yang2025kat}. GRAIN uses an amplitude-restoring normalization within
each learned rational response.

Normalization determines the coordinates in which an activation is learned.
Layer normalization centers and rescales within-example features
\citep{ba2016layer}, while RMS normalization uses their root mean square
without mean centering \citep{zhang2019rmsnorm}. For trainable rational
activations, the interaction also involves the coefficients themselves:
affine changes before a rational function can be absorbed into a coefficient
reparameterization \citep{tang2026expressivity}. GRAIN ties the normalization
scale to the current feature group and restores it after the rational map.
Its derivative therefore combines the elementwise rational derivative with
the response induced by a change in group scale. TILLER uses both contributions
when relating incoming matrix directions to feature changes.

Adaptive optimizers use gradient statistics to transform update directions.
AdamW separates parameter shrinkage from the adaptive gradient step
\citep{loshchilov2019decoupled}; Adafactor approximates gradient second
moments with row and column factors \citep{shazeer2018adafactor}, and CAME
adds confidence-guided scaling to factorized adaptation \citep{luo2023came}.
Shampoo uses tensor-structured preconditioners \citep{gupta2018shampoo},
and SOAP applies Adam in the corresponding preconditioner eigenbasis
\citep{vyas2025soap}. Muon instead transforms matrix momentum through
approximate orthogonalization \citep{jordan2024muon}, with connections to
spectral-norm geometry \citep{bernstein2024old} and extensions to larger
language-model training \citep{liu2025muon}. TILLER constructs its local
directions from Muon and factorized adaptive updates, with the learned
activation response controlling a rotation between them.

Activation-informed optimization and low-dimensional search provide further
context for this construction. K-FAC approximates layerwise Fisher blocks
from activations and backpropagated derivatives \citep{martens2015kfac}.
Newton--Muon uses a matrix quadratic surrogate to introduce input-second-moment
preconditioning \citep{du2026newtonmuon}. Krylov Subspace Descent optimizes
over a subspace generated from gradient and curvature information
\citep{vinyals2012krylov}. TILLER coordinates coefficients of its selected
group directions through their sampled loss responses. Its temporal response
metric uses deterministic spectral sketching from Frequent Directions and
the isotropic correction of Robust Frequent Directions
\citep{ghashami2016frequent,luo2019robust}. The resulting quadratic model
couples directions across groups through their joint loss effects.

Training schedules offer another way to combine optimization behavior.
SWATS transitions from Adam to SGD using a projection-based trigger
\citep{keskar2017switching}; Schedule-Free optimization uses iterate averaging
to alter the role of learning-rate scheduling \citep{defazio2024schedulefree}.
TILLER--Muon combines response-guided coordination with matrix-level Muon
continuation, transferring momentum while retaining GRAIN and the AdamW state.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Full TILLER and TILLER--Muon share a response-guided update construction
for Transformers with GRAIN activations. Full TILLER uses this construction
throughout training; TILLER--Muon combines it with a state-preserving Muon
continuation. We first define their common activation, then develop the
update rule and the two training schedules.

\subsection{GRAIN: groupwise rational activations}

GRAIN (Groupwise Rational Activation with Internal Normalization) gives
each feature group a learned nonlinear response. It separates response
shape from input amplitude: a rational function acts on normalized
features, and the group scale is restored at its output. For an input
$x\in\mathbb R^d$, split the intermediate features into $G$ groups of width
$w$, with incoming and outgoing projections
$A_g\in\mathbb R^{w\times d}$ and $B_g\in\mathbb R^{d\times w}$.
The block output is
\begin{equation}
 y=\sum_{g=1}^G B_g h_g,\qquad
 h_g=\rho_g\phi_g(u_g),\qquad
 u_g=\frac{A_gx}{\rho_g},\quad
 \rho_g=\sqrt{\|A_gx\|_2^2/w+\epsilon_{\rm act}}.
 \label{eq:overview-grain}
\end{equation}
Here $\rho_g$ is the stabilized group root mean square (RMS),
$u_g$ contains the normalized features, and $\phi_g$ acts elementwise:
\begin{equation}
 \phi_g(u)=
 \frac{\sum_{j=0}^{5}a_{gj}u^j}
 {1+\sum_{j=1}^{4}|b_{gj}|\,|u|^j}.
 \label{eq:overview-rational}
\end{equation}
The learned coefficients $a_{gj},b_{gj}$ control the response shape
\citep{molina2020pade,boulle2020rational}, and the positive denominator
excludes real poles. Internal RMS normalization \citep{zhang2019rmsnorm}
lets the rational function operate on relative feature magnitudes as
their absolute scale changes. Restoring $\rho_g$ preserves amplitude
information for the outgoing projection. All groups start from a common
SiLU approximation and subsequently learn separate shapes. With
$Gw=3H/2$, the two projections contain the same number of dense weights
as a SwiGLU block of intermediate width $H$ \citep{shazeer2020glu}.

The two projections expose complementary information about a proposed
update. An incoming direction changes the features through the
activation Jacobian $J_g=\partial h_g/\partial(A_gx)$; an outgoing
direction acts directly on $h_g$. TILLER uses both paths to assess a
direction in the context of the learned nonlinearity. The Jacobian and
its interpretation are derived in Appendix~\ref{sec:grain}.

\subsection{Full and hybrid TILLER optimization}

TILLER (Tangents Informed by a Loss Ledger for Equal-budget Reweighting)
makes two linked choices: the orientation of each matrix update and
the strengths with which group updates act together. Let $i=(\ell,g)$
index the $C=LG$ layer--group pairs in a network of depth $L$.
For either projection role, form a Muon direction $p_i$ and a
factorized adaptive direction $a_i$ from the same momentum
\citep{jordan2024muon,liu2025muon,shazeer2018adafactor}.
The former rebalances matrix singular values; the latter uses row and
column gradient histories to rescale coordinates. Removing the component
of $a_i$ parallel to $p_i$ gives
$a_i^\perp=a_i-\inner{a_i}{p_i}p_i/\frob{p_i}^2$.
This orthogonal component provides an alternative orientation without
changing the norm of the parent direction.

The activation response sets the amount of rotation. A participation
statistic $\pi_i$ measures the breadth of the response, using the
singular values of $J_i$ for an incoming update and the coordinates
of $h_i$ for an outgoing update. A congruence statistic $\kappa_i$
compares the learned response with its initialization at the same
current inputs. Both emphasize positions with greater loss sensitivity.
The fraction $e_i=\pi_i(1-\kappa_i^2)$ allocates more direction energy
to the tangent when a broad response has departed from its initial
geometry. The candidate is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde q_i=
 \sqrt{1-e_i}\,p_i+
 \sigma_i\sqrt{e_i}\,
 \frac{\frob{p_i}}{\frob{a_i^\perp}}a_i^\perp,
 \qquad
 \sigma_i=\operatorname{sign}\inner{g_i}{a_i^\perp}.
 \label{eq:overview-chord}
\end{equation}
Here $g_i$ is the clipped gradient, $\operatorname{sign}(0)=1$, and
$0\leq e_i\leq1$. The sign orients the tangent toward descent;
orthogonality gives $\frob{\widetilde q_i}=\frob{p_i}$.
The selected direction $q_i$ is the candidate when both it and the
parent have positive gradient contraction, and is the parent otherwise.
Appendix~\ref{sec:directions} specifies the statistics and selection.

The resulting directions $q_i^A,q_i^B$ are then coordinated through
their loss responses. At a shared example--token position $k$, let
$x_{k\ell}$ be the block input, $h_{ki}$ its group features, and
$\xi_{k\ell}$ the downstream loss derivative at the block output.
The scalar response of the paired group direction is
\begin{equation}
 S_{ki}=
 \inner{\xi_{k\ell}}
 {B_iJ_{ki}q_i^Ax_{k\ell}+q_i^Bh_{ki}}.
 \label{eq:overview-score}
\end{equation}
Each column of $S$ describes one group across the same probes. For a
coefficient vector $c$, $Sc$ is their combined response. Its squared
norm captures reinforcement and cancellation between group contributions,
including contributions from different layers. TILLER retains a
discounted history of these measurements in a loss ledger. A Robust
Frequent Directions sketch preserves dominant response combinations
and an isotropic remainder \citep{ghashami2016frequent,luo2019robust}.

Let $G_t$ be the resulting response metric, $r_t$ its cross term with
decoupled weight decay, and
$s_{t,i}=\inner{g_i^A}{q_i^A}+\inner{g_i^B}{q_i^B}$
the current minibatch descent signal. At learning rate $\eta$, the
coefficients are chosen using the quadratic surrogate
\begin{equation}
 F_t(c)=-\eta s_t^\top c+
 \frac{\eta^2}{2}\bigl(c^\top G_tc+2r_t^\top c\bigr),
 \qquad
 \sum_i\omega_ic_i^2=\sum_i\omega_i,\quad
 \omega_i=\frob{q_i^A}^2+\frob{q_i^B}^2.
 \label{eq:overview-coordinate-model}
\end{equation}
The linear term rewards current descent; the quadratic term measures
the joint response through the ledger. The constraint redistributes
a fixed squared direction norm across groups. Coefficients can
strengthen, attenuate, or reverse a group according to its contribution
to the joint proposal. The metric is applied through its sketch factor,
and a projected solver selects coefficients that improve this surrogate
over $c=\boldsymbol1$ while preserving layerwise descent.

The selected coefficients update
$A_i\leftarrow(1-\eta\lambda_{\rm wd})A_i-\eta c_iq_i^A$
and analogously for $B_i$, where $\lambda_{\rm wd}$ is weight decay.
Attention uses the same response-guided rotation with layerwise
statistics. Rational coefficients and the remaining parameter classes
use AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019decoupled}.

Let $T$ be the training horizon and $T_s\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$ the
duration of loss-informed coordination. Full TILLER uses $T_s=T$.
TILLER--Muon uses $T_s<T$: it applies TILLER for the first $T_s$ updates and
then continues with ordinary Muon on the full feed-forward and attention
matrices. The transition transfers the first-moment buffers, retains
the AdamW state, and continues the same learning-rate schedule.
GRAIN and its learned coefficients remain active throughout. The hybrid
reduces ongoing coordination work while retaining the learned
representation and gradient history. It provides a time-saving
continuation when later-stage gains from further coordination are small.
The switch point can be chosen for the training regime.
