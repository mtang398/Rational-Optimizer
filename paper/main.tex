\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We introduce a learned activation and an optimizer for Transformer
feed-forward blocks. GRAIN (Groupwise Rational Activation with Internal
Normalization) learns a separate nonlinear response for each feature group.
TILLER (Tangents Informed by a Loss Ledger for Equal-budget Reweighting)
uses these responses to guide matrix updates and coordinate them across
the network. The activation's local geometry maps parameter directions
to feature changes and their contributions to the loss.

\subsection{GRAIN: learning groupwise activation responses}

The distribution and scale of intermediate features change during
training. GRAIN evaluates a learned rational function in normalized
coordinates, then restores the input scale. For a feed-forward input
$x\in\mathbb R^d$, split the intermediate features into $G$ groups of width
$w$, with incoming and outgoing projections
$A_g\in\mathbb R^{w\times d}$ and $B_g\in\mathbb R^{d\times w}$.
The block output is
\begin{equation}
 y=\sum_{g=1}^G B_g h_g,\qquad
 h_g=\rho_g\phi_g(u_g),\qquad
 u_g=\frac{A_gx}{\rho_g},\quad
 \rho_g=\sqrt{\|A_gx\|_2^2/w+\epsilon_{\rm act}}.
 \label{eq:overview-grain}
\end{equation}
Here $\rho_g$ is the stabilized group root mean square (RMS),
$u_g$ contains the normalized features, and $\phi_g$ acts elementwise:
\begin{equation}
 \phi_g(u)=
 \frac{\sum_{j=0}^{5}a_{gj}u^j}
 {1+\sum_{j=1}^{4}|b_{gj}|\,|u|^j}.
 \label{eq:overview-rational}
\end{equation}
The learned coefficients $a_{gj},b_{gj}$ control the response shape
\citep{molina2020pade,boulle2020rational}; the denominator is at least one.
Internal RMS normalization \citep{zhang2019rmsnorm} makes relative feature
magnitudes the arguments of this function. Multiplication by $\rho_g$
then carries the group's amplitude into its output. Every group starts
from the same SiLU approximation and learns its own response.
Setting $Gw=3H/2$ gives the two projections the same dense weight count
as a SwiGLU block with intermediate width $H$ \citep{shazeer2020glu}.

GRAIN also provides a direct description of how an update changes its
features. Let $J_g=\partial h_g/\partial(A_gx)$. An incoming direction
$q_g^A$ changes the features by $J_gq_g^Ax$, whereas an outgoing direction
$q_g^B$ acts on the features as $q_g^Bh_g$. The Jacobian contains the
elementwise derivative and a correction for the change in group scale:
\begin{equation}
 J_g=\operatorname{Diag}(\phi'_g(u_g))
 +\frac{1}{w}
 \bigl[\phi_g(u_g)-u_g\odot\phi'_g(u_g)\bigr]u_g^\top,
 \label{eq:overview-jacobian}
\end{equation}
where $\odot$ denotes elementwise multiplication. These two response
paths, through $J_g$ and through $h_g$, provide the information used by
TILLER. Appendix~\ref{sec:grain} derives the Jacobian.

\subsection{TILLER: constructing and coordinating loss-informed updates}

TILLER first chooses a direction within each matrix block, then chooses
how strongly the blocks should act together. Index the $C=LG$
layer--group pairs by $i=(\ell,g)$, where $L$ is the Transformer depth.
For either matrix role, let $g_i$ be its clipped loss gradient,
$p_i$ its Muon direction, and $a_i$ a direction formed by rescaling the
same momentum with factorized gradient second moments
\citep{jordan2024muon,liu2025muon,shazeer2018adafactor}.
The component
$a_i^\perp=a_i-\inner{a_i}{p_i}p_i/\frob{p_i}^2$
is orthogonal to the Muon direction. It supplies a tangent along which
TILLER can rotate that direction at fixed Frobenius norm.

The rotation depends on how the learned activation currently responds.
A participation statistic $\pi_i$ measures how broadly response energy
is distributed: across singular directions of $J_i$ for the incoming
matrix, and across feature coordinates of $h_i$ for the outgoing matrix.
A congruence statistic $\kappa_i$ measures alignment with the initial
activation's response, evaluated at the current inputs. Both statistics
emphasize positions with greater downstream loss sensitivity.
With $e_i=\pi_i(1-\kappa_i^2)$, TILLER forms
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde q_i=
 \sqrt{1-e_i}\,p_i+
 \sigma_i\sqrt{e_i}\,
 \frac{\frob{p_i}}{\frob{a_i^\perp}}a_i^\perp,
 \qquad
 \sigma_i=\operatorname{sign}\inner{g_i}{a_i^\perp}.
 \label{eq:overview-chord}
\end{equation}
Here $e_i\in[0,1]$ is the fraction of squared direction norm assigned to
the tangent, and $\operatorname{sign}(0)=1$ for its orientation.
A broad response that has changed from initialization permits a larger
rotation toward the adaptive direction.
Orthogonality gives $\frob{\widetilde q_i}=\frob{p_i}$.
TILLER selects this candidate when it retains positive gradient
contraction, with the precise selection rule and response statistics
in Appendix~\ref{sec:directions}.

The selected incoming and outgoing directions, now denoted
$q_i^A,q_i^B$, affect the loss jointly with directions in other groups.
To measure these interactions, TILLER samples the same example--token
positions across layers. At probe $k$, let $x_{k\ell}$ be the block input,
$h_{ki}$ its group features, and $\xi_{k\ell}$ the downstream loss
gradient at the block output. The group's scalar loss response is
\begin{equation}
 S_{ki}=
 \inner{\xi_{k\ell}}
 {B_iJ_{ki}q_i^Ax_{k\ell}+q_i^Bh_{ki}}.
 \label{eq:overview-score}
\end{equation}
Each column records one group direction's responses across probes.
For group coefficients $c$, the vector $Sc$ combines those responses;
the cross terms in $\|Sc\|_2^2$ capture effects that reinforce or offset
each other. Appendix~\ref{sec:loss-measurements} derives this measurement
and its relation to the minibatch gradient.

TILLER maintains a discounted second moment of these responses in a
loss ledger, using Robust Frequent Directions to retain dominant
response combinations and an isotropic spectral remainder
\citep{ghashami2016frequent,luo2019robust}.
Let $G_t$ be the resulting response metric, $r_t$ its cross term with
decoupled weight decay, and
$s_{t,i}=\inner{g_i^A}{q_i^A}+\inner{g_i^B}{q_i^B}$
the current minibatch descent signal. At learning rate $\eta$, the
coefficients are chosen using the quadratic surrogate
\begin{equation}
 F_t(c)=-\eta s_t^\top c+
 \frac{\eta^2}{2}\bigl(c^\top G_tc+2r_t^\top c\bigr),
 \qquad
 \sum_i\omega_ic_i^2=\sum_i\omega_i,\quad
 \omega_i=\frob{q_i^A}^2+\frob{q_i^B}^2.
 \label{eq:overview-coordinate-model}
\end{equation}
The linear term rewards current descent, while the quadratic term
penalizes the magnitude of the combined loss response.
The constraint preserves the total squared norm of the structured
direction. Thus equal-budget reweighting allocates this norm among
groups according to their joint loss effects.
Signed coefficients can strengthen, attenuate, or reverse a group.

A projected solver proposes coefficients, which are evaluated in the
full surrogate and checked for layerwise descent
(Appendix~\ref{sec:transaction}). The selected coefficients update
$A_i\leftarrow(1-\eta\lambda_{\rm wd})A_i-\eta c_iq_i^A$
and analogously for $B_i$, where $\lambda_{\rm wd}$ is weight decay.
Attention uses the same response-guided rotation with layerwise
statistics. Rational coefficients and the remaining parameter classes
use AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019decoupled}.
Appendices~\ref{sec:ledger} and~\ref{app:algorithm} give the ledger,
solver, and complete update.

\section{GRAIN: normalized responses and their derivatives}
\label{sec:grain}
\label{app:grain-calculus}

GRAIN learns an activation shape in coordinates defined by the input
group's own scale. This separates two aspects of the forward map: the
rational function determines how relative feature values are transformed,
and the group RMS carries their amplitude through the transformation.
The derivative of this map describes the two paths through which the
incoming and outgoing matrix directions affect the block output.

For one input $x\in\mathbb R^d$, the feed-forward block computes
$z=Ax$, $h=\Phi(z)$, and $y=Bh$, where
$A\in\mathbb R^{H_r\times d}$, $B\in\mathbb R^{d\times H_r}$,
and $H_r=Gw$. A group consists of a row block $A_g$, the corresponding
features $z_g=A_gx\in\mathbb R^w$, and a column block $B_g$.
Its normalized input and output are
\begin{equation}
 \rho_g=\sqrt{\|z_g\|_2^2/w+\epsilon_{\rm act}},\qquad
 u_g=z_g/\rho_g,\qquad
 h_g=\Phi_g(z_g)=\rho_g\phi_g(u_g).
 \label{eq:grain-normalize}
\end{equation}
The block sums the group contributions as $y=\sum_g B_gh_g$.
Each $\phi_g$ has the rational form in
equation~\ref{eq:overview-rational}, applied elementwise with
group-specific coefficients. Its denominator is at least one for
every real input, so finite coefficients introduce no real poles.
All groups begin from a common SiLU fit and subsequently learn
independently. Appendix~\ref{app:implementation} specifies the
initial coefficients.

An incoming update changes both $u_g$ and $\rho_g$.
Suppressing the group index, differentiation of
$\rho=(z^\top z/w+\epsilon_{\rm act})^{1/2}$ and $u=z/\rho$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_z\rho=u/w,\qquad
 \frac{\partial u}{\partial z}
 =\rho^{-1}\left(I-\frac{uu^\top}{w}\right).
 \label{eq:grain-normalization-derivative}
\end{equation}
Let $f=\phi(u)$, $d=\phi'(u)$, and $r=f-u\odot d$.
The product rule for $\Phi(z)=\rho\phi(u)$ now gives
\begin{align}
 J=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z}
 &=\frac{fu^\top}{w}
   +\operatorname{Diag}(d)\left(I-\frac{uu^\top}{w}\right)
   \nonumber\\
 &=\operatorname{Diag}(d)+\frac{ru^\top}{w}.
 \label{eq:grain-jacobian}
\end{align}
These identities hold wherever $\phi$ is differentiable. At zero
coordinates, the absolute-value terms use the automatic-differentiation
selection $\operatorname{sign}(0)=0$; $J$ denotes that selected derivative
there.

The diagonal term applies the local slope separately to each feature.
The second term accounts for their shared change in scale.
For a linear response $\phi(u)=a u$, $r=0$ and $J=aI$:
the normalization and scale restoration cancel. For a nonlinear
response, $r$ records the difference between the function value and its
local linear contribution. Thus the same learned shape determines both
the transformed features and their sensitivity to a perturbation.

For a feature perturbation $v$ and a downstream derivative $b$, the two
useful forms of this Jacobian are
\begin{equation}
 Jv=d\odot v+r\,\frac{u^\top v}{w},\qquad
 J^\top b=d\odot b+u\,\frac{r^\top b}{w}.
 \label{eq:grain-jacobian-actions}
\end{equation}
The first maps an incoming perturbation to a feature change; the second
maps loss sensitivity back through the activation. An update to $B_g$
instead acts directly on $h_g$. This distinction explains why TILLER
uses Jacobian geometry for incoming directions and feature geometry
for outgoing directions.

\section{TILLER: from activation geometry to coordinated updates}

TILLER uses the activation response at two levels. Within a matrix
block, the response determines a rotation between a Muon direction
and a direction with adaptive coordinate scaling. Across blocks,
loss responses determine how those directions should combine.
The first construction supplies a direction for each group; the
second assigns coefficients to the resulting collection.

\subsection{Constructing response-guided directions}
\label{sec:directions}

Index the layer--group pairs by $i=(\ell,g)$ and the two projection
roles by $r\in\{A,B\}$. For each role, let $g_i^r$ be the current clipped
gradient and $m_i^r$ its Nesterov momentum. We construct a Muon direction
$p_i^r$ from the approximate polar factor of this momentum, and an
adaptive direction $a_i^r$ from the same momentum divided by
factorized gradient second moments
\citep{jordan2024muon,bernstein2024old,liu2025muon,shazeer2018adafactor}.
The two directions retain different information: the polar map
rebalances singular values, while the adaptive scaling reflects
the history of individual row and column gradient magnitudes.
Their numerical definitions are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}.

The adaptive direction can change the orientation of $p_i^r$ through
its orthogonal component. Suppressing the role index, define
\begin{equation}
 a_i^\perp=a_i-\frac{\inner{a_i}{p_i}}{\frob{p_i}^2}p_i,
 \qquad \frob{p_i}>0,
 \label{eq:tangent-orientation}
\end{equation}
where $\inner{\cdot}{\cdot}$ is the Frobenius inner product for matrices.
This tangent determines a plane of possible rotations.
We choose the rotation magnitude from two complementary properties
of the learned activation: the breadth of its response and its
departure from the initial response.

Both properties are measured on shared example--token probes.
For probe $k$, write $h_{ki}$ and $J_{ki}$ for the group features
and activation Jacobian, and let $\xi_{k\ell}\in\mathbb R^d$ be the
downstream loss derivative at the block output. An incoming update
acts through $J_{ki}$, whose squared singular values describe its
response energy. An outgoing update acts on $h_{ki}$, whose squared
coordinates describe the available feature energy. The corresponding
participation ratios are
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{pr}_A(J)
 =\frac{[\operatorname{tr}(JJ^\top)]^2}
        {w\frob{JJ^\top}^2},\qquad
 \operatorname{pr}_B(h)=\frac{\|h\|_2^4}{w\|h\|_4^4}.
 \label{eq:response-summary-components}
\end{equation}
Each ratio equals $1/w$ when all energy lies in one component and
one when it is uniform across the $w$ components. For example,
if exactly $m$ singular directions carry equal energy,
$\operatorname{pr}_A=m/w$. Participation therefore expresses
response breadth on a common scale for the two matrix roles.

We weight these measurements by the loss sensitivity of the
corresponding path:
\begin{equation}
 \alpha^A_{ki}=\|J_{ki}^\top B_i^\top\xi_{k\ell}\|_2^2/w,
 \qquad
 \alpha^B_{ki}=\|\xi_{k\ell}\|_2^2/d.
 \label{eq:response-sensitivity}
\end{equation}
The weighted participation is
\begin{equation}
 \pi_i^A=
 \frac{\sum_k\alpha^A_{ki}\operatorname{pr}_A(J_{ki})}
      {\sum_k\alpha^A_{ki}},\qquad
 \pi_i^B=
 \frac{\sum_k\alpha^B_{ki}\operatorname{pr}_B(h_{ki})}
      {\sum_k\alpha^B_{ki}}.
 \label{eq:participation-main}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the rotation reflects the response at positions where
the loss is sensitive to changes in that group.

To measure learned change, compare the current response with the
initial rational function evaluated at the same current input.
Define response kernels
$\Gamma^A_{ki}=J_{ki}J_{ki}^\top$ and
$\Gamma^B_{ki}=h_{ki}h_{ki}^\top$, and let
$\Gamma^{r,0}_{ki}$ use the initial rational coefficients.
All other quantities, including the input and its RMS scale,
remain at their current values. Their weighted congruence is
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_i^r=
 \frac{\sum_k\alpha^r_{ki}
       \inner{\Gamma^r_{ki}}{\Gamma^{r,0}_{ki}}}
 {\sqrt{\left(\sum_k\alpha^r_{ki}\frob{\Gamma^r_{ki}}^2\right)
        \left(\sum_k\alpha^r_{ki}\frob{\Gamma^{r,0}_{ki}}^2\right)}}.
 \label{eq:congruence-main}
\end{equation}
This is a cosine between collections of weighted response kernels.
It equals one when the two collections agree up to a common
positive scale. Comparing them at the same input ties this
measurement to the learned activation shape.

Participation and congruence together set the fraction of direction
energy assigned to the adaptive tangent:
\begin{equation}
 e_i^r=\pi_i^r\bigl(1-(\kappa_i^r)^2\bigr).
 \label{eq:departure-energy}
\end{equation}
A response spread over many components permits a larger rotation
when its geometry has changed from initialization. A concentrated
response reduces that rotation through $\pi_i^r$; an unchanged
response gives $e_i^r=0$ through $\kappa_i^r$.
Both factors are evaluated separately for incoming and outgoing
directions.

\label{app:response-proof}
The bounds needed for this construction follow directly from the
definitions. For any nonnegative values $v_j$,
$\sum_jv_j^2\leq(\sum_jv_j)^2\leq w\sum_jv_j^2$.
Taking $v_j$ to be squared singular values of $J$, or squared
coordinates of $h$, gives
$\operatorname{pr}_A,\operatorname{pr}_B\in[1/w,1]$
for nonzero responses. Congruence is at most one by
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the weighted kernels. Its numerator is
nonnegative because
$\inner{JJ^\top}{J^0J^{0\top}}=\frob{J^\top J^0}^2$
and
$\inner{hh^\top}{h^0h^{0\top}}=(h^\top h^0)^2$.
Thus $0\leq e_i^r\leq1$. Zero-response conventions appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}.

We can now form the rotated direction. With role indices
suppressed again, choose
$\sigma_i=1$ when $\inner{g_i}{a_i^\perp}\geq0$ and $-1$ otherwise,
and set
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde q_i=
 \sqrt{1-e_i}\,p_i+
 \sigma_i\sqrt{e_i}\frac{\frob{p_i}}{\frob{a_i^\perp}}a_i^\perp.
 \label{eq:tangent-chord}
\end{equation}
The sign makes the tangent contribution agree with current descent.
Writing $e_i=\sin^2\vartheta_i$ reveals the construction as a
rotation through angle $\vartheta_i$ in the plane of $p_i$ and
$a_i^\perp$.

\begin{proposition}[Norm-preserving rotation]
For $e_i\in[0,1]$, $p_i\neq0$, and $a_i^\perp\neq0$, the candidate
in equation~\ref{eq:tangent-chord} satisfies
$\frob{\widetilde q_i}=\frob{p_i}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\ref{eq:tangent-orientation} gives
$\inner{p_i}{a_i^\perp}=0$. Expanding the squared norm therefore yields
\[
 \frob{\widetilde q_i}^2
 =(1-e_i)\frob{p_i}^2
 +e_i\frac{\frob{p_i}^2}{\frob{a_i^\perp}^2}
       \frob{a_i^\perp}^2
 =\frob{p_i}^2.
\]
\end{proof}

The selected direction $q_i$ is $\widetilde q_i$ when the candidate
is finite and both
$\inner{g_i}{p_i}>0$ and $\inner{g_i}{\widetilde q_i}>0$;
otherwise it is $p_i$. A zero parent or tangent also selects $p_i$.
\label{eq:chord-selection}
Either selection preserves the parent norm.
The resulting pair $(q_i^A,q_i^B)$ now defines one structured
direction whose strength can be coordinated with the other groups.

\subsection{A joint loss model for group updates}
\label{sec:loss-measurements}

The directions constructed above occupy disjoint parameter blocks,
but their effects on the loss overlap. A coefficient chosen for one
group therefore changes the context in which the other groups act.
TILLER measures this interaction by evaluating all group directions
at shared activation positions.

For probe $k$ and coordinate $i=(\ell,g)$, let $x_{k\ell}$ be the
block input, $h_{ki}=\Phi_i(A_ix_{k\ell})$ the group features, and
$\xi_{k\ell}$ the downstream loss derivative at the block output.
The derivative is expressed on the clipped summed-loss scale
specified in Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}.
Perturbing the incoming and outgoing projections along
$(q_i^A,q_i^B)$ produces
\begin{equation}
 \delta z_{ki}=q_i^Ax_{k\ell},\qquad
 \delta h_{ki}=J_{ki}\delta z_{ki},\qquad
 \delta y_{ki}=B_i\delta h_{ki}+q_i^Bh_{ki}.
 \label{eq:directional-path}
\end{equation}
The last expression follows from differentiating
$(B_i+\alpha q_i^B)\Phi_i((A_i+\alpha q_i^A)x_{k\ell})$
at $\alpha=0$. Its first term follows the incoming perturbation
through the learned activation; its second term changes the
outgoing projection of the current features. Pairing both terms
with $\xi_{k\ell}$ gives the group's loss response:
\begin{align}
 S_{t,ki}
 &=\inner{\xi_{k\ell}}{B_iJ_{ki}q_i^Ax_{k\ell}+q_i^Bh_{ki}}
 \nonumber\\
 &=\inner{q_i^Ax_{k\ell}}{J_{ki}^\top B_i^\top\xi_{k\ell}}
   +\inner{q_i^Bh_{ki}}{\xi_{k\ell}}.
 \label{eq:functional-score}
\end{align}
The second form uses the Jacobian action in
equation~\ref{eq:grain-jacobian-actions}.
At differentiability points, each score is the contribution from
one activation position to the directional loss derivative.
Averaging over all positions recovers the clipped minibatch
derivative. The probe matrix $S_t\in\mathbb R^{K\times C}$,
where $C=LG$, retains $K$ such positions across all group
directions. The exact current minibatch derivative for each
coordinate is also available from the parameter gradients:
\begin{equation}
 s_{t,i}=\inner{g_{t,i}^A}{q_{t,i}^A}
        +\inner{g_{t,i}^B}{q_{t,i}^B}.
 \label{eq:current-score}
\end{equation}
This contraction supplies the linear descent term, while the
probe rows describe how contributions vary together.

For coefficients $c\in\mathbb R^C$, the combined probe response is
$S_tc$. The quantity $\|S_tc\|_2^2/K$ depends on cross products
between its columns. To illustrate what these products reveal,
consider two probes and two group responses. Columns $(2,0)^\top$
and $(0,2)^\top$ each have mean one, and their sum has mean squared
response four. If both columns are $(2,0)^\top$, the individual
means are unchanged but the combined mean squared response is
eight. The response metric distinguishes these combinations
through their overlap across probes.

Decoupled weight decay contributes to the same output perturbation.
Applying equation~\ref{eq:functional-score} to
$(\lambda_{\rm wd}A_i,\lambda_{\rm wd}B_i)$ and summing over
groups gives its probe response $a_t^{\rm dec}\in\mathbb R^K$.
The total response penalty expands as
\begin{equation}
 \frac1K\|S_tc+a_t^{\rm dec}\|_2^2
 =c^\top\frac{S_t^\top S_t}{K}c
  +2c^\top\frac{S_t^\top a_t^{\rm dec}}{K}
  +\frac{\|a_t^{\rm dec}\|_2^2}{K}.
 \label{eq:functional-response-energy}
\end{equation}
The last term is constant in $c$. The first two terms motivate
a response metric $G_t$ and a score--decay cross term $r_t$.
TILLER estimates them from a discounted history of measurements,
as described in the next subsection.

The coordination problem balances current descent against this
joint response penalty. Let
$\omega_i=\frob{q_i^A}^2+\frob{q_i^B}^2$ and
$\mathcal E_0=\sum_i\omega_i$. Because the coordinates occupy
disjoint parameter blocks, their structured update satisfies
\[
 \|\Delta\theta(c)\|_2^2
 =\eta^2\sum_i\omega_i c_i^2,\qquad
 \Delta\theta(c)=-\eta\{c_iq_i^A,c_iq_i^B\}_{i=1}^C.
\]
Thus $\sum_i\omega_ic_i^2=\mathcal E_0$ preserves the squared
norm of the uncoordinated direction $c=\boldsymbol1$.
At learning rate $\eta>0$, TILLER uses the surrogate
\begin{align}
 F_t(c)&=-\eta s_t^\top c+
 \frac{\eta^2}{2}\left(c^\top G_tc+2r_t^\top c\right),
 \label{eq:transaction-objective}\\
 \text{with}\quad
 &\sum_i\omega_ic_i^2=\mathcal E_0.
 \label{eq:transaction-budget}
\end{align}
The linear term favors combinations aligned with the current
minibatch gradient. The quadratic term penalizes their joint
squared loss response, including interaction with decay.
Fixing the norm makes the choice depend on how that norm is
distributed among groups. Signed coefficients permit a group
to reverse when that change benefits the joint proposal.


\subsection{Maintaining the loss ledger}
\label{sec:ledger}

The coefficient model needs both a current descent signal and
a description of how directions interact. TILLER obtains the
first from $s_t$ on every minibatch. It obtains the second from
shared probes and carries those measurements forward in a
discounted loss ledger.

The shared probes are refreshed on step 1 and every eight steps
thereafter, using $K=32$ example--token positions. Between
refreshes, the ledger receives a rank-one measurement aligned
with the current minibatch contraction. Let
$\nu=\|S_{\rm ref}\|_{\rm F}/\sqrt K$ be the RMS row norm at
the latest probe refresh. Define
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_t=
 \begin{cases}
 \nu/\|s_t\|_2,&\|s_t\|_2>0,\\
 0,&\|s_t\|_2=0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 S_t=\boldsymbol1_K(\gamma_ts_t)^\top,\qquad
 a_t^{\rm dec}=\boldsymbol1_K\gamma_td_t^{\rm dec},
 \label{eq:trace-matched-rows}
\end{equation}
where $d_t^{\rm dec}$ is the current gradient contraction
with the GRAIN decay direction.
When $s_t\neq0$,
$\|S_t\|_{\rm F}^2=K\gamma_t^2\|s_t\|_2^2
=\|S_{\rm ref}\|_{\rm F}^2$.
This preserves the trace of the latest probe second moment
while updating its orientation to the current descent signal.
Probe refreshes restore variation across positions; intervening
steps supply fresh information along $s_t$.

The ledger's uncompressed target is
\begin{equation}
 G_t^\star=\beta_2G_{t-1}^\star+
 (1-\beta_2)S_t^\top S_t/K,\qquad
 G_1^\star=S_1^\top S_1/K.
 \label{eq:ledger-target}
\end{equation}
For any $c$, $c^\top G_t^\star c$ is a discounted average
of squared responses of that coefficient combination.
The recorded responses use the parameters and directions
from their respective steps. The discount therefore gives
greater weight to the geometry encountered more recently.

TILLER represents this history using a factor $\mathcal B_t$
and an isotropic remainder $\tau_tI$:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat G_t=\mathcal B_t^\top\mathcal B_t/K+\tau_tI.
 \label{eq:rfd-metric}
\end{equation}
The factor represents dominant combinations of group responses.
The remainder assigns a common contribution to all coefficient
directions, including those outside the factor's span.
Given the previous history, the current rows enter through
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A_t=
 \begin{bmatrix}
 \sqrt{\beta_2}\mathcal B_{t-1}\\
 \sqrt{1-\beta_2}S_t
 \end{bmatrix},\qquad
 G_t=\mathcal A_t^\top\mathcal A_t/K+\beta_2\tau_{t-1}I.
 \label{eq:current-ledger-metric}
\end{equation}
This $G_t$ supplies the current coefficient model.
The cross term follows the same discount:
\begin{equation}
 r_t=\beta_2r_{t-1}+
 (1-\beta_2)S_t^\top a_t^{\rm dec}/K.
 \label{eq:decay-cross}
\end{equation}
At the first step,
$\mathcal A_1=\mathcal B_1=S_1$, $\tau_0=\tau_1=0$, and
$r_1=S_1^\top a_1^{\rm dec}/K$.

To carry the new history to the next step, TILLER compresses
$\mathcal A_t$ with Robust Frequent Directions
\citep{ghashami2016frequent,luo2019robust}.
It retains at most $r=64$ singular directions and subtracts
the largest discarded squared singular value $\Delta_t$
from each retained squared singular value.
The isotropic remainder then updates as
\begin{equation}
 \tau_t=\beta_2\tau_{t-1}+\frac{\Delta_t}{2K}.
 \label{eq:rfd-tail}
\end{equation}
The new rows thus enter the current model at full resolution,
and their compressed representation becomes part of the
next step's history.

The half-threshold in equation~\ref{eq:rfd-tail} has a
geometric interpretation. After shrinkage, the omitted
contribution lies between zero and $\Delta_t/K$ in every
direction. Assigning the midpoint of this interval limits
the largest absolute error. The following result quantifies
its effect on the response metric.
\label{app:rfd-proof}

\begin{proposition}[Error of the compressed response metric]
Let $K>0$, $0\leq\beta_2\leq1$, $\tau_{t-1}\geq0$, and
$\mathcal A=U\operatorname{Diag}(\sigma)V^\top$,
with singular values in nonincreasing order.
If $\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal A)>r$, set
$\Delta=\sigma_{r+1}^2$ and
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B=
 \operatorname{Diag}\!\left(
 \sqrt{\sigma_1^2-\Delta},\ldots,\sqrt{\sigma_r^2-\Delta}
 \right)V_{1:r}^\top.
 \label{eq:rfd-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
Otherwise set $\Delta=0$ and choose a factor with at most
$r$ rows satisfying
$\mathcal B^\top\mathcal B=\mathcal A^\top\mathcal A$.
For
\[
 G=\mathcal A^\top\mathcal A/K+\beta_2\tau_{t-1}I,\qquad
 \widehat G=\mathcal B^\top\mathcal B/K+
       \left(\beta_2\tau_{t-1}+\frac{\Delta}{2K}\right)I,
\]
we have
\begin{equation}
 \widehat G\succeq0,\qquad
 \|\widehat G-G\|_2\leq\frac{\Delta}{2K}.
 \label{eq:rfd-error}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The retained squared singular values and the isotropic
remainder are nonnegative, so $\widehat G$ is positive
semidefinite. If $\Delta=0$, the two metrics coincide.
Otherwise, on a retained right-singular direction,
shrinkage subtracts $\Delta/K$ and the remainder adds
$\Delta/(2K)$, for a net error of $-\Delta/(2K)$.
On a discarded direction with squared singular value
$\lambda\in[0,\Delta]$, the error is
$(\Delta/2-\lambda)/K$.
A null direction has error $\Delta/(2K)$.
These directions form an orthonormal basis, and every
absolute eigenvalue error is at most $\Delta/(2K)$.
\end{proof}

The bound translates directly to the penalty used for
coordination. For any coefficient vector $c$,
\begin{equation}
 |c^\top\widehat Gc-c^\top Gc|
 \leq\|\widehat G-G\|_2\|c\|_2^2
 \leq\frac{\Delta}{2K}\|c\|_2^2.
 \label{eq:rfd-response-error}
\end{equation}
It also tracks error over the discounted history.
Writing $E_t=\widehat G_t-G_t^\star$, each compression
adds an error $D_t$ with
$\|D_t\|_2\leq\Delta_t/(2K)$, so
$E_t=\beta_2E_{t-1}+D_t$ and $E_1=0$. Iteration yields
\begin{equation}
 \|E_t\|_2\leq
 \sum_{j=2}^t\beta_2^{\,t-j}\frac{\Delta_j}{2K}.
 \label{eq:rfd-history-error}
\end{equation}
The same discount that emphasizes recent responses
therefore attenuates older compression errors.

\subsection{Choosing the group coefficients}
\label{sec:transaction}
\label{app:transaction-proof}

The ledger supplies $G_t$ and $r_t$, while the current
gradient supplies $s_t$. Coefficient selection combines these
quantities in equation~\ref{eq:transaction-objective}.
A change of coordinates turns the weighted norm constraint
into a sphere and reduces the projected solution to a scalar
multiplier equation.

For $\omega_i>0$, let $W=\operatorname{Diag}(\omega)$
and $y=W^{1/2}c$. Substituting $c=W^{-1/2}y$ and dividing
the objective by $\eta^2$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal Q_t(y)=\frac12y^\top M_ty-b_t^\top y,\quad
 \|y\|_2^2=\mathcal E_0,\\
 M_t=W^{-1/2}G_tW^{-1/2},\quad
 b_t=W^{-1/2}(s_t/\eta-r_t).
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:whitened-transaction}
\end{equation}
The coefficient construction assumes $b_t\neq0$, in addition
to positive block energies and learning rate. Its Lagrangian is
$\mathcal J(y,\lambda)=
\tfrac12y^\top M_ty-b_t^\top y+
\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(\|y\|_2^2-\mathcal E_0)$.
Differentiation with respect to $y$ gives the stationarity
equation $(M_t+\lambda I)y=b_t$. The multiplier controls
the norm of the proposed update in these coordinates.

TILLER constructs a proposal on the positive-multiplier
branch in a subspace generated by $b_t$ and repeated actions
of $M_t$. A reorthogonalized Lanczos recurrence
\citep{lanczos1950iteration} produces a basis $Q_k$,
with $k\leq32$. In the absence of breakdown, its span is
$\operatorname{span}\{b_t,M_tb_t,\ldots,M_t^{k-1}b_t\}$.
Each metric action follows directly from the ledger:
\begin{equation}
 M_tv=
 W^{-1/2}\mathcal A_t^\top
 (\mathcal A_tW^{-1/2}v)/K+
 \beta_2\tau_{t-1}W^{-1}v.
 \label{eq:ledger-metric-action}
\end{equation}
Let $T_k^+$ be the positive-semidefinite projection of the
accumulated Lanczos matrix. With $y=Q_kz$, the projected
stationarity equation is
\begin{equation}
 (T_k^++\lambda I)z=Q_k^\top b_t,\qquad
 \|z\|_2^2=\mathcal E_0.
 \label{eq:projected-secular}
\end{equation}
Diagonalizing
$T_k^+=U\operatorname{Diag}(\theta)U^\top$
and writing $\gamma=U^\top Q_k^\top b_t$ reduces
the norm condition to
\begin{equation}
 \psi(\lambda)=
 \sum_j\frac{\gamma_j^2}{(\theta_j+\lambda)^2}
 =\mathcal E_0.
 \label{eq:secular-equation}
\end{equation}
For $\lambda>0$, nonnegative $\theta_j$ and nonzero
$\gamma$ give
$\psi'(\lambda)=-2\sum_j
\gamma_j^2/(\theta_j+\lambda)^3<0$.
Thus bisection determines the unique positive root when
one lies in the chosen interval. The resulting proposal is
$c=W^{-1/2}Q_kU\operatorname{Diag}((\theta+\lambda)^{-1})
\gamma$.

The proposal is evaluated in the full objective
$F_t$, using the ledger factor and isotropic remainder.
It is selected when it is finite, lowers
$F_t$ relative to $c=\boldsymbol1$, and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \frac{|c^\top Wc-\mathcal E_0|}
      {\max\{\mathcal E_0,1\}}\leq10^{-8}.
 \label{eq:coefficient-norm-check}
\end{equation}
TILLER also requires positive aggregate contractions
with the current gradient and Nesterov momentum in each
layer and matrix role:
\begin{equation}
 \sum_g c_{\ell g}
 \inner{g_{\ell g}^r}{q_{\ell g}^r}>0,\qquad
 \sum_g c_{\ell g}
 \inner{m_{\ell g}^r}{q_{\ell g}^r}>0,
 \quad r\in\{A,B\}.
 \label{eq:transaction-contractions}
\end{equation}
These inequalities are checked for both the proposed
coefficients and $\boldsymbol1$.
If the positive root is unavailable or a check fails,
the step uses $c=\boldsymbol1$.
The selected coefficients scale both projections of
each group, completing the coordination of the
response-guided directions.

\section{Algorithm specification}
\label{app:implementation}
\label{sec:distributed}
\label{app:algorithm}
\label{app:numerical}

A training step combines the constructions above in the following order.
The forward and backward passes provide the clipped parameter gradients
and, on a probe refresh, the activation values and downstream loss
derivatives. The optimizer then constructs directions, measures their
joint response, and applies the selected coefficients.

\begin{enumerate}
 \item Update Nesterov momentum and factorized second moments for the
 GRAIN projections and attention matrices, and construct their Muon
 and adaptive directions.
 \item On a probe refresh, evaluate the participation and congruence
 statistics; otherwise reuse their latest values. Form the
 response-guided rotations and select the matrix directions.
 \item Evaluate the shared probe scores and decay responses on a
 refresh step. At other steps, use the current gradient
 contractions and equation~\ref{eq:trace-matched-rows}.
 \item Combine the current rows with the previous ledger to form
 $G_t$ and $r_t$. Construct and evaluate the coefficient proposal.
 Compress the response factor and retain the updated ledger
 for the next step.
 \item Apply the paired GRAIN updates
 \[
 A_i\leftarrow(1-\eta\lambda_{\rm wd})A_i-\eta c_iq_i^A,\qquad
 B_i\leftarrow(1-\eta\lambda_{\rm wd})B_i-\eta c_iq_i^B.
 \]
 Apply the response-guided directions to attention, and update the
 rational coefficients and remaining parameter classes with AdamW
 \citep{loshchilov2019decoupled}.
\end{enumerate}

For the activation, the group width is $w=256$ and
$\epsilon_{\rm act}=10^{-6}$. The expanded width is $H_r=Gw$.
All groups begin from the same SiLU fit. In ascending powers, the coefficients
are
\begin{align}
  (a^{\rm init}_0,\ldots,a^{\rm init}_5)
  ={}&(2.4915714620520875\!\times\!10^{-5},\
  0.5000668663485823,\ 0.2499441908995771,\nonumber\\
  &0.0526200910151016,\ 0.005525765640115002,\
  0.00024199321178747251),\\
  (b^{\rm init}_1,\ldots,b^{\rm init}_4)
  ={}&(0.00022554017910995938,\ 0.10511420350691994,\nonumber\\
  &2.8656992394109812\!\times\!10^{-5},\
  0.0004816962110562025).
\end{align}
The ten coefficients subsequently learn independently in every group.


The matrix directions use the same momentum in their polar and
adaptive constructions.
Let $g_t$ denote the parameter gradient after microbatch accumulation,
distributed averaging, and global-norm clipping.
For each pair of group blocks $i=(\ell,g)$ and role
$r\in\{A,B\}$, \tiller{} starts from a Nesterov--Muon direction $p_i^r$:
with $\beta_1=0.95$, it forms
\begin{equation}
  v_{t,i}^r=\beta_1v_{t-1,i}^r+(1-\beta_1)g_{t,i}^r,
  \qquad
  m_{t,i}^r=\beta_1v_{t,i}^r+(1-\beta_1)g_{t,i}^r,
  \label{eq:nesterov-momentum}
\end{equation}
followed by a five-step Newton--Schulz polar map
applied independently to each group block and Muon's role-dependent RMS
adjustment \citep{jordan2024muon,bernstein2024old,liu2025muon}. To set the groupwise polar directions on the nominal Frobenius scale
of their full projection matrix, we apply
\begin{equation}
  \chi_{\rm grp}
  =\sqrt{\frac{\min\{H_r,d\}}{G\min\{w,d\}}},
  \label{eq:group-polar-calibration}
\end{equation}
Writing $\mathcal P_5$ for this blockwise polar map, Muon's adjustment is
$\mu_{\rm mlp}=0.2\sqrt{\max\{H_r,d\}}$ for either GRAIN matrix role. The
parent group direction is
$p_{t,i}^r=\mu_{\rm mlp}\chi_{\rm grp}\mathcal P_5(m_{t,i}^r)$.
In parallel, \tiller{} maintains
factorized row and column second moments, in the spirit of memory-efficient
factored adaptation \citep{shazeer2018adafactor}. For a grouped matrix with
gradient $g_i$ and Nesterov momentum $m_i$, suppressing role indices,
\begin{align}
  R_t&=\beta_2 R_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)\operatorname{rowsum}(g_i^2),\\
  C_t&=\beta_2 C_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)\operatorname{colsum}(g_i^2),\\
  V_{t,jk}&=\frac{R_{t,j}C_{t,k}}
  {\max\{\sum_sR_{t,s},\delta_{\rm fp}\}}\frac{1}{1-\beta_2^t},
  \qquad
  a_i=\mu_{\rm mlp}\frac{m_i}{\sqrt{V_t}+\epsilon_{\rm opt}}.
  \label{eq:factored-direction}
\end{align}
Here $\operatorname{rowsum}$ and $\operatorname{colsum}$ sum squared
entries over the columns and rows, respectively. We set $\beta_2=0.95$,
$\epsilon_{\rm opt}=10^{-8}$, and
$\delta_{\rm fp}=\operatorname{finfo}(\texttt{float32}).\operatorname{tiny}$. The same $\beta_2$ sets the time scale of the loss ledger. When the row-moment total is zero, $V_t=0$.


The approximate polar map $\mathcal P_5$ transposes a tall
input and casts the working matrix to bfloat16. Its Frobenius
norm is accumulated in float32, lower bounded by $10^{-7}$,
and cast to bfloat16 for normalization.
With $(a,b,c)=(3.4445,-4.775,2.0315)$, its five iterations are
\begin{equation}
 X_{j+1}=aX_j+b(X_jX_j^\top)X_j+
 c(X_jX_j^\top)^2X_j.
 \label{eq:newton-schulz-map}
\end{equation}
The iterations run in bfloat16, after which the original
orientation is restored. Factorized second moments are
maintained in float32.

The response statistics use deterministic shared probes at
steps $1,9,17,\ldots$. All quantities in a probe row refer
to the same example--token position. If the backward pass
averages over $R$ positions per microbatch and $M$ accumulated
microbatches, a captured output derivative $\bar\xi_{k\ell}$
is converted to
\begin{equation}
 \xi_{k\ell}=RM\chi_{\rm clip}\bar\xi_{k\ell},
 \label{eq:cotangent-rescaling}
\end{equation}
where $\chi_{\rm clip}$ is the realized global gradient-clipping
factor. This is the derivative of the summed microbatch loss,
scaled by $\chi_{\rm clip}$. The clipping factor is held fixed
when forming the directional measurements.
For zero responses,
$\operatorname{pr}_A(0)=\operatorname{pr}_B(0)=0$.
If either role has zero total sensitivity, both participation
values are set to one. If either kernel-congruence denominator
vanishes, both congruence values are set to one.
Congruence is also set to one when the rational coefficients
equal their initialization. These conventions select zero
departure when the response comparison is undefined.

Attention uses the same rotation construction with statistics
aggregated within its layer. Let
$\overline\pi_\ell^r=G^{-1}\sum_g\pi_{\ell g}^r$.
For each role, obtain $\kappa_\ell^r$ by summing the numerator
and the two squared-norm sums in
equation~\ref{eq:congruence-main} over groups before
normalization. Then
\begin{equation}
 \pi_\ell^{\rm att}
 =\sqrt{\overline\pi_\ell^A\overline\pi_\ell^B},\qquad
 \kappa_\ell^{\rm att}=\sqrt{\kappa_\ell^A\kappa_\ell^B},\qquad
 e_\ell^{\rm att}=\pi_\ell^{\rm att}
       \left[1-(\kappa_\ell^{\rm att})^2\right].
 \label{eq:attention-response}
\end{equation}
The participation term combines the breadth of both projection
roles, while the congruence term allows a change in either
response to affect the attention rotation.
This fraction guides the QKV and attention-output matrices.
The 16 attention heads are partitioned into blocks of four
for the polar map; the stacked QKV direction is scaled by
$1/\sqrt3$ for its three constituent projections.
The adaptive tangent uses matrix-level factorized moments.
The group coefficients $c_i$ apply to the paired GRAIN
projections; attention receives its layerwise rotation.
Rational coefficients, embeddings, normalization parameters,
and remaining tensors use AdamW.

The coefficient solver uses double precision. Its Lanczos
recurrence performs two reorthogonalization passes per basis
vector. At an early breakdown, a deterministic cosine vector
is reorthogonalized against the existing basis to start a new
block, with zero coupling between blocks. The basis is checked
for orthogonality before use.
If $\bar T_k=U\operatorname{Diag}(\bar\theta)U^\top$ is the
accumulated projected matrix, the solver sets
$T_k^+=U\operatorname{Diag}((\bar\theta)_+)U^\top$.
The positive multiplier is bracketed by
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_{\rm lo}=\epsilon_{\rm mach}C,\qquad
 \lambda_{\rm hi}=
 \frac{\|b_t\|_2}{\sqrt{\mathcal E_0}}
 +\lambda_{\max}(T_k^+)+\lambda_{\rm lo}.
 \label{eq:multiplier-bracket}
\end{equation}
Since $\theta_j\geq0$,
$\psi(\lambda_{\rm hi})\leq
\|b_t\|_2^2/\lambda_{\rm hi}^2\leq\mathcal E_0$.
When $\psi(\lambda_{\rm lo})\geq\mathcal E_0$,
the interval contains the positive root and the solver
takes 64 bisection steps.
Otherwise the step retains $c=\boldsymbol1$.
The returned proposal is checked in the full ledger
objective as specified in Appendix~\ref{sec:transaction}.
