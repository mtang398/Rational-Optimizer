\begin{abstract}
We introduce a joint activation--optimizer design that achieves lower mean
validation loss than AdamW, Muon, and SOAP in Transformer pretraining at
matched training-token budgets. Our approach uses the model's learned
nonlinear responses to guide both the construction of matrix updates and
their joint allocation across groups and layers. \textbf{GRAIN},
\textbf{G}roupwise \textbf{R}ational \textbf{A}ctivation with \textbf{I}nternal
\textbf{N}ormalization, learns a rational activation function for each
feature group in normalized coordinates, allowing different groups to
adapt their nonlinear response shapes during training.
\textbf{TILLER}, \textbf{T}angent-\textbf{I}nformed \textbf{L}oss
\textbf{L}edger for \textbf{E}qual-budget \textbf{R}eweighting, uses these
learned responses to shape matrix updates and estimate their first-order effects on
the loss. Measuring these effects jointly reveals how the loss-response patterns of
different updates reinforce or offset one another, information that their
individual average descent scores do not capture. TILLER maintains a
running summary of these cross-update relationships and combines it with
current gradients to determine the relative strengths of updates across
groups and layers.
The gains hold across every tested corpus and model size with both SwiGLU
and GRAIN baseline models, demonstrating improvements beyond activation
replacement alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Learning an activation function gives a Transformer control over both its
feature representation and the response of those features to weight updates.
Fixed nonlinearities such as GELU and the SiLU gate in SwiGLU provide
effective transformations \citep{hendrycks2016gelu,shazeer2020glu}.
Rational activations make the response shape trainable
\citep{molina2020pade}, with approximation advantages that motivate their
use as expressive neural representations
\citep{boulle2020rational,tang2026expressivity}. Group-shared rational
functions bring this flexibility to Transformer feed-forward blocks
\citep{yang2025kat}. The changing response also supplies information for
optimizing the matrices around it: learning the nonlinearity changes how
an update propagates through the network.

The incoming and outgoing projections expose two complementary paths.
An incoming update changes features through the activation Jacobian;
an outgoing update recombines the activated features. Relating
these changes to the downstream loss gives a functional description of
each matrix direction. The interaction between directions is equally
important. Two groups can have the same average descent signal while
their loss responses reinforce or offset one another at shared
activation positions. Separate descent scores leave these relationships
unresolved. Measuring the responses together makes them available for
choosing the strengths of simultaneous updates.

We introduce GRAIN, Groupwise Rational Activation with Internal
Normalization, as the learned representation for this construction.
Each feature group evaluates its own rational function on
RMS-normalized coordinates and multiplies the result by the same
normalization scale \citep{zhang2019rmsnorm}. The learned coefficients
control the response to relative feature magnitudes, while the output
retains a dependence on input amplitude. Its Jacobian describes both
the local rational slope and the effect of changing the group scale.
The features and their derivatives provide the two response paths used
to guide optimization.

Matrix-aware optimization uses matrix structure and activation sensitivity
to shape updates \citep{martens2015kfac,vyas2025soap,liu2025muon}.
TILLER, Tangent-Informed Loss Ledger for Equal-budget Reweighting,
uses the learned responses to construct matrix directions and coordinate
their joint effect on the loss. It uses the response
geometry to rotate an orthogonalized momentum direction along a
factorized adaptive tangent, with orientation selected by the gradient
\citep{shazeer2018adafactor}. It then records
the selected feed-forward directions' loss responses at shared
positions in a compact temporal ledger. Together with the current
gradients, this history defines a joint model for allocating update
strength across groups and layers. The allocation preserves the
combined norm of that step's feed-forward directions, so the ledger
changes where the update acts according to the responses it produces.

This construction gives two training methods. Full TILLER continually
adapts directions and their joint allocation throughout training.
The staged method applies TILLER for an initial interval, then continues
from the learned model with a chosen optimizer. GRAIN remains trainable in both
methods. Full TILLER maintains loss-response coordination along the
complete trajectory; staged training combines a coordinated training stage
with the update rule and computational cost of its continuation
optimizer.

Across five pretraining corpora and both evaluated model sizes, GRAIN
with full TILLER achieves the lowest mean validation loss among the
tested activation--optimizer configurations at matched token budgets.
The comparisons include AdamW, Muon, SOAP, and factorized adaptive
optimizers. At the larger model size, the evaluated staged
configuration also achieves lower mean validation loss than every
baseline, while using less total training time than full TILLER on
every corpus.

\section{Related Work}

Pad\'{e} Activation Units learn rational curves jointly with network weights
\citep{molina2020pade}. Kolmogorov--Arnold Transformer (KAT) implements
group-shared rational functions followed by linear maps; its reported
configuration shares numerator coefficients across groups \citep{yang2025kat}.
GRAIN evaluates each group's rational function in RMS-normalized
coordinates \citep{zhang2019rmsnorm} and restores the group scale at its
output. It retains group sharing while learning both numerator and
denominator coefficients independently for each group.

Approximation theory establishes parameter-efficiency separations between
rational and ReLU networks \citep{boulle2020rational}.
\emph{Rational Neural Networks have Expressivity Advantages}, by Maosen Tang
and Alex Townsend, extends these comparisons to bounded-parameter
smooth-activation networks and analyzes coefficient redundancy under
learned affine normalization
\citep{tang2026expressivity}. GRAIN uses an internal normalization--rescaling
map with no additional learned affine transform.

Muon orthogonalizes momentum \citep{jordan2024muon}, while Adafactor
estimates gradient second moments from row and column statistics
\citep{shazeer2018adafactor}. TILLER combines these direction constructions
using the learned activation response. Loss-weighted features and Jacobians
determine the extent of a rotation from the polar direction along an
adaptive tangent, with its orientation selected by the current gradient.

Joint update models differ in their search directions and the information
used to compare them. K-FAC constructs parameter-space Fisher approximations from activation and
backpropagated-derivative moments, including cross-layer blocks
\citep{martens2015kfac}. Newton--Muon derives input-second-moment
preconditioning from a matrix quadratic surrogate \citep{du2026newtonmuon}.
Krylov Subspace Descent optimizes the nonlinear objective over a
curvature-generated Krylov subspace \citep{vinyals2012krylov}.
TILLER places its quadratic model on coefficients multiplying selected
pairs of incoming and outgoing group directions. Current gradients supply
the linear term. A discounted history of shared-position response probes,
augmented between probes by current-gradient information, supplies the
quadratic penalty. Its cross terms couple coefficients across groups
and layers; the allocation preserves
the combined feed-forward direction norm before weight decay.

Optimizer switching combines update rules along a training trajectory.
SWATS selects an Adam-to-SGD transition using a projection-based trigger
\citep{keskar2017switching}. Our staged method applies TILLER until a chosen
step, then continues with a selected optimizer while retaining the learned
parameters and trainable GRAIN activations.



\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We introduce GRAIN in Transformer feed-forward blocks and TILLER
to coordinate matrix updates using GRAIN's loss response. We first define
the block, then the update shared by full-horizon and staged training.

\subsection{GRAIN}

GRAIN (Groupwise Rational Activation with Internal Normalization)
replaces a fixed nonlinearity with group-shared rational functions.
For input $x\in\mathbb R^d$, partition the intermediate features
into $G$ groups of width $w$. The incoming and outgoing projections
of group $g$ are $A_g\in\mathbb R^{w\times d}$ and
$B_g\in\mathbb R^{d\times w}$. Its feed-forward block is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 z_g=A_gx,\qquad
 \rho_g=\sqrt{\|z_g\|_2^2/w+\epsilon_{\rm act}},\\
 h_g=\rho_g\phi_g(z_g/\rho_g),\qquad
 y=\sum_{g=1}^{G}B_gh_g.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:overview-grain}
\end{equation}
The curve acts coordinatewise on RMS-normalized features and its
output is multiplied by the same group scale
\citep{zhang2019rmsnorm}. The scale is computed per token and
group, with stabilizer $\epsilon_{\rm act}>0$.

Each group learns a rational curve \citep{molina2020pade,yang2025kat}:
\begin{equation}
 \phi_g(u)=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{5}a_{gj}u^j}
 {1+\sum_{j=1}^{4}|b_{gj}|\,|u|^j}.
 \label{eq:overview-rational}
\end{equation}
The denominator is at least one, so the curve has no poles.
All coefficients are shared across channels and token positions
within a group and learned independently across groups. We initialize
each curve with the same rational approximation to SiLU and train
its coefficients jointly with the projection weights.
Appendix~\ref{sec:grain} derives the Jacobian of the normalized
activation and its dependence on input scale.

\subsection{TILLER}

TILLER (Tangent-Informed Loss Ledger for Equal-budget Reweighting)
uses the learned activation response to construct matrix directions and
allocate their strengths jointly. For an $L$-layer model, let
$i=(\ell,g)$ index the $C=LG$ feed-forward groups. Each group receives
incoming and outgoing directions $(q_i^A,q_i^B)$ and a shared coefficient
$c_i$. The directions determine how each group changes; the coefficients
coordinate these changes across the model.

For either matrix in a pair, a calibrated polar direction $p$ and a
factorized adaptive direction $a$ transform the same momentum
\citep{jordan2024muon,liu2025muon,shazeer2018adafactor}. Write
$a^\perp=a-\inner{a}{p}p/\frob p^2$.

The incoming direction is guided by the activation Jacobian,
and the outgoing direction by the activated features.
For each role, loss-weighted participation $\pi\in[0,1]$ measures
the spread of this response. Congruence $\kappa\in[0,1]$ compares
its response kernel with that of the initial rational curve
at the same current inputs. These summaries set the rotation:
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde q=\sqrt{1-e}\,p+
 \sigma\sqrt e\frac{\frob p}{\frob{a^\perp}}a^\perp,
 \qquad e=\pi(1-\kappa^2),
 \label{eq:overview-rotation}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ makes the tangent's gradient contraction nonnegative.
The rotation preserves the parent norm.
An unchanged response kernel retains $p$; broader responses permit
larger rotations for a given departure from initialization.
We retain $p$ for a degenerate or nonfinite rotation, or when the
parent $p$ or candidate $\widetilde q$ has nonpositive gradient
contraction. Matrix-size scaling of the selected direction gives $q$.
Appendix~\ref{sec:directions} defines the summaries, selection
conditions, and norm-preserving geometry.

To coordinate these directions, periodic probes measure their loss
responses at $K$ example--token positions shared across layers.
The probes also refresh $\pi$ and $\kappa$; between probes, directions
are recomputed from current moments using the latest response summaries.
For block input
$x_{k\ell}$, activated features $h_{ki}$, and activation Jacobian
$J_{ki}=\partial h_{ki}/\partial z_{ki}$, the paired response is
\begin{equation}
 S_{t,ki}=\inner{\xi_{k\ell}}
 {B_iJ_{ki}q_i^Ax_{k\ell}+q_i^Bh_{ki}},
 \label{eq:overview-score}
\end{equation}
where $\xi_{k\ell}$ is the derivative of the summed minibatch loss at
the block output, multiplied by the gradient-clipping factor.
The combined response is $S_tc$: its squared norm includes cross
terms between different groups and layers.
Appendix~\ref{sec:loss-measurements} derives this differential and
its relation to current-gradient descent.

A response matrix contributes the Gram metric $S_t^\top S_t/K$,
whose off-diagonal entries measure interactions between pairs.
The response $a_t^{\rm dec}$ to feed-forward weight decay contributes
the cross term $S_t^\top a_t^{\rm dec}/K$.
Their discounted histories supply the current metric $G_t$ and
cross term $r_t$. Between probes, current gradient contractions
supply repeated rows calibrated to the latest measured response scale.
A Robust Frequent Directions sketch retains this history as a row
factor and an isotropic remainder
\citep{ghashami2016frequent,luo2019robust}.
The newest rows enter the metric before compression.
Appendix~\ref{sec:ledger} gives the ledger construction and its
approximation bounds.

Let $g_i^A,g_i^B$ be the accumulated, averaged, clipped gradients,
$s_{t,i}=\inner{g_i^A}{q_i^A}+\inner{g_i^B}{q_i^B}$ their descent
score, and $\omega_i=\frob{q_i^A}^2+\frob{q_i^B}^2$.
The allocation balances current descent against the joint loss response:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 F_t(c)=-\eta_ts_t^\top c+
 \frac{\eta_t^2}{2}(c^\top G_tc+2r_t^\top c),\\
 \sum_i\omega_ic_i^2=\sum_i\omega_i,
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:overview-coordinate-model}
\end{equation}
where $\eta_t$ is the learning rate.
The linear term rewards current descent; the quadratic term penalizes
the joint response of the directions and feed-forward weight decay.
The constraint preserves the combined feed-forward direction norm of
$c=\boldsymbol1$, before decay, while permitting signed coefficients.
A coefficient proposal is used when
it improves this model and satisfies the norm and layerwise
contraction conditions; otherwise $c=\boldsymbol1$.
Appendix~\ref{sec:transaction} gives the projected solver and acceptance
conditions.

With decay rate $\lambda_{\rm wd}$, the resulting step applies
\[
 A_i\leftarrow(1-\eta_t\lambda_{\rm wd})A_i-\eta_tc_iq_i^A,
 \qquad
 B_i\leftarrow(1-\eta_t\lambda_{\rm wd})B_i-\eta_tc_iq_i^B.
\]
The coefficients jointly allocate these feed-forward pairs.
An attention matrix $D$ uses a response-guided direction $q^D$ formed
from its own moments and layerwise GRAIN summaries, with update
$D\leftarrow(1-\eta_t\lambda_{\rm wd})D-\eta_tq^D$.
Rational coefficients and all remaining parameters use AdamW
\citep{loshchilov2019decoupled}.
Tables~\ref{tab:notation} and~\ref{tab:algorithm} collect the notation
and the complete training loop.

\subsubsection{Full-horizon TILLER}

For training horizon $T$, full-horizon TILLER applies this common
update at every step. It continually adapts the matrix directions
and their joint allocation as GRAIN's nonlinearities evolve.

\subsubsection{Staged training}

For a chosen transition $T_s<T$, staged training applies the same
TILLER update through $T_s$ and then continues the feed-forward and
attention updates with an optimizer $\mathcal U$.
Its state initializer $\mathcal I$ transfers stored first-moment
histories compatible with the continuation's recurrence and matrix
grouping; additional statistics use $\mathcal U$'s initialization.
The first continuation update acts on the learned matrices at $T_s+1$.
Model parameters and the learning-rate schedule continue across
the transition. Rational coefficients and remaining parameters retain
their AdamW state, and GRAIN remains trainable.
Response collection and ledger updates end at $T_s$.
